How the numbers are counted

Every gain in Parts 1–5 is a ratio of roofline bounds, counted in one of three ways.

A roofline time is a lower bound on the time of a pass, so each throughput is an upper bound, and \(\Gamma\) is the ratio of two such bounds, not a measurement. Part 9 compares these bounds with throughput measured in vLLM. The three accountings below differ in what they include, and each number in the blogpost says which one it uses. For DiffusionGemma on an H200:

In the interactive figures and the cards, the intensities \(I^{\mathrm{AR}}\) and \(I^{\mathrm{TD}}\) are those of Definition 3, without the vocabulary, except in the intensity tabs of Figure 16 and the calculator's maps, which use the exact accounting at the end of this chapter; next to an exact gain, the regime of a pass is given instead as the share of the peak compute that it uses. The cards quote the closed-form values of the propositions, at zero context where Part 2 does, unless a readout says exact. A fourth variant, the kernel-by-kernel timing rule of Part 1, can be switched on in Figure 10 and in the calculator.

Figure 11 · interactive

A canvas reads the cache once for all its queries, except for the tokens that a sparse layer selects.

Columns are the cached tokens of one sequence and rows the queries of a pass. A filled cell is a pair (query, cached token) that the layer scores; the bar under each block marks the tokens whose keys and values the pass reads. Choose the kind of layer and the number of queries.

A toy cache of \(C = 40\) tokens, with a window of \(w = 10\) and \(m = 5\) selected tokens per query; DiffusionGemma has \(w = 1{,}023\) and a canvas of \(b = 256\) queries, and the sparse variant of this chapter \(m = 2{,}048\). The FLOPs of a pass are proportional to its full cells, plus, in a sparse layer, every cell of its rows, full or pale, at the indexer's smaller cost \(\alpha_I\) each; its bytes are proportional to its filled bars. This is the content of Table 2. In a dense or windowed layer the bar is the same for one query and for \(b\), so a canvas has \(b\) times the intensity of an AR step. In a sparse layer each query selects its own tokens, independently and uniformly here, as tokens select experts under (A3), and the bar is the union of the selections, of expected size \(U_{C,m}(b)\); the indexer scores every cached token, selected or not, and its index keys are read once for the whole canvas. The canvas's own positions, which its queries also attend to, are not drawn.
