How the numbers are counted

Every gain in Parts 1–5 is a ratio of roofline bounds, counted in one of three ways.

A roofline time is a lower bound on the time of a pass, so each throughput is an upper bound, and \(\Gamma\) is the ratio of two such bounds, not a measurement. Part 9 compares these bounds with throughput measured in vLLM. The three accountings below differ in what they include, and each number in the blogpost says which one it uses. For DiffusionGemma on an H200:

In the interactive figures and the cards, the intensities \(I_{AR}\) and \(I_{TD}\) are those of Definition 3, without the vocabulary, except in the intensity tabs of Figure 18 and the calculator's maps, which use the exact accounting at the end of this chapter; next to an exact gain, the regime of a pass is given instead as the share of the peak compute that it uses. The cards quote the closed-form values of the propositions, at zero context where Part 2 does, unless a readout says exact. A fourth variant, the kernel-by-kernel timing rule of Part 1, can be switched on in Figure 11 and in the calculator.

Under (A1), the best batch is the one at which a TD pass reaches the ridge.

The curve is hidden. Drag the marker to the batch at which you expect TD's lead over AR to be largest, then reveal it.

Exact roofline bound \(\Gamma(B)\) at a fixed context of 1,024: DiffusionGemma, BF16, \(b = 256\), \(S = 14\), vocabulary included. Under (A1), left of \(B^\star_{TD}\) both decoders are memory-bound and \(\Gamma \approx \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\) (Proposition 3, without the vocabulary) rises as the batch dilutes the linear penalty. Right of it a TD pass is compute-bound and \(\Gamma \approx I^\star/(\omega I_{AR})\) falls, roughly like \(1/B\) while the weights dominate AR's bytes, down to parity at \(B_\times\) where there is one. With the kernels of a pass timed one after another instead (Part 1), the dense layers of a TD pass are compute-bound before its experts are, and the peak no longer sits at a single transition: it is lower on every accelerator here, and on most of them it comes later (batch 9 instead of 5 on an H200). Figure 7 · interactive

The gain depends on the accelerator only through its ridge point.

The curve is the gain for a chip of ridge point \(I^\star = \pi/\beta\); each dot is an accelerator. Drag the marker along the axis, select a dot, or switch to long context.

Exact roofline bounds, DiffusionGemma, BF16, \(b = 256\), \(S = 14\). Multiplying the peak \(\pi\) and the bandwidth \(\beta\) by the same factor divides the time of every pass by the same factor, so \(\Gamma\) depends on the accelerator only through \(I^\star\), and the accelerators lie on the curve. Over this range a higher ridge point never lowers the gain, at short or at long context. Short context: \(C = 1{,}024\), at the batch that maximises \(\Gamma\); on the flat stretch near \(I^\star = 60\), batch 1 is best and both decoders are memory-bound, so the gain is \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi\) whatever the ridge. Long context: \(C = 2^{20}\) at batch 1; the dashed line is the limit \(I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{attn}})\) as \(C \to \infty\), linear in \(I^\star\). Over this range the curve lies above the line, because at batch 1 the weights are still about a quarter of an AR step's bytes; above \(I^\star \approx 620\) a TD pass at batch 1 is itself memory-bound at this context, and the curve is flat. The curve assumes unlimited memory; a hollow dot marks an accelerator in which the model and its KV cache do not fit. Both axes are logarithmic.

Figure 12 · interactive

A canvas reads the cache once for all its queries, except for the tokens that a sparse layer selects.

Columns are the cached tokens of one sequence and rows the queries of a pass. A filled cell is a pair (query, cached token) that the layer scores; the bar under each block marks the tokens whose keys and values the pass reads. Choose the kind of layer and the number of queries.

A toy cache of \(C = 40\) tokens, with a window of \(w = 10\) and \(m = 5\) selected tokens per query; DiffusionGemma has \(w = 1{,}023\) and a canvas of \(b = 256\) queries, and the sparse variant of this chapter \(m = 2{,}048\). The FLOPs of a pass are proportional to its full cells, plus its pale cells at the indexer's smaller cost \(\alpha_I\) each, and its bytes to its filled bars, which is the content of Table 3. In a dense or windowed layer the bar is the same for one query and for \(b\), so a canvas has \(b\) times the intensity of an AR step. In a sparse layer each query selects its own tokens, independently and uniformly here, as tokens select experts under (A5), and the bar is the union of the selections, of expected size \(U_{C,m}(b)\); the pale cells are the indexer's scan, whose index keys are read once. The canvas's own positions, which its queries also attend to, are not drawn.
