They are shown in both reading modes; in "intuition" mode the box folds to its title and this sentence. --> The sizes the costs depend on: active and total parameters, how many distinct experts a set of positions touches, and the attention FLOPs and KV bytes per cached token. Per sequence, a TD pass does \(b\) times the FLOPs of an AR step; the weight bytes are shared by the batch and the cache bytes are not. Intensity is FLOPs per byte read from memory; a pass is compute-bound when its intensity reaches the ridge point \(I^\star = \pi/\beta\), 206 on an H200. A penalty is how many more bytes per sequence a TD pass reads than an AR step: \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) from the experts its \(b\) positions touch, \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) from the cached tokens that sparse layers select. The gain is the ratio of the two decoders' attainable FLOP rates, divided by the \(\omega\) passes a canvas costs. If AR is compute-bound TD loses; otherwise the gain is the smaller of \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi\), set by bytes, and \(I^\star/(\omega I_{AR})\), set by the compute AR leaves idle. Each intensity is the byte-weighted average of its linear and attention parts, so it lies between them, nearer to the part that carries more of the bytes. A canvas touches almost every expert, so at batch 1 TD streams the total parameters where AR streams the active ones (a penalty of 7.9), and the penalty vanishes once the batch alone touches every expert. With dense attention and full windows, the weights dominate an AR step's bytes up to a context that shrinks as the batch grows, like \(1/B\) once the batch touches every expert, because the batch shares the weights but each sequence brings its own cache. With sparse attention each query selects its own cached tokens and a canvas reads their union: the MoE penalty again, with the canvas length in the role of the batch. With full windows, attention overtakes the weights in an AR step's bytes at the context of Proposition 4, and in a position's FLOPs at \(C_F(1-\sigma)\), which does not depend on the batch; as the context grows both intensities move to their attention values, \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) for AR and \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) for TD with dense attention. TD wins exactly when a pass serves more tokens per read than it pays in penalty (\(\mathrm{TPF} \ge \Pi\)) and AR uses at most a fraction \(1/\omega\) of the compute (\(I_{AR} \le I^\star/\omega\)). With dense attention and full windows, a batch cannot saturate compute on cache bytes, since each sequence brings its own cache, but a canvas can: once \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \gt I^\star\), a longer context makes a TD pass compute-bound at a smaller batch, not a larger one. Timing a pass as a chain of kernels instead of one fully overlapped kernel is never faster and at most twice slower; it moves DiffusionGemma's best batch from 5 to 9.
