They are shown in both reading modes; in "intuition" mode the box folds to its title and this sentence. --> The sizes the costs depend on: active and total parameters, how many distinct experts a set of positions touches, and the attention FLOPs and KV bytes per cached token. Per sequence, a TD pass does \(b\) times the FLOPs of an AR step; the weight bytes are shared by the batch and the cache bytes are not. Intensity is FLOPs per byte read from memory; a pass is compute-bound when its intensity reaches the ridge point \(I^\star = \pi/\beta\), 206 on an H200. A penalty is the ratio of the bytes a TD pass reads per sequence to those an AR step reads: \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) exceeds 1 because the \(b\) positions of a canvas touch more experts, and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) because sparse layers select more cached tokens for them. The gain is the ratio of the two decoders' attainable FLOP rates, divided by the \(\omega\) passes a canvas costs. If AR is compute-bound, TD loses; otherwise the gain is the smaller of \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi\), set by bytes, and \(I^\star/(\omega I_{AR})\), set by the compute AR leaves idle. Each intensity is the byte-weighted average of its linear and attention parts, so it lies between them, nearer to the part that carries more of the bytes. A canvas touches almost every expert, so at batch 1 TD streams the total parameters where AR streams the active ones (a penalty of 7.9), and the penalty falls to 1 once the batch alone touches every expert. With dense attention and full windows, the weights dominate an AR step's bytes up to a context that shrinks as the batch grows, affine in \(1/B\) once the batch touches every expert, because the batch shares the weights but each sequence brings its own cache. With sparse attention each query selects its own cached tokens and a canvas reads their union: the expert penalty \(\Pi_E\) at batch 1 again, with the cached tokens in the role of the experts. With full windows, attention overtakes the weights in an AR step's bytes at the context of Proposition 4, and in a position's FLOPs at \(C_F(1-\sigma)\), which does not depend on the batch; as the context grows both intensities move to their attention values, \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) for AR and \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) for TD with dense attention. TD wins exactly when a pass serves more tokens per read than it pays in penalty (\(\mathrm{TPF} \ge \Pi\)) and AR uses at most a fraction \(1/\omega\) of the compute (\(I_{AR} \le I^\star/\omega\)). With dense attention and full windows, a batch cannot saturate compute on cache bytes, since each sequence brings its own cache, but a canvas can: once \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \gt I^\star\), a longer context makes a TD pass compute-bound at a smaller batch, not a larger one. Timing a pass as a chain of kernels instead of one fully overlapped kernel is never faster and at most twice as slow; on an H200 it moves DiffusionGemma's best batch from 5 to 9. The empirical ceiling of a rate is the highest value measured on this GPU (for attention, a sustained reference rate); the empirical roofline of a component is \(\max(F_c/\hat\pi_c,\ Q_c/\hat\beta)\) at those ceilings. \(U^{\mathrm{meas}}\) counts the experts a pass actually touches; \(U^{\mathrm{freq}}\) is what independent tokens with the measured popularity would touch; \(N^{\mathrm{eff}}\) is the number of uniformly routed tokens that would touch \(U^{\mathrm{meas}}\). Measured routing enters Proposition 1 only through the expert weights each pass reads: the expert penalty becomes \(U^{\mathrm{meas}}_{TD}/U^{\mathrm{meas}}_{AR}\), and AR's intensity changes with them. A component's efficiency is its empirical roofline over its measured time; its recoverable time is what remains above the time of the best kernel available, timed at one representative shape. The measured gain over the bound equals TD's step efficiency over AR's, so the bound is conservative exactly when TD runs closer to its roofline.
