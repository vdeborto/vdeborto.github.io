\[ \begin{gathered} \omega = S\,r + 1,\\[4pt] \Gamma = \frac{1}{\omega}\cdot\frac{\min(I_{TD},\,I^\star)}{\min(I_{AR},\,I^\star)}. \end{gathered} \]

\(S\) denoising passes, each costing \(r\) forward passes (\(r \lt 1\) for a smaller denoiser), and one commit pass that writes the canvas to the KV cache. The intensity of a pass does not depend on \(S\), so under Proposition 1 the gain is exactly proportional to \(1/\omega\): halving \(S\) from 14 to 7 takes \(\omega\) from 15 to 8 and multiplies \(\Gamma\) by \(15/8\) in every regime, whatever it costs in quality.

Proposition 1 · Figure 1

\[ \begin{gathered} \mathrm{TPF} = \frac{b}{\omega},\qquad I_{TD} = \frac{b}{\Pi}\,I_{AR},\\[4pt] B^\star_{TD} \approx \frac{I^\star s_wW_{TD}}{2b\,N_{\mathrm{act}}}. \end{gathered} \]

While a TD pass is memory-bound, \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi\) grows with \(b\). Once it is compute-bound, \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega I_{AR})\) no longer depends on \(b\): a longer canvas reaches that regime at a smaller batch, and so raises the best gain, which sits at \(B^\star_{TD}\) or one batch below it.

The gain when the linear layers dominate · Figure 3

\[ \begin{gathered} \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) = \frac{W_{TD}(B)}{W_{AR}(B)},\\[4pt] W_{AR}(B) = W_{\mathrm{ne}} + L\,U_{E,k}(B)\,W^e,\\[2pt] W_{TD}(B) = W_{\mathrm{ne}} + L\,U_{E,k}(Bb)\,W^e,\\[4pt] U_{E,k}(N) = E\Bigl[1 - \bigl(1 - \tfrac{k}{E}\bigr)^{N}\Bigr]. \end{gathered} \]

\(U_{E,k}(N)\) is the expected number of distinct experts that \(N\) positions touch in one layer. A canvas routes \(Bb\) positions and touches almost all \(E = 128\); an AR step routes \(B\) and touches about \(kB\), with \(k = 8\). The dense part \(W_{\mathrm{ne}} = 1.66\)B is read by both and dilutes the ratio from \(E/k = 16\) to 7.9. Dense models have \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} = 1\).

Proposition 3 · Definition 4

\[ \begin{gathered} \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C) - 1 = \frac{\nu_S\,\bigl[\,U_{C,m}(b) - m\,\bigr]}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}(C)},\\[4pt] U_{C,m}(b) = C\Bigl[1 - \bigl(1 - \tfrac{m}{C}\bigr)^{b}\Bigr]. \end{gathered} \]

Only sparse layers contribute: each query selects its own \(m\) cached tokens, and the canvas reads the union, \(U_{C,m}(b)\) tokens, up to \(\min(bm, C)\). This is the MoE penalty again, with cached tokens in the role of experts and the canvas length in the role of the batch. It is 1 while \(C \le m\), largest for \(m \lt C \lesssim bm\), and returns to 1 as \(C \to \infty\), when the union stops growing and the indexer's scan carries the bytes.

Proposition 5 · sparse attention

\[ Q^{X}(B, C) = \underbrace{\frac{s_wW_X(B)}{B}}_{\text{weights}} + \underbrace{Q^{X}_{\mathrm{attn}}(C)}_{\text{cache}}. \]

For \(X \in \{AR, TD\}\), the weights are shared by the batch and each sequence brings its own cache. A larger batch divides the weight bytes per sequence and leaves the cache bytes unchanged, so it raises both intensities at short context. At first it helps TD more, because \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) falls; once a TD pass is compute-bound it only raises \(I_{AR}\), and \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega I_{AR})\) falls, down to parity near batch 109.

Proposition 4 · Figure 3

\[ \begin{gathered} I_X = \theta^X_{\mathrm{lin}}\,I^X_{\mathrm{lin}} + \theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}}\,I^X_{\mathrm{attn}},\\[4pt] \theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}} = \frac{Q^X_{\mathrm{attn}}}{Q^X}. \end{gathered} \]

Each intensity is the byte-weighted average of its two parts (Proposition 2). As \(C\) grows, the cache's share \(\theta_{\mathrm{attn}}\) goes to 1 and the intensities go to their attention values: \(I_{AR} \to I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 8\) on DiffusionGemma's global layers, far below every ridge, and \(I_{TD} \to b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 2{,}048\), above every ridge in this note.

Proposition 2 · Figure 4

Both passes run at the peak, so time is FLOPs, and a canvas spends \(\omega\) times AR's FLOPs per token: \(\Gamma \le 1/\omega\), equal to it under Proposition 1. AR reaches the ridge at

\[ \begin{gathered} B^\star_{AR} = \frac{I^\star s_wW_{\mathrm{tot}}}{2N_{\mathrm{act}}}\\[4pt] = \frac{206 \times 2 \times 24.5\text{B}}{2 \times 3.08\text{B}} \approx 1{,}640, \end{gathered} \]

This closed form takes zero context. Holding 1,640 sequences of 9k tokens, the 1k prompt and 8k generated tokens of the running example, would take about 650 GB of KV cache, more than four H200s hold; and by Proposition 8, at a long enough context no batch makes an AR step compute-bound.

Corollary 1 · Proposition 8

Time is bytes. Per sequence, an AR step reads \(Q^{AR}\) for one token; a canvas reads \(\omega\) times \(Q^{TD} = \Pi\,Q^{AR}\) for \(b\) tokens. Hence

\[ \Gamma = \frac{Q^{AR}}{\omega\,\Pi\,Q^{AR}/b} = \frac{\mathrm{TPF}}{\Pi}. \]

TD's extra FLOPs are hidden under the memory time. At batch 1, \(\mathrm{TPF} = 256/15 = 17.1\) and \(\Pi \approx \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) = 7.9\), so \(\Gamma \approx 2.2\); the exact accounting gives 2.53.

Corollary 1 · Figure 1

TD runs at the peak \(\pi\) and AR at \(I_{AR}\,\beta\), so TD's FLOPs run \(I^\star/I_{AR}\) times faster and it does \(\omega\) times as many. The gain is the compute that AR leaves idle, divided by TD's extra work. At short context \(I_{AR}\) grows with the batch and \(\Gamma\) reaches 1 near batch 109. At long context \(I_{AR} \to I_{\mathrm{attn}}\), and at any batch
