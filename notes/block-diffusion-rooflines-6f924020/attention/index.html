The attention part

Part 4 of Block Diffusion Rooflines (Previous: 3 · The linear part | Next: 5 · The whole picture)

At long context a pass reads mostly the KV cache, and every sequence brings its own, so a larger batch no longer raises the intensity of either decoder. A canvas still does: its \(b\) queries share one read of the cache. This chapter gives the costs of dense, sliding-window and sparse attention layers, shows that the selection penalty of a sparse layer is the MoE penalty with the canvas in the role of the batch, and derives the gain when attention dominates.

At long context the attention terms dominate: a pass costs what it reads of each sequence's KV cache. Three kinds of attention layer appear in current models: dense, sliding-window, and sparse in the style of DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA), summarised in the remark below. Table 4 gives, per sequence, what one query costs on each and what a canvas of \(b\) queries costs.

RemarkHow DSA works

For a query at position \(t\), with \(C\) cached tokens \(s\), a DSA layer does three things.

  1. Scan. A small indexer with \(H_I\) heads of dimension \(h_I\), in FP8, scores every cached token:
    \[ I_{t,s} = \sum_{j=1}^{H_I} w_{t,j}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(q^I_{t,j}\cdot k^I_s\bigr),\qquad q^I_{t,j} = W^I_{q,j}\,h_t,\quad k^I_s = W^I_k\,h_s, \]
    \[ \begin{gathered} I_{t,s} = \sum_{j=1}^{H_I} w_{t,j}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(q^I_{t,j}\cdot k^I_s\bigr),\\[4pt] q^I_{t,j} = W^I_{q,j}\,h_t,\qquad k^I_s = W^I_k\,h_s, \end{gathered} \]
    with query-dependent weights \(w_{t,j}\). The index keys \(k^I_s\) are cached, \(\nu_I = H_Ih_Is_I\) bytes per token, and the scan costs \(\alpha_I = 2H_Ih_I\) FLOPs per query and per cached token.
  2. Select. \(S_t = \) the \(m\) cached tokens with the largest \(I_{t,s}\) (\(m = 2048\) in DeepSeek-V3.2).
  3. Attend. Ordinary attention over the selected set, \(u_t = \mathrm{Attn}\bigl(q_t, \{k_s, v_s : s \in S_t\}\bigr)\): \(\alpha_S = 4n_q\sum h_\ell\) FLOPs and \(\nu_S\) KV bytes per selected token, the same constants as a dense layer, over \(m\) tokens instead of \(C\).

The indexer is trained to match the dense attention distribution (KL divergence, during a short dense warm-up and then during sparse training) and receives no gradient from the language-modelling loss. For the AR decoder the scan is the only attention cost that still grows with \(C\); for a canvas, the scan's cache is read once for all \(b\) queries, while each query's selected tokens are its own.

dense (global) \(\alpha_G C\) \(\nu_G C\) \(\nu_G C\), once for \(b\) queries 1

Dense and windowed layers behave alike: the canvas reads the cache once and every query uses it. In a sparse layer the scan behaves the same way, but the selected tokens do not: each query attends to its own \(m\), so the canvas reads the union of \(b\) selections, between \(m\) and the whole cache, for FLOPs that stay at \(m\) per query.

Proposition 5The penalty of a sparse layer

In short. With sparse attention each query selects its own cached tokens and a canvas reads their union: the MoE penalty again, with the canvas length in the role of the batch.

Under (A2), with independent selections, \(\class{mj-td}{I^{TD}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}/\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) with

\[ \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C) - 1 = \frac{\nu_S\,[\,U_{C,m}(b) - m\,]}{\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C)}. \]
  1. \(1 \le \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \le 1 + \dfrac{\nu_S\min(bm, C)}{\nu_I C + \nu_S m}\): the penalty comes only from the selected tokens, and is bounded by what the canvas can read of them beyond one query.
  2. Three stages of context, for a model whose attention is a sparse layer: \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = 1\) for \(C \le m\); \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \approx \dfrac{(\nu_I + \nu_S)\,C}{\nu_I C + \nu_S m}\) for \(m \lt C \lesssim bm\), when the canvas's selections cover the cache; \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \to 1\) as \(C \to \infty\), when the union stops growing at \(bm\) and the scan's bytes dominate.
  3. With a free scan (\(\alpha_I = \nu_I = 0\)) and no other attention layers, \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = \Pi_{\mathrm{sel}} = U_{C,m}(b)/m\), between 1 and \(\min(b, C/m)\), and non-decreasing in \(b\) and \(C\): the exact twin of the expert penalty \(\Pi_E = U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B)\), with the canvas length in the role of the batch size.
Proof

The identity is Definition 4 on the attention part: \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{TD}_{\mathrm{attn}}} - \class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \nu_S[U_{C,m}(b) - \min(C,m)]\), and \(\min(C,m) = m\) for \(C \ge m\). (1) \(m \le U_{C,m}(b) \le \min(bm, C)\), and \(\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}} \ge \nu_I C + \nu_S m\). (2) For \(C \le m\), \(U_{C,m}(b) = C = \min(C,m)\). For \(bm \gg C\), \((1-m/C)^b \approx e^{-bm/C} \to 0\), so \(U_{C,m}(b) \to C\). As \(C \to \infty\), \((1-m/C)^b \to 1 - bm/C\), so \(U_{C,m}(b) \to bm\), a constant, while \(\nu_I C\) grows. (3) \(U_{C,m}\) is increasing and concave in its argument with \(U_{C,m}(1) = m\), so \(U_{C,m}(b)/m \le b\), and \(U_{C,m}(b) \le C\); monotonicity in \(C\) is that of \(C[1 - (1-m/C)^b]\). \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 6When attention dominates

In short. With full windows, attention overtakes the weights in an AR step's bytes at the context of Proposition 4, and in a position's FLOPs at \(C_F(1-\sigma)\), which does not depend on the batch; as the context grows both intensities move to their attention values, \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) for AR and \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) for TD with dense attention.

Under (A2), with the windows full (\(C \ge w\)):

  1. Attention carries at least a share \(x\) of an AR step's bytes exactly when \(C \ge C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, 1-x)\), the threshold of Proposition 4.
  2. Attention carries at least a share \(x\) of a position's FLOPs exactly when \(C \ge C_F\bigl(\tfrac{x}{1-x} - \sigma\bigr)\), with \(C_F = 2N_{\mathrm{act}}/\alpha_G\) and \(\sigma = \alpha_Ww/(2N_{\mathrm{act}})\); the same for AR and TD.
  3. As \(C\) grows, \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}\) move monotonically toward \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I^{TD}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\). With dense attention these are \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\); with sparse attention they are \(I_{\mathrm{idx}}\) and \(b\,I_{\mathrm{idx}}\), the index cache dominating the attention part once \(\nu_IC \gg \nu_S\,bm\).
Proof

(1) is Proposition 4 with the complementary share. (2) With dense attention \(\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}/\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}} \ge x \iff (1-x)(\alpha_GC + \alpha_Ww) \ge 2xN_{\mathrm{act}}\). (3) For \(C \ge w\) each intensity is a ratio of affine functions of \(C\) with positive denominator once \(U_{C,m}\) has saturated at \(bm\), hence monotone, with the stated limits; in the sparse case the \(\nu_IC\) and \(\alpha_IC\) terms dominate. \(\blacksquare\)

\(C_F\) is the context at which global attention alone matches the linear FLOPs; for a dense model with attention in every layer and a 4× MLP it is about \(6d\). For DiffusionGemma \(C_F = 37.6\)k and \(\sigma = 6.8\%\). Attention FLOPs and attention bytes become the majority at very different contexts: a tenth of the FLOPs at 1.6k tokens, half at 35k; half of an AR step's bytes at 291k tokens for batch 1 and 27k for batch 64.

The gain when attention dominates

When attention carries most of the bytes, Corollary 1 reads \(\Gamma = \min\bigl(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}},\ I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}})\bigr)\) as long as \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}} \lt I^\star\), and nothing in it depends on the batch, because a cache belongs to one sequence. Table 6 gives the three quantities for a model whose attention is of one kind, with the windows full and \(C \ge m\).
