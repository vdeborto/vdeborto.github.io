The attention part

Part 4 of Block Diffusion Rooflines: dense, windowed and sparse attention; the selection penalty as the twin of the expert penalty; when attention dominates, and the gain there.

Part 4 of Block Diffusion RooflinesPrevious: 3 · The linear partNext: 5 · The whole picture

At long context a pass reads mostly the KV cache, and every sequence brings its own, so a larger batch no longer raises the intensity of either decoder. A canvas still does: its \(b\) queries share one read of the cache. This chapter gives the costs of dense, sliding-window and sparse attention layers, shows that the selection penalty of a sparse layer is the expert penalty \(\Pi_E\) with the canvas in the role of the batch, and derives the gain when attention dominates.

At long context the attention terms dominate: a pass costs what it reads of each sequence's KV cache. Three kinds of attention layer appear in current models: dense, sliding-window, and sparse in the style of DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA; DeepSeek-AI, 2025), summarised in Remark 1 below. Table 4 gives, per sequence, what one query costs on each and what a canvas of \(b\) queries costs.

Remark 1How DSA works

For a query at position \(i\), with hidden state \(x_i\) and \(C\) cached tokens \(j\), a DSA layer does three things.

  1. Scan. A small indexer with \(H_I\) heads of dimension \(h_I\), in FP8, scores every cached token:
    \[ z_{i,j} = \sum_{a=1}^{H_I} \gamma_{i,a}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(q^I_{i,a}\cdot k^I_j\bigr),\qquad q^I_{i,a} = W^I_{q,a}\,x_i,\quad k^I_j = W^I_k\,x_j, \]
    \[ \begin{gathered} z_{i,j} = \sum_{a=1}^{H_I} \gamma_{i,a}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(q^I_{i,a}\cdot k^I_j\bigr),\\[4pt] q^I_{i,a} = W^I_{q,a}\,x_i,\qquad k^I_j = W^I_k\,x_j, \end{gathered} \]
    with query-dependent weights \(\gamma_{i,a}\). The \(H_I\) heads share one index key per token, so the cache of one sparse layer holds \(k^I_j\) at \(h_Is_I\) bytes per token, with \(s_I\) bytes per element (1 in FP8), while its scan costs \(2H_Ih_I\) FLOPs per query and per cached token; \(\nu_I\) and \(\alpha_I\) of Definition 1 sum these over the sparse layers. Hence the scan's intensity for one query is \(I_{\mathrm{idx}} = \alpha_I/\nu_I = 2H_I/s_I\): 16 FLOP/byte for the 8 FP8 heads used here, and about 124 for DeepSeek-V3.2's 64, whose cache also holds one FP32 scale per key (reference code). The reference indexer computes its queries from the query projection's low-rank latent rather than from \(x_i\); the book's form, with \(q^I\) a projection of \(x_i\), is the version for a model without that latent.
  2. Select. \(\mathcal M_i = \) the \(m\) cached tokens with the largest \(z_{i,j}\) (\(m = 2048\) in DeepSeek-V3.2).
  3. Attend. Ordinary attention over the selected set, \(u_i = \mathrm{Attn}\bigl(q_i, \{k_j, v_j : j \in \mathcal M_i\}\bigr)\): \(\alpha_S = 4n_q\sum h_\ell\) FLOPs and \(\nu_S\) KV bytes per selected token, the same constants as a dense layer, over \(m\) tokens instead of \(C\).

The indexer is trained to match the dense attention distribution (KL divergence, during a short dense warm-up and then during sparse training) and receives no gradient from the language-modelling loss. For the AR decoder the scan is the only attention cost that still grows with \(C\); for a canvas, the scan's cache is read once for all \(b\) queries, while each query's selected tokens are its own.

Dense and windowed layers behave alike: the canvas reads the cache once and every query uses it. In a sparse layer the scan behaves the same way, but the selected tokens do not: each query attends to its own \(m\), so the canvas reads the union of \(b\) selections, between \(m\) and the whole cache, for FLOPs that stay at \(m\) per query.

The FLOPs of a pass are proportional to its full cells, plus its pale cells at the indexer's smaller cost \(\alpha_I\) each, and its bytes to its filled bars, which is the content of Table 4. In a dense or windowed layer the bar is the same for one query and for \(b\), so a canvas has \(b\) times the intensity of an AR step. In a sparse layer each query selects its own tokens, independently and uniformly here, as tokens select experts under (A5), and the bar is the union of the selections, of expected size \(U_{C,m}(b)\); the pale cells are the indexer's scan, whose index keys are read once. The canvas's own positions, which its queries also attend to, are not drawn.

Proposition 5The penalty of a sparse layer

In short. With sparse attention each query selects its own cached tokens and a canvas reads their union: the expert penalty \(\Pi_E\) again, with the canvas length in the role of the batch.

Under (A2) and (A5), \(\class{mj-td}{I^{TD}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}/\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) with

\[ \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C) - 1 = \frac{\nu_S\,[\,U_{C,m}(b) - \min(C, m)\,]}{\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C)}. \]
  1. For \(C \ge m\), \(1 \le \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \le 1 + \dfrac{\nu_S\,[\min(bm, C) - m]}{\nu_I C + \nu_S m} \le b\): the penalty comes only from the selected tokens, and is bounded by what the canvas can read of them beyond one query.
  2. For a model whose attention is a sparse layer, the context falls into three stages:
    • \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = 1\) for \(C \le m\);
    • for \(C \ge m\), \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C)\,\dfrac{\nu_I C + \nu_S m}{(\nu_I + \nu_S)\,C} \to 1\) as \(C/(bm) \to 0\), when the canvas's selections cover the cache;
    • \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \to 1\) as \(C \to \infty\), when the union stops growing at \(bm\) and the scan's bytes dominate.
  3. With a free scan (\(\alpha_I = \nu_I = 0\)) and no other attention layers, \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = \Pi_{\mathrm{sel}}\), which for \(C \ge m\) is \(U_{C,m}(b)/m\), between 1 and \(\min(b, C/m)\), and non-decreasing in \(b\) and \(C\): the exact twin of the expert penalty \(\Pi_E = U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B)\), with the canvas length in the role of the batch size.
Proof

The identity is Definition 4 on the attention part: \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{TD}_{\mathrm{attn}}} - \class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \nu_S[U_{C,m}(b) - \min(C,m)]\), and \(\min(C,m) = m\) for \(C \ge m\). (1) \(m \le U_{C,m}(b) \le \min(bm, C)\), and \(\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}} \ge \nu_I C + \nu_S m\). (2) For \(C \le m\), \(U_{C,m}(b) = C = \min(C,m)\). For \(C \ge m\), \(0 \le (1-m/C)^b \le e^{-bm/C} \to 0\) as \(C/(bm) \to 0\), so \(U_{C,m}(b)/C \to 1\) and \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{TD}_{\mathrm{attn}}}/((\nu_I + \nu_S)C) \to 1\), while \(\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \nu_I C + \nu_S m\). As \(C \to \infty\), \(C\,[1-(1-m/C)^b] \to bm\), a constant, while \(\nu_I C\) grows. (3) \(U_{C,m}\) is increasing and concave in its argument with \(U_{C,m}(1) = m\), so \(U_{C,m}(b)/m \le b\), and \(U_{C,m}(b) \le C\); monotonicity in \(C\) is that of \(C[1 - (1-m/C)^b]\). \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 6When attention dominates

In short. With full windows, attention overtakes the weights in an AR step's bytes at the context of Proposition 4, and in a position's FLOPs at \(C_F(1-\sigma)\), which does not depend on the batch; as the context grows both intensities move to their attention values, \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) for AR and \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) for TD with dense attention.

Under (A2) and (A5), with the windows full (\(C \ge w\)):

  1. With dense attention, attention carries at least a share \(x\) of an AR step's bytes exactly when \(C \ge C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, 1-x)\), the threshold of Proposition 4.
  2. With dense attention, attention carries at least a share \(x\) of a position's FLOPs exactly when \(C \ge C_F\bigl(\tfrac{x}{1-x} - \sigma\bigr)\), with \(C_F = 2N_{\mathrm{act}}/\alpha_G\) and \(\sigma = \alpha_Ww/(2N_{\mathrm{act}})\); the same for AR and TD.
  3. With dense attention, \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}\) are monotone in \(C\) on \(C \ge w\), and tend to \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\). With sparse attention, \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\) is monotone on \(C \ge m\) and tends to \(I_{\mathrm{idx}}\), and \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}} \to b\,I_{\mathrm{idx}}\) as \(C \to \infty\).
Proof

(1) is Proposition 4 with the complementary share. (2) With dense attention \(\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}/\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}} \ge x \iff (1-x)(\alpha_GC + \alpha_Ww) \ge 2xN_{\mathrm{act}}\). (3) For \(C \ge w\), and \(C \ge m\) with sparse layers, \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\), and \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}\) with dense attention, are ratios of affine functions of \(C\) with positive denominators, hence monotone, with the stated limits. For \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}\) with sparse attention, \(U_{C,m}(b) \to bm\), so the \(\nu_IC\) and \(\alpha_IC\) terms dominate as \(C \to \infty\). \(\blacksquare\)

Note that \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}\) need not be monotone with sparse attention: for \(m \lt C \lesssim \nu_S\,bm/\nu_I\) the penalty \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) rises and falls (Figure 11), and \(\class{mj-td}{I^{TD}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}/\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) moves the opposite way.

\(C_F\) is the context at which global attention alone matches the linear FLOPs; for a dense model with attention in every layer and a 4× MLP it is about \(6d\). For DiffusionGemma \(C_F = 37.6\)k and \(\sigma = 6.8\%\). Attention FLOPs and attention bytes reach a given share at very different contexts:

  • a tenth of the FLOPs at 1.6k tokens, and half at 35k;
  • half of an AR step's bytes at 291k tokens for batch 1, and at 27k for batch 64.

The gain when attention dominates

When attention carries most of the bytes and most of the FLOPs of both decoders, \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \approx \class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}} \approx \class{mj-td}{I^{TD}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\), and Corollary 1 reads \(\Gamma = \min\bigl(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}},\ I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}})\bigr)\) as long as \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}} \lt I^\star\); nothing in it depends on the batch, because a cache belongs to one sequence. Both conditions are needed. At large batch attention dominates the bytes long before it dominates the FLOPs, which takes \(C \gtrsim C_F = 37.6\)k: at batch 96 and context 16k, a point that fits in memory, attention carries 52% of an AR step's bytes but 33% of its FLOPs, and Proposition 1 gives 1.56 where this formula gives 2.41. For DiffusionGemma on an H200 (\(I^\star = 206\), \(\omega = 15\), \(b = 256\), \(m = 2048\)), the triple \((\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}, \class{mj-td}{I^{TD}_{\mathrm{attn}}}, \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}})\) and the gain of each kind of layer are:

  • Dense: 8, 2048, 1; TD is compute-bound and the gain is 1.72.
  • Sliding window: 2, 512, 1; the gain would be 6.9, but the window bytes are fixed at \(\nu_Ww\) and dominate the weights only for \(B \gtrsim 230\), past the parity batch \(B_\times \approx 109\), so this limit is not reached.
  • Sparse:
    • at 128k, 13, 158, 22, with TD memory-bound and gain 0.79;
    • at 1M, 16, 311, 13, with TD compute-bound and gain 0.88;
    • as \(C \to \infty\), \(I_{\mathrm{idx}} = 16\), 4096, 1, with gain \(I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{idx}}) = 0.86\).

In the sparse row the limits as \(C \to \infty\) are \(I_{\mathrm{idx}}\), \(b\,I_{\mathrm{idx}}\) and 1, since \(U_{C,m}(b) \to bm\) while the scan's terms keep growing; the gain then tends to \(I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{idx}})\) when \(I_{\mathrm{idx}} \lt I^\star \lt b\,I_{\mathrm{idx}}\). Between \(m\) and \(bm\) the canvas reads nearly the whole cache on the selected tokens, which is the one part of the cost on which it reads more than an AR step; a TD pass can be memory-bound there even though it is compute-bound on the scan. For DiffusionGemma the index cache is small, \(\nu_I = \nu_S/32\), so the selected tokens carry most of a canvas's attention bytes up to \(C \approx \nu_S\,bm/\nu_I \approx 17\)M, where the two terms are equal, and the penalty is large between about 32k and 1M: 11 at 32k, 22 at 128k, 13 at 1M. In the limit an AR step scans at \(I_{\mathrm{idx}} = 16\), twice the dense \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\), so the limit gain 0.86 is half the dense 1.72 and below 1. In particular, under independent selections, sparse attention removes TD's long-context advantage once attention dominates both decoders; with DeepSeek-V3.2's 64 index heads the limit is 0.11. Overlapping selections lower the penalty, not the limit; the calculator models them by the fraction \(\zeta \le 1\) of a canvas's selections that are independent.
