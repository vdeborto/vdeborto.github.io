Empirical rooflines

Part 5 of Block Diffusion Rooflines: the ceilings one H200 sustains, the experts each decoder actually touches, and the bound they give.

Part 5 of Block Diffusion RooflinesPrevious: 4 · ExercisesNext: 6 · The measurement stack

Proposition 1 has two inputs that Parts 1–4 do not measure: the accelerator's rates, taken from its datasheet, and the experts each pass touches, taken from assumption (A3). This chapter measures both on one H200, for DiffusionGemma and Gemma 4. The measured ceilings lower the bound at every point, and routing moves it most: up at small batch, down at large batch.

What one H200 sustains

The H200 datasheet gives 989 TFLOP/s in BF16, 1,979 in FP8 and 4.8 TB/s at the 1,980 MHz boost clock, and no rate for attention. A kernel that keeps the tensor cores (the matrix-multiply units) busy reaches the 700 W power cap, and the GPU then lowers its clock. We therefore measure the rates kernels reach, as in the Empirical Roofline Toolkit (Lo et al., 2014): several benchmarks per rate, with the clock and the power logged during each.

Definition 5Empirical ceilings and empirical roofline

The empirical ceiling of a rate is the highest value that its benchmarks reach on random data, except for attention, whose ceiling is a sustained reference rate:

  • \(\hat\pi_{\mathrm{fp8}}\), \(\hat\pi_{\mathrm{bf16}}\): GEMMs (general matrix multiplications) of 11 shapes from \(2048^3\) to \(32768 \times 4096 \times 4096\), each run for 0.5 s, a short burst of an \(8192^3\) GEMM, and the model's own projections at 1 to 16,384 tokens, replayed from CUDA graphs1 in bursts of about 2 ms;
  • \(\hat\pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\): FlashAttention 3 at head dimension 128 on long square sequences, sustained for 0.5 s. Attention kernels at other shapes can exceed it in short bursts,2 so for attention the ceiling is a sustained reference rate rather than a maximum;
  • \(\hat\beta\): reads and copies of 0.25–8 GB buffers.

A component \(c\) of a pass (experts, dense GEMMs, window attention, global attention, vocabulary head) with \(F_c\) FLOPs and \(Q_c\) bytes of memory traffic has the empirical roofline

\[ \tau_c = \max\Bigl(\frac{F_c}{\hat\pi_c},\ \frac{Q_c}{\hat\beta}\Bigr), \]

with \(\hat\pi_c\) the ceiling of its kind: FP8 GEMM for dense layers and experts, attention for attention, BF16 GEMM for the vocabulary head. The empirical roofline of a step of decoder \(X\) is the sum over its components, \(\tau^X = \sum_c \tau_c\), the kernel-by-kernel rule of Part 1.

  • Power, not the tensor cores, limits sustained compute. GEMMs from \(4096^3\) up that run for half a second hold 1,275–1,530 MHz at 675–695 W and reach 700 TFLOP/s in BF16 and 1,336 in FP8. Per clock cycle, the BF16 rate is 0.93 of the datasheet's, so the kernels are close to ideal and the shortfall is the clock.3 Kernels inside the engine run for milliseconds between memory-bound kernels and see higher clocks, hence the 2 ms bursts count towards the ceiling.
  • Attention has its own ceiling. FlashAttention 3 sustains 638 TFLOP/s, 13% below a BF16 GEMM at the same clock, a gap we attribute to the softmax between its two matrix products.
  • Small GEMMs are bound by latency. With 64 tokens or fewer, the model's projections other than the vocabulary head take 5–13 µs whatever the batch and stream their weights at 0.9–3 TB/s, below \(\hat\beta\); this is the floor of an AR step at small batch.4

Since the ceilings lower compute more than bandwidth (Table 4), they barely change \(\Gamma\) where both decoders are memory-bound and lower it where TD is compute-bound and AR is not.

Which experts a pass touches

The expert weights a pass reads, and so the linear penalty of Part 2, depend on how many distinct experts its tokens select. Under (A3), tokens select independently and uniformly, and \(N\) tokens touch \(U_{E,k}(N) = E\,[1 - (1 - k/E)^N]\) of the \(E = 128\) experts of a layer, with \(k = 8\) per token: a canvas of 256 positions touches all 128 even at batch 1. Our measurement harness, roofscope, counts the router's choices instead: a counter in the engine's router records, for every pass and MoE layer, how many tokens went to each expert.

Definition 6Measured routing

A pass holds \(N\) tokens: \(N = B\) for an AR step and \(N = Bb\) for a TD pass. For a decoder at batch \(B\) and context \(C\):

  • \(U_{\mathrm{meas}}\) is the number of distinct experts a pass touches in one MoE layer, averaged over the 30 layers and the timed passes; across the three repeats of a point, its median.
  • \(\rho_e\) is the probability that a token selects expert \(e\), estimated per layer from all passes of a run, and \(U_{\mathrm{freq}} = \sum_{e=1}^{E} \bigl[1 - (1 - \rho_e)^N\bigr]\) is the count that \(N\) independent tokens with these frequencies would touch.5
  • \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}\), the effective number of independent tokens, solves \(U_{E,k}(N_{\mathrm{eff}}) = U_{\mathrm{meas}}\): the number of tokens that, routed independently and uniformly, would touch as many experts as the pass does.

Then \(U_{\mathrm{freq}}/U_{E,k}(N)\) measures the effect of uneven expert popularity, and \(U_{\mathrm{meas}}/U_{\mathrm{freq}}\) the effect of dependence between the tokens of a pass. Since the frequencies \(\rho_e\) are pooled over all sequences of a run, the second ratio also contains popularity that differs from one sequence to another.

Remark 2Proposition 1 at measured routing

Proposition 1 depends on routing only through the expert weights that a pass reads, \(s_wL\,U\,W^e\) bytes for \(L\) MoE layers of \(W^e\) parameters per expert. Replacing (A3) by the measured counts therefore amounts to replacing \(N\) by \(N_{\mathrm{eff}}\) in the weights of both decoders, \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)\) with \(\class{mj-ar}{U^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{meas}}}\) experts per layer and \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}(B)\) with \(\class{mj-td}{U^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{meas}}}\). In particular, the expert penalty of Proposition 3 becomes

\[ \Pi_E = \frac{U_{E,k}(\class{mj-td}{N^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{eff}}})}{U_{E,k}(\class{mj-ar}{N^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{eff}}})} = \frac{\class{mj-td}{U^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{meas}}}}{\class{mj-ar}{U^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{meas}}}}, \]

and AR's linear intensity \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) = 2BN_{\mathrm{act}}/(s_w\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B))\) changes with \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}\), and so do the expert share \(\theta_E\), the parity batch and every threshold of Part 2 that depends on \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}\) or \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}\), such as \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\). The FLOPs, the KV-cache bytes and the ceilings are unchanged.
