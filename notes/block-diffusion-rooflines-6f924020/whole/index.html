The whole picture

Part 5 of Block Diffusion Rooflines (Previous: 4 · The attention part | Next: 6 · Calculator)

Parts 3 and 4 treat the two extremes, a pass that reads only weights and one that reads only KV cache. This chapter covers every context in between: it states when TD wins (Proposition 7) and the batch it needs to saturate compute (Proposition 8), sorts the cases into eight regimes, compares accelerators and models, and shows the exact gain over batch and context.

Proposition 2 makes each intensity a byte-weighted average of the two parts of §3 and §4. Putting them back into Proposition 1 gives the general answer.

Proposition 7When TD wins

In short. TD wins exactly when a pass serves more tokens per read than it pays in penalty (\(\mathrm{TPF} \ge \Pi\)) and AR uses at most a fraction \(1/\omega\) of the compute (\(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \le I^\star/\omega\)).

Under (A1) to (A4), with \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} = \theta_{\mathrm{lin}}\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}} + \theta_{\mathrm{attn}}\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\) and \(\Pi - 1 = \theta_{\mathrm{lin}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1) + \theta_{\mathrm{attn}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} - 1)\) (shares of an AR step's bytes):

  1. If AR is compute-bound, \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \ge I^\star\), TD loses: \(\Gamma \le 1/\omega\).
  2. Otherwise \(\Gamma \ge 1\) exactly when both \(\mathrm{TPF} \ge \Pi\) and \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \le I^\star/\omega\): a TD pass must serve more tokens per read than it pays in penalty, and AR must use at most a fraction \(1/\omega\) of the compute, so that TD's \(\omega\) times more FLOPs fit in what AR leaves idle.
  3. The gain is \(\Gamma = \min\bigl(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi,\ I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}})\bigr)\), largest where the two are equal, at the batch where a TD pass reaches the ridge.
Proof

(1) and (3) are Corollary 1 with Proposition 2's expressions. (2) A minimum of two numbers is at least 1 exactly when both are. \(\blacksquare\)

Read with §3 and §4: at short context \(\Pi = \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} = \class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(B)\), so the first condition is a lower bound on the batch (the MoE penalty must fall below TPF) and the second an upper bound (\(B_\times\)); at long context \(\Pi = \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} = \class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\), and neither depends on the batch. For DiffusionGemma on an H200, both conditions hold at every batch up to 109 at short context, and at every batch at long context.

Proposition 8The batch needed to saturate compute

In short. With dense attention and full windows, a batch cannot saturate compute on cache bytes, since each sequence brings its own cache, but a canvas can: once \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \gt I^\star\), a longer context makes a TD pass compute-bound at a smaller batch, not a larger one.

Let \(B^\star_n(C)\) be the smallest batch at which a pass with \(n\) queries per sequence (\(n = 1\) for AR, \(n = b\) for TD) is compute-bound, or \(\infty\) if there is none, with dense attention and the windows full (\(C \ge w\)).

  1. If \(n\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \lt I^\star\), then \(B^\star_n(C)\) is non-decreasing in \(C\), and infinite beyond some finite context.
  2. If \(n\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \gt I^\star\), then \(B^\star_n(C)\) is non-increasing in \(C\), and equal to 1 beyond some finite context.

AR is in the first case whenever \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} \lt I^\star\), which holds for every model and accelerator here. TD is in the second case exactly when \(b \gt T_c = I^\star/I_{\mathrm{attn}}\).

Proof

Write \(D_n(C) = n\,\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}(C) - I^\star \class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C)\). Multiplying out \(I_n \ge I^\star\) gives \(B\,D_n(C) \ge I^\star s_wW_n(B)\): each sequence contributes \(n\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}\) FLOPs, of which \(I^\star \class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\) are needed to make its own cache reads compute-bound, and what is left, summed over the batch, has to cover the weights. The right side is positive, so no batch works when \(D_n \le 0\). For \(C \ge w\), \(D_n\) is affine in \(C\) with slope \((nI_{\mathrm{attn}} - I^\star)\,\nu_G\). A smaller \(D_n\) can only remove batches from the feasible set, so a negative slope makes \(B^\star_n\) non-decreasing and eventually infinite; a positive slope makes the set grow until batch 1 works, once \(D_n \ge I^\star s_wW_n(1)\). Nothing needs a closed form for \(W_n(B)\), so the argument covers MoE models. \(\blacksquare\)

The canvas does for the KV cache what batch does for the weights.

A larger batch does not help with cache bytes, because every sequence brings its own cache. AR uses each cache byte for one query, so at long context its intensity drifts toward \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\), far below the ridge, whatever the batch. A canvas uses each cache byte for \(b\) queries, and once \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \gt I^\star\) a longer context makes compute easier to saturate, not harder. When \(W_n\) does not depend on \(B\), solving the inequality gives \(B^\star_n(C) = \lceil I^\star s_wW_n/D_n(C)\rceil\) for \(D_n \gt 0\), which at zero context is the \(\class{mj-ar}{B^\star_{AR}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}}\) of §3.

Here \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 8\) and \(T_c = 206/8 \approx 26\). AR needs a batch of 1,640 at zero context, and past 135 tokens no batch is enough. TD with \(b = 256\) needs 6.4 at zero context, and batch 1 is enough from about 224k tokens. TD with \(b = 32\) first needs a slightly larger batch while the windows fill (\(b\,I_{\mathrm{win}} = 64 \lt I^\star\)), then a smaller one once global attention dominates (\(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 256 \gt I^\star\)). TD with \(b = 16\) is below \(T_c\) and behaves like AR. The grey region is where the weights and the batch's cache exceed the H200's 141 GB: at 1k tokens about 390 sequences fit, far below the 1,640 that AR would need, so for DiffusionGemma on an H200 AR decoding is memory-bound at every batch that fits.

Eight regimes

Each minimum in Proposition 1 asks whether one decoder is compute- or memory-bound. Answering both questions at short context (\(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} = \class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}\), \(\Pi = \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\)) and at long context with dense attention (\(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} = I_{\mathrm{attn}}\), \(\Pi = 1\)) gives eight regimes. Four of them are the ones of §3 and §4; the other four need AR compute-bound, which no batch that fits in memory achieves here.

RemarkKernels run one after another

In short. Timing a pass as a chain of kernels instead of one fully overlapped kernel is never faster and at most twice slower; it moves DiffusionGemma's best batch from 5 to 9.

(A1) gives a pass the time of one kernel in which all of its compute and memory traffic overlap. A pass is a chain of kernels, and adding their roofline times, \(\sum_c \max(F_c/\pi, Q_c/\beta)\) over kernel classes, is never faster and at most twice slower. For an AR step at moderate batch every class is memory-bound and nothing changes. For a TD pass the dense layers are compute-bound from batch 1 while the experts stay memory-bound until \(Bbk/E \approx I^\star\), batch 13 here; the best batch moves from 5 to 9 and the peak gain from 5.1× to 3.7×. The calculator offers both rules.

The exact maps

The maps drop the propositions' simplifications and use the exact accounting (appendix), vocabulary included, over batch × context. Every line is a contour of the computed grid. Grey cells do not fit in the accelerator's memory: weights, KV cache and one pass's logits, which are \(b\) times larger for a TD canvas than for an AR step. On the gain maps, grey marks where TD does not fit and hatching where AR does not fit either. Hover or tap a cell for its values.

Context pulls AR toward \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 8\) from either side, never reaching the ridge, and pulls TD toward \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 2048\), across it. On the gain map, the bottom-left is R1, the band above the TD-compute line is R2, and the colour flattens toward R6's 1.7 at long context; with sparse layers it keeps rising instead.
