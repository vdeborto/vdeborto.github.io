The linear part

Part 3 of Block Diffusion Rooflines (Previous: 2 · The gain | Next: 4 · The attention part)

At short context a pass reads mostly weights, which the batch shares, so the batch \(B\) sets both intensities. This chapter computes the MoE penalty, the extra expert weights that a canvas reads (7.9 for DiffusionGemma at batch 1, falling to 1 as the batch grows), states up to which context the linear layers dominate, and derives the gain in that regime. In particular, the best batch is the one at which a TD pass just becomes compute-bound.

At short context the attention terms are negligible and a pass costs what it reads of the weights: \(\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B)\) for an AR step, \(\class{mj-td}{W_{TD}}(B)\) for a TD pass, which uses every byte for \(b\) times more positions.

The numbers behind the batch-1 headline, from the exact model at 1k context, written as a back-of-the-envelope calculation. Each column reads from the bytes per generated token to the share of the accelerator's peak that this rate of tokens uses; that share is \(I/I^\star\).

                       AR step                                  TD canvas (ω = 15 passes)
bytes per token        7.9 GB  (7.6 GB weights, 0.2 GB cache)     3.1 GB  (ω · 49 GB / 256 positions)
bandwidth bound        4.8 TB/s / 7.9 GB   =   609 tok/s          4.8 TB/s / 3.1 GB   = 1,541 tok/s
compute at that rate   609 × 8.2 GFLOP     =   5.0 TFLOP/s        1,541 × 143 GFLOP   =   220 TFLOP/s
share of peak          5.0 / 989           =   0.5 %  = I_AR/I*   220 / 989           =    22 %  = I_TD/I*
gain                                                              1,541 / 609 = 2.53

With vocabulary included, hence 7.6 GB of weights for 3.08B active parameters: the 0.74B embedding matrix is read for every token. Both decoders are far below the ridge, so the gain is the ratio of bytes per token, \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi\): TD reads 7.9/3.1 = 2.5 times fewer bytes per token.

Proposition 3The MoE penalty

In short. A canvas touches almost every expert, so at batch 1 TD streams the total parameters where AR streams the active ones (a penalty of 7.9), and the penalty vanishes once the batch alone touches every expert.

Under (A2), \(\class{mj-td}{I^{TD}_{\mathrm{lin}}} = b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\), with \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1 = \theta_E\,(\Pi_E - 1)\), where \(\theta_E = L\,U_{E,k}(B)\,W^e/\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B)\) is the expert share of the weights an AR step reads and \(\Pi_E = U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B)\). Moreover:

  1. \(1 \le \Pi_E \le \min(b,\ E/k)\), and \(\Pi_E\) is non-increasing in \(B\): \(\Pi_E - 1 \le (E/k)\,e^{-kB/E}\), so the penalty disappears at large batch.
  2. At \(b \gg E/k\), a canvas touches every expert and \(\Pi_E \approx E/U_{E,k}(B)\), which at batch 1 is \(E/k\); then \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) \approx W_{\mathrm{tot}}/N_{\mathrm{act}}\), total over active parameters.
  3. For a dense model (\(k = E\)), \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} \equiv 1\).
Proof

The first identity is Definition 4 restricted to the linear part; the split of \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1\) is Proposition 2's argument applied to the dense and expert parts of the weights, the dense part having penalty 1. (1) \(U_{E,k}\) is increasing and concave with \(U_{E,k}(0) = 0\) and \(U_{E,k}(N) \le \min(kN, E)\), so \(U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B) \le b\) by concavity and \(\le E/U_{E,k}(B) \le E/k\); and \(U_{E,k}(Bb) - U_{E,k}(B) \le E(1-k/E)^B \le Ee^{-kB/E}\) with \(U_{E,k}(B) \ge k\). (2) \(U_{E,k}(Bb) \ge E(1 - e^{-kb/E}) \approx E\), and \(U_{E,k}(1) = k\); \(\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(1) = W_{\mathrm{ne}} + LkW^e = N_{\mathrm{act}}\). (3) \(U_{E,k}(N) = E\) for all \(N \ge 1\). \(\blacksquare\)

For DiffusionGemma, \(E/k = 16\) and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) = 24.5/3.08 = 7.9\): a canvas streams all 24.5B parameters where a token streams the 3.08B it activates, and the dense 1.66B, shared by both, dilute the ratio from 16 to 7.9. By batch 48 (three times \(E/k\)) an AR step touches every expert too, and the penalty is gone.

Proposition 4When the linear layers dominate

In short. With dense attention and full windows, the weights dominate an AR step's bytes up to a context that shrinks as the batch grows, like \(1/B\) once the batch touches every expert, because the batch shares the weights but each sequence brings its own cache.

Under (A2), with the windows full (\(C \ge w\)) and dense attention, an AR step spends at least a share \(x\) of its bytes on weights exactly when

\[ C \;\le\; C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, x) = \frac{1}{\nu_G}\Bigl(\frac{1-x}{x}\cdot\frac{s_w\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B)}{B} - \nu_W\,w\Bigr). \]
\[ \begin{gathered} C \le C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, x)\\[2pt] = \frac{1}{\nu_G}\Bigl(\frac{1-x}{x}\,\frac{s_w\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B)}{B} - \nu_W w\Bigr). \end{gathered} \]

Moreover:

  1. \(C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B,x)\) is non-increasing in \(B\): the weights are shared across the batch, the cache is not. Once the batch touches every expert (\(\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B) = W_{\mathrm{tot}}\)), and for dense models at every batch, it falls exactly like \(1/B\); below that it falls more slowly, since \(\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B)\) still grows with \(B\).
  2. For a TD pass, replace \(\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}\) by \(\class{mj-td}{W_{TD}}\): its threshold is \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\) times further out, up to the window term.
  3. With sparse layers, replace \(\nu_G C\) by \(\nu_S\min(C,m) + \nu_I C\) for an AR step and by \(\nu_S U_{C,m}(b) + \nu_I C\) for a TD pass.
Proof

\(\class{mj-ar}{\theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}} \ge x \iff s_w\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}/B \ge \tfrac{x}{1-x}\,(\nu_GC + \nu_Ww)\); solve for \(C\). (1) \(\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B)/B\) is non-increasing because \(U_{E,k}\) is concave with \(U_{E,k}(0) = 0\), and constant over \(B\) once \(U_{E,k}(B) = E\). (2) and (3) substitute the TD and sparse byte counts of Definition 2. \(\blacksquare\)

DiffusionGemma's cache is small (2 KV heads in 5 global layers, \(\nu_G = 20\) kB per token, plus a fixed 210 MB per sequence for the windows), so its linear layers dominate for a long way. A dense GQA model such as Llama-3-8B, with 131 kB per token and 16 GB of weights, reaches equal weight and cache bytes at \(BC \approx 122\)k.

The gain when the linear layers dominate

With \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \approx \class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}\) and \(\Pi \approx \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\), Corollary 1 reads \(\Gamma = \min\bigl(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}},\ I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}})\bigr)\) as long as AR is memory-bound. The two terms cross where a TD pass reaches the ridge, and the three batch thresholds follow from \(b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} = I^\star\), \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}} = I^\star\) and \(I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}) = 1\):

\[ \class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}} = \frac{I^\star s_w\class{mj-td}{W_{TD}}}{2b\,N_{\mathrm{act}}},\qquad \class{mj-ar}{B^\star_{AR}} = \frac{I^\star s_wW_{\mathrm{tot}}}{2N_{\mathrm{act}}},\qquad B_\times = \frac{\class{mj-ar}{B^\star_{AR}}}{\omega}. \]
\[ \begin{gathered} \class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}} = \frac{I^\star s_w\class{mj-td}{W_{TD}}}{2b\,N_{\mathrm{act}}},\qquad \class{mj-ar}{B^\star_{AR}} = \frac{I^\star s_wW_{\mathrm{tot}}}{2N_{\mathrm{act}}},\\[4pt] B_\times = \frac{\class{mj-ar}{B^\star_{AR}}}{\omega}. \end{gathered} \]
  • Both memory-bound, \(B \lt \class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}}\): \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\). The gain rises with the batch as \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) falls.
  • TD compute-bound, \(\class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}} \le B \lt \class{mj-ar}{B^\star_{AR}}\): \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(B))\). Since \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}\) grows with the batch the gain falls like \(1/B\), reaching parity at \(B_\times\). AR need not be compute-bound to win: \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}} \gt I^\star/\omega\) is enough.
  • The best batch is \(\class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}}\), where the two expressions meet: \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(\class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}})\), i.e. 6.5 at batch 6.4 under the propositions and 5.1 at batch 5 in the exact model. Since \(\class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}} \approx \class{mj-ar}{B^\star_{AR}}/b\), a longer canvas moves it earlier and raises it.

The closed forms for \(\class{mj-ar}{B^\star_{AR}}\) and \(B_\times\) assume that AR's batch already touches every expert; for DiffusionGemma on an H200 the three thresholds are 6.4, 1,640 and 109.

Over integer batches the maximum is the last memory-bound batch or the first compute-bound one. With the kernels of a pass timed one after another instead (the remark of §5), the dense layers of a TD pass are compute-bound before its experts are, and the peak no longer sits at a single transition: it is lower on every accelerator here, and on most of them it comes later (batch 9 instead of 5 on an H200).
