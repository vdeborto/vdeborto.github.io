The linear part

Part 2 of Block Diffusion Rooflines: the expert and linear penalties, when the linear layers dominate, and the gain in that regime.

Part 2 of Block Diffusion RooflinesPrevious: 1 · Setup and the gainNext: 3 · The attention part

At short context, a pass reads mostly weights. The batch shares these weights, hence the batch \(B\) sets both intensities. In this chapter, we compute the linear penalty \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\), i.e., the ratio of the weight bytes a TD pass reads to those an AR step reads. For DiffusionGemma, it is 7.9 at batch 1 and falls to 1 as the batch grows. We then state up to which context the linear layers dominate, and derive the gain in that regime. In particular, the best batch is the one at which a TD pass just becomes compute-bound.

We start with short contexts, where the attention terms are negligible. Hence, the bytes of a pass are those of the weights it reads: \(s_w\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)\) for an AR step and \(s_w\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}(B)\) for a TD pass. The TD pass computes \(b\) times as many positions while reading \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\) times the weight bytes, so its linear intensity is \(b/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\) times AR's (Proposition 3 below).

At batch 1 and context 1k, for example, an AR step reads 6.4 GB per token: 6.17 GB of weights and 0.23 GB of cache. A TD canvas reads \(15 \times 49.2\) GB over 256 positions, i.e., 2.9 GB per token. Both are far below the ridge, so the gain is the ratio of bytes, 6.4/2.9 = 2.2, the value of Proposition 1. These counts leave out the vocabulary, as (A2) does; with it the gain is 2.53 (Table 1).

The expert and linear penalties

Proposition 3The expert penalty and the linear penalty

Under (A2) and (A3), \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{lin}}} = b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\), with \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1 = \theta_E\,(\Pi_E - 1)\), where \(\theta_E = L\,U_{E,k}(B)\,W^e/\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)\) is the expert share of the weights an AR step reads and \(\Pi_E = U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B)\). Moreover:

  1. \(1 \le \Pi_E \le \min(b,\ E/k)\), and \(\Pi_E\) is non-increasing in \(B\), with \(\Pi_E - 1 \le (E/k)\,e^{-kB/E}\), so \(\Pi_E \to 1\) as \(B \to \infty\).
  2. As \(b \to \infty\), a canvas touches every expert: \(\Pi_E \to E/U_{E,k}(B)\), which at batch 1 is \(E/k\), and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) \to W_{\mathrm{tot}}/N_{\mathrm{act}}\), total over active parameters, with \(W_{\mathrm{tot}} = W_{\mathrm{ne}} + L\,E\,W^e\).
  3. For a dense model (\(k = E\)), \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} \equiv 1\).
  4. \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} \le b\), and with Propositions 2 and 5, \(\Pi \le b\).
Proof

The first identity is Definition 4 restricted to the linear part; the split of \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1\) is Proposition 2's argument applied to the dense and expert parts of the weights, the dense part having penalty 1. (1) \(U_{E,k}\) is increasing and concave with \(U_{E,k}(0) = 0\) and \(U_{E,k}(N) \le \min(kN, E)\), so \(U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B) \le b\) by concavity and \(\le E/U_{E,k}(B) \le E/k\). With \(q = (1-k/E)^B\), \(\Pi_E = (1-q^b)/(1-q) = 1 + q + \dots + q^{b-1}\), which is increasing in \(q\), and \(q\) decreases with \(B\); and \(U_{E,k}(Bb) - U_{E,k}(B) \le E(1-k/E)^B \le Ee^{-kB/E}\) with \(U_{E,k}(B) \ge k\). (2) \(E \ge U_{E,k}(Bb) \ge E(1 - e^{-kb/E}) \to E\) as \(b \to \infty\), and \(U_{E,k}(1) = k\); \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(1) = W_{\mathrm{ne}} + LkW^e = N_{\mathrm{act}}\), while \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}(1) \to W_{\mathrm{ne}} + LEW^e = W_{\mathrm{tot}}\). (3) \(U_{E,k}(N) = E\) for all \(N \ge 1\). (4) \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1 = \theta_E(\Pi_E - 1) \le b - 1\) by (1) and \(\theta_E \le 1\). By Proposition 2, \(\Pi\) is a weighted mean of \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\), and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \le b\): by Proposition 5(1) for \(C \ge m\), and since its identity gives \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = 1\) for \(C \le m\). \(\blacksquare\)

For DiffusionGemma at batch 1, a canvas of 256 positions touches all 128 experts of a layer and an AR token touches 8, so \(\Pi_E = E/k = 16\). The 1.66B dense parameters are read by both and dilute this to \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) = 24.5/3.08 = 7.9\) (Figure 8).

When the linear layers dominate

Proposition 4When the linear layers dominate

Under (A2) and (A3), with the windows full (\(C \ge w\)) and dense attention, an AR step spends at least a share \(x\) of its bytes on weights exactly when

\[ C \;\le\; C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, x) = \frac{1}{\nu_G}\Bigl(\frac{1-x}{x}\cdot\frac{s_w\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)}{B} - \nu_W\,w\Bigr). \]
\[ \begin{gathered} C \le C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, x)\\[2pt] = \frac{1}{\nu_G}\Bigl(\frac{1-x}{x}\,\frac{s_w\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)}{B} - \nu_W w\Bigr). \end{gathered} \]

Moreover:

  1. \(C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B,x)\) is non-increasing in \(B\): the weights are shared across the batch, the cache is not. Once the batch touches every expert (\(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B) \to W_{\mathrm{tot}}\), as \(B \to \infty\) under (A3)), and for dense models at every batch, it is affine in \(1/B\), a multiple of \(1/B\) minus the constant window term; below that it falls more slowly, since \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)\) still grows with \(B\).
  2. For a TD pass, replace \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}\) by \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}\): its threshold is \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\) times further out, up to the window term.
  3. With sparse layers, replace \(\nu_G C\) by \(\nu_S\min(C,m) + \nu_I C\) for an AR step and by \(\nu_S U_{C,m}(b) + \nu_I C\) for a TD pass.
Proof

\(\class{mj-ar}{\theta^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}} \ge x \iff s_w\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}/B \ge \tfrac{x}{1-x}\,(\nu_GC + \nu_Ww)\); solve for \(C\). (1) \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)/B\) is non-increasing because \(U_{E,k}\) is concave with \(U_{E,k}(0) = 0\). When \(U_{E,k}(B) = E\) (at every \(B\) for a dense model, and as \(B \to \infty\) for an MoE under (A3)), \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B) = W_{\mathrm{tot}}\) is constant, so \(C_{\mathrm{lin}}\) is affine in \(1/B\). (2) and (3) substitute the TD and sparse byte counts of Definition 2. \(\blacksquare\)

DiffusionGemma's cache is small: 2 KV heads in 5 global layers, i.e., \(\nu_G = 20\) KiB per token, plus a fixed 210 MB per sequence for the windows. Therefore, its linear layers dominate for a long way. On the other hand, a dense model with grouped-query attention (GQA) such as Llama 3.1 8B has 128 KiB per token and 14 GB of weights without the vocabulary. It reaches equal weight and cache bytes at \(BC \approx 107\)k.

The gain when the linear layers dominate

We can now derive the gain in this regime. With \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx \class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}\) and \(\Pi \approx \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\), Corollary 1 reads \(\Gamma = \min\bigl(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}},\ I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}})\bigr)\) as long as AR is memory-bound. The two terms cross where a TD pass reaches the ridge. The three batch thresholds, \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\) where a TD pass reaches the ridge, \(\class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}\) where an AR step does, and the parity batch \(B_\times\), follow from \(b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} = I^\star\), \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}} = I^\star\) and \(I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}) = 1\):

\[ \class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} = \frac{I^\star s_w\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}}{2b\,N_{\mathrm{act}}},\qquad \class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star} = \frac{I^\star s_wW_{\mathrm{tot}}}{2N_{\mathrm{act}}},\qquad B_\times = \frac{\class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}}{\omega}. \]
\[ \begin{gathered} \class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} = \frac{I^\star s_w\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}}{2b\,N_{\mathrm{act}}},\qquad \class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star} = \frac{I^\star s_wW_{\mathrm{tot}}}{2N_{\mathrm{act}}},\\[4pt] B_\times = \frac{\class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}}{\omega}. \end{gathered} \]
  • Both memory-bound, \(B \lt \class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\): \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\). The gain rises with the batch as \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) falls2.
  • TD compute-bound, \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} \le B \lt \class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}\): \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(B))\). Since \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}\) grows with the batch, the gain falls, exactly as \(1/B\) once an AR step touches every expert, and reaches parity at \(B_\times\). Note that AR need not be compute-bound to win: \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}} \gt I^\star/\omega\) is enough.
  • The best batch is \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\), where the two expressions meet: \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star})\), i.e., 6.5 at batch 6.4 under the propositions3 and 5.3 at batch 5 in the exact accounting at context 1k, both on an H200 (Figure 10). Since \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} \approx \class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}/b\), a longer canvas lowers the best batch and raises the gain there.

The closed forms for \(\class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}\) and \(B_\times\) assume that AR's batch already touches every expert. For DiffusionGemma on an H200, the three thresholds are 6.4, 1,640 and 109.
