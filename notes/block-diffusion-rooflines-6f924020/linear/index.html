Weights and cache

Part 2 of Block Diffusion Rooflines: the expert and linear penalties and the gain when the linear layers dominate; dense, windowed and sparse attention, the selection penalty as the twin of the expert penalty, and the gain when attention dominates; the batch that saturates compute at any context.

Part 2 of Block Diffusion RooflinesPrevious: 1 · Setup and the gainNext: 3 · Throughput calculator

In this section, we go into the details of the results of Proposition 1. In fact, we are going to analyse two specific regimes:

  • At short context, the network can be approximated by its linear layers only.
  • At large context, the network can be approximated by its attention layers only.

Investigating those different regimes also highlight the roles of the different quantities that dictate the throughput gains of TD over AR. For instance, one of our main conclusion will be that the canvas size \(b\) is for the attention layers what the batch size \(B\) is for the linear layers, i.e., the critical hyperparameter which maximises the arithmetic intensity of that specific operation.

Finally, by looking at each operation independently we can better understand what drives the limitations and the gains of each kernel. This is especially useful as some operations might be memory bound while other might be compute bound.

Each section is split into the following categories:

  • What are the sparsity penalty and main quantities in that regime (linear or attention)?
  • When does the regime dominates?
  • What is the throughput gain in that regime limit?

Linear layers

The expert and linear penalties

The main result of this section is to compare the arithmetic intensity of TD against the one of AR in Proposition 3. In particular, we show that \(\frac{b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}}{\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)}\). This means that, looking only at the linear layers, if \(b/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) > 1\) a forward pass of TD is more efficient than one of AR. However, for this to happen we need \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) \leq b \). For this to happen, we need to control the sparsity penalty that is due to the MoE in linear layers. This is the purpose of Proposition 4, in which we show that the penalty disappears as \(B\) grows since then both AR and TD pay the price of loading most of the experts. Another specific setting in which the penalty disappears is for dense models where there is no sparsity.

Proposition 3Linear layer arithmetic intensity

Under (A2) and (A3), let \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) = \class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}(B)/\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)\) be the linear penalty (Definition 4), which depends on \(b\) through \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}\), \(\theta_E = L\,U_{E,k}(B)\,W^e/\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B) \in (0,1)\) the expert share of the weights an AR step reads, and \(\Pi_E = U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B)\) the expert penalty. Then

\[ \class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{lin}}} = \frac{b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}}{\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)},\qquad \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} = 1 + \theta_E\,(\Pi_E - 1),\qquad 1 \le \Pi_E \le \min\Bigl(b,\ \frac{E}{k}\Bigr). \]
\[ \begin{gathered} \class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{lin}}} = \frac{b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}}{\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)},\qquad \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1 = \theta_E\,(\Pi_E - 1),\\[4pt] 1 \le \Pi_E \le \min\Bigl(b,\ \frac{E}{k}\Bigr). \end{gathered} \]
Proof

The first identity is Definition 4 restricted to the linear part; the split of \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1\) is Proposition 2's argument applied to the dense and expert parts of the weights, the dense part having penalty 1. \(U_{E,k}\) is increasing and concave with \(U_{E,k}(0) = 0\) and \(U_{E,k}(N) \le \min(kN, E)\), so \(U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B) \le b\) by concavity and \(\le E/U_{E,k}(B) \le E/k\). \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 4The linear penalty at the extremes

Under (A2) and (A3):

  1. Large batch: \(\Pi_E\) is non-increasing in \(B\), with
    \[ \Pi_E \le 1 + \frac{E}{k}\,e^{-kB/E}, \]
    so \(\Pi_E \to 1\) and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} \to 1\) as \(B \to \infty\).
  2. Long canvas: as \(b \to \infty\), a canvas touches every expert: \(\Pi_E \to E/U_{E,k}(B)\), which at batch 1 is \(E/k\), and the linear penalty at batch 1 tends to total over active parameters,
    \[ \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) \to \frac{W_{\mathrm{tot}}}{N_{\mathrm{act}}},\qquad W_{\mathrm{tot}} = W_{\mathrm{ne}} + L\,E\,W^e. \]
  3. Dense model (\(k = E\)): \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} \equiv 1\).
Proof

(1) With \(q = (1-k/E)^B\), \(\Pi_E = (1-q^b)/(1-q) = 1 + q + \dots + q^{b-1}\), which is increasing in \(q\), and \(q\) decreases with \(B\); and \(U_{E,k}(Bb) - U_{E,k}(B) \le E(1-k/E)^B \le Ee^{-kB/E}\) with \(U_{E,k}(B) \ge k\). Since \(\theta_E \le 1\), \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1 = \theta_E(\Pi_E - 1) \to 0\) as well (Proposition 3). (2) \(E \ge U_{E,k}(Bb) \ge E(1 - e^{-kb/E}) \to E\) as \(b \to \infty\), and \(U_{E,k}(1) = k\); \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(1) = W_{\mathrm{ne}} + LkW^e = N_{\mathrm{act}}\), while \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}(1) \to W_{\mathrm{ne}} + LEW^e = W_{\mathrm{tot}}\). (3) \(U_{E,k}(N) = E\) for all \(N \ge 1\). \(\blacksquare\)

When are we in the linear layer regime?

We have now established that the arithmetic intensity of TD and AR are linked through the canvas size \(b\) and the linear penalty \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\). Recall that in Proposition 2, we have shown that for each method the arithmetic intensity \(I^X\) with \(X \in \{\mathrm{AR}, \mathrm{TD}\}\) can be decomposed between \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}^X\), the linear layer regime, and \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}^X\) as follows: \( I^X = \theta_{\mathrm{lin}}^X I_{\mathrm{lin}}^X + \theta_{\mathrm{attn}}^X I_{\mathrm{attn}}^X\). In Proposition 5, we give a condition on the batch size \(B\) and the context \(C\) so that the \(\theta_{\mathrm{lin}}^X \geq x\).

Proposition 5When the linear layers dominate

Under (A2) and (A3), with the windows full (\(C \ge w\)) and dense attention, let, for \(X \in \{\mathrm{AR}, \mathrm{TD}\}\) and \(x \in (0,1)\),

\[ C^X_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, x) = \frac{1}{\nu_G}\Bigl(\frac{1-x}{x}\cdot\frac{s_wW^X(B)}{B} - \nu_W\,w\Bigr). \]
\[ \begin{gathered} C^X_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, x)\\[2pt] = \frac{1}{\nu_G}\Bigl(\frac{1-x}{x}\,\frac{s_wW^X(B)}{B} - \nu_W w\Bigr). \end{gathered} \]

Then \(\theta^X_{\mathrm{lin}} \ge x\) exactly when \(C \le C^X_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, x)\). Moreover, \(C^X_{\mathrm{lin}}(\cdot, x)\) is non-increasing in \(B\), \(\class{mj-td}{C^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{lin}}} \ge \class{mj-ar}{C^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}\), and \(\max(C^X_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, x), 0) \to 0\) as \(B \to \infty\).

Proof

With dense attention and \(C \ge w\), Definition 2 gives \(Q^X_{\mathrm{attn}} = \nu_GC + \nu_Ww\) for both decoders, so \(\theta^X_{\mathrm{lin}} \ge x \iff s_wW^X(B)/B \ge \tfrac{x}{1-x}\,(\nu_GC + \nu_Ww)\); solve for \(C\). \(W^X(B)/B\) is non-increasing because \(U_{E,k}\) is concave with \(U_{E,k}(0) = 0\) (for TD, \(U_{E,k}(Bb)/B = b\,U_{E,k}(Bb)/(Bb)\)), and \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}} \ge \class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}\). As \(B \to \infty\), \(W^X(B)/B \le W_{\mathrm{tot}}/B \to 0\), so \(C^X_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, x) \to -\nu_Ww/\nu_G \lt 0\). \(\blacksquare\)

The gain when the linear layers dominate

We finish our investigation of the linear regime by stating Corollary 2, which is a further simplification of Corollary 1, in the linear layer regime.

Corollary 2The gain when the linear layers dominate

Under the assumptions of Proposition 1, assume that the linear layers dominate both passes, i.e., \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx \class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}\), \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}} \approx \class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}\) and \(\Pi \approx \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\), that \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(B) \lt I^\star\), and that \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) is non-increasing in the batch2. Then

\[ \Gamma = \min\Bigl(\frac{\mathrm{TPF}}{\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)},\ \frac{I^\star}{\omega\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(B)}\Bigr), \]

which rises with the batch up to the batch at which a TD pass reaches the ridge,

\[ \class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} = \frac{I^\star s_w\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star})}{2b\,N_{\mathrm{act}}}, \]

and falls beyond it, as \(1/B\) once an AR step touches every expert. Moreover:

  1. Best batch: if \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} \ge 1\) and a batch of that size fits, \(\Gamma\) is maximal at \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\), where it equals \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star})\); otherwise it is maximal at \(B = 1\), or at the largest batch that fits.
  2. Parity: if \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} \le B_\times\), then \(\Gamma = 1\) at \(B_\times = \class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}/\omega\), with \(\class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star} = I^\star s_wW_{\mathrm{tot}}/(2N_{\mathrm{act}})\) the batch at which an AR step that touches every expert reaches the ridge.
Proof

By Definition 2 and Proposition 3, \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(B) = 2BN_{\mathrm{act}}/(s_w\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B))\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(B) = 2BbN_{\mathrm{act}}/(s_w\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}(B))\). Since \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}} \lt I^\star\), AR is memory-bound and Corollary 1(1) gives the formula. Its first term is the smaller exactly when \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{lin}}} \lt I^\star\), i.e., \(B \lt \class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\); it rises because \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) is non-increasing, and the second term falls because \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)/B\) is non-increasing (proof of Proposition 5), as \(1/B\) when \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}} = W_{\mathrm{tot}}\). Since \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}(B)/B\) is decreasing, \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}\) is increasing in \(B\), so \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\) is unique. (1) is Corollary 1(2): case (c) when \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} \ge 1\) fits, case (a) when \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} \lt 1\), and case (b) when no batch that fits reaches the ridge. (2) With \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}} = W_{\mathrm{tot}}\), \(I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}) = 1\) gives \(B_\times = \class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}/\omega\), which lies on the falling branch when \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} \le B_\times\); otherwise \(\Gamma(B_\times) = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B_\times) \lt 1\). Since \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B) \le W_{\mathrm{tot}}\), the batch at which AR actually reaches the ridge is at most \(\class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}\), and equal to it within rounding for DiffusionGemma, where \(U_{E,k}(1{,}640)\) differs from \(E\) by a relative amount \((15/16)^{1640}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Attention layers

Dense, windowed and sparse attention

The case of attention layers is maybe more subtle and interesting. In fact, attention layers are really were TD shines above AR. Indeed, in order to maximize the arithmetic intensity of linear layers for AR (or TD) one can simply increase the batch size \(B\). And therefore, we can have \(I^{X}_{\mathrm{lin}} \geq I^\star\) for \(X \in \{\mathrm{AR}, \mathrm{TD}\}\) which means that if \(I^{X} \approx I^{X}_{\mathrm{lin}}\) then both models are in the compute-bound regime and TD is not needed, as per Corollary 1. On the other hand, for linear layers, the KV cache is not shared by the batch and therefore increasing the batch size cannot ensure \(I^{X}_{\mathrm{attn}} \geq I^\star\) for \(X \in \{\mathrm{AR}, \mathrm{TD}\}\). However, we will show that the canvas size plays the role of the batch size.

Before diving into the throughput gain in the attention layer case, we will first investigate what is the equivalent of the MoE sparsity in the attention case. It turns out that sparse attention layers (like DeepSeek Sparse Attention) are the MoE sparsity equivalent. We start by recalling how DSA works.

Remark 1How DSA works

For a query at position \(i\), with hidden state \(x_i\) and \(C\) cached tokens \(j\), a DSA layer does three things.

  1. Scan. A small indexer with \(n_I\) heads of dimension \(h_I\) (\(n_{I,\ell}\) and \(h_{I,\ell}\) of Definition 1, for the layer at hand), in FP8, scores every cached token:
    \[ z_{i,j} = \sum_{a=1}^{n_I} \gamma_{i,a}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(q^I_{i,a}\cdot k^I_j\bigr),\qquad q^I_{i,a} = W^I_{q,a}\,x_i,\quad k^I_j = W^I_k\,x_j, \]
    \[ \begin{gathered} z_{i,j} = \sum_{a=1}^{n_I} \gamma_{i,a}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(q^I_{i,a}\cdot k^I_j\bigr),\\[4pt] q^I_{i,a} = W^I_{q,a}\,x_i,\qquad k^I_j = W^I_k\,x_j, \end{gathered} \]
    with query-dependent weights \(\gamma_{i,a}\).3 TThe scan's intensity for one query is \(I_{\mathrm{idx}} = \alpha_I/\nu_I = 2n_I/s_I\).4
  2. Select. \(\mathcal M_i = \) the \(m\) cached tokens with the largest \(z_{i,j}\) (\(m = 2{,}048\) in DeepSeek-V3.2).5
  3. Attend. Ordinary attention over the selected set, \(u_i = \mathrm{Attn}\bigl(q_i, \{k_j, v_j : j \in \mathcal M_i\}\bigr)\): \(\alpha_S = 4n_q\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal S}h_\ell\) FLOPs and \(\nu_S\) KV bytes per selected token, summed over the sparse layers: the same constants as a dense layer, over \(m\) tokens instead of \(C\).

The selection procedure is what hurts TD over AR, since for a canvas \(b\) we will request more cached tokens.

Sparse attention penalties

Dense and windowed layers behave alike: the canvas reads the cache once, and every query uses it. In a sparse layer, the scan behaves the same way. On the other hand, the selected tokens do not. Each query attends to its own \(m\) tokens, hence the canvas reads the union of \(b\) selections, which lies between \(m\) and the whole cache, while the FLOPs stay at \(m\) per query.

Proposition 6Attention layer arithmetic intensity

Under (A2) and (A3), let \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C) = \class{mj-td}{Q^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C)/\class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C)\) be the attention penalty (Definition 4). Then

\[ \class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \frac{b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}}{\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C)},\qquad \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C) = 1 + \frac{\nu_S\,[\,U_{C,m}(b) - \min(C, m)\,]}{\class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C)},\qquad 1 \le \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C) \le b. \]
\[ \begin{gathered} \class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \frac{b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}}{\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C)},\qquad 1 \le \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C) \le b,\\[4pt] \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C) = 1 + \frac{\nu_S\,[\,U_{C,m}(b) - \min(C, m)\,]}{\class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C)}. \end{gathered} \]
Proof

The identity is Definitions 2 and 4 on the attention part: \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} - \class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \nu_S[U_{C,m}(b) - \min(C,m)]\). \(U_{C,m}\) is increasing and concave with \(U_{C,m}(0) = 0\) and \(U_{C,m}(1) = \min(C, m)\), so \(\min(C,m) \le U_{C,m}(b) \le b\min(C,m)\), and \(\nu_S \min(C,m) \le \class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 7The attention penalty at the extremes

Under (A2) and (A3):

  1. Short context (\(C \le m\)): \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = 1\).
  2. Longer context (\(C \ge m\)), for a model whose attention is a sparse layer:
    \[ 1 - e^{-bm/C} \le \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C)\,\frac{\nu_I C + \nu_S m}{(\nu_I + \nu_S)\,C} \le 1, \]
    so for \(C \ll bm\) the canvas's selections cover the cache.
  3. Long context: if \(\nu_I \gt 0\), \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \to 1\) as \(C \to \infty\).
  4. Dense or windowed attention: \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \equiv 1\).
Proof

(1) For \(C \le m\), \(U_{C,m}(b) = C = \min(C,m)\). (2) For \(C \ge m\), \(0 \le (1-m/C)^b \le e^{-bm/C}\), so \(C(1 - e^{-bm/C}) \le U_{C,m}(b) \le C\) and \((\nu_I + \nu_S)C(1 - e^{-bm/C}) \le \class{mj-td}{Q^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} \le (\nu_I + \nu_S)C\), while \(\class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \nu_I C + \nu_S m\). (3) As \(C \to \infty\), \(C\,[1-(1-m/C)^b] \to bm\), a constant, while \(\nu_I C\) grows. (4) A dense or windowed layer reads the same cached bytes for one query and for \(b\) (Definition 2). \(\blacksquare\)

When are we in the attention layer regime?

When does the attention layer regime is all that matters? The following proposition (Proposition 8) answers to that question. While Proposition 8 can be seen as a rephrasing of Proposition 5, we still state it to highlight the impact of the large context \(C\).

Proposition 8When the attention layers dominate

Under (A2) and (A3), with the windows full (\(C \ge w\)) and dense attention, let, for \(X \in \{\mathrm{AR}, \mathrm{TD}\}\) and \(x \in (0,1)\),

\[ C^X_{\mathrm{attn}}(B, x) = \frac{1}{\nu_G}\Bigl(\frac{x}{1-x}\cdot\frac{s_wW^X(B)}{B} - \nu_W\,w\Bigr) = C^X_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, 1-x). \]
\[ \begin{gathered} C^X_{\mathrm{attn}}(B, x) = C^X_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, 1-x)\\[2pt] = \frac{1}{\nu_G}\Bigl(\frac{x}{1-x}\,\frac{s_wW^X(B)}{B} - \nu_W w\Bigr). \end{gathered} \]

Then \(\theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}} \ge x\) exactly when \(C \ge C^X_{\mathrm{attn}}(B, x)\). Moreover, \(C^X_{\mathrm{attn}}(\cdot, x)\) is non-increasing in \(B\), \(\class{mj-td}{C^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} \ge \class{mj-ar}{C^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\), and \(\max(C^X_{\mathrm{attn}}(B, x), 0) \to 0\) as \(B \to \infty\).

Proof

\(\theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}} = 1 - \theta^X_{\mathrm{lin}}\), so this is Proposition 5 with the share \(1-x\). \(\blacksquare\)

The gain when attention layers dominate

When attention carries most of the bytes and FLOPs of both passes, the batch drops out of Corollary 1: a cache belongs to one sequence, so both of its terms depend only on the context (Corollary 3).

Corollary 3The gain when attention dominates

Under the assumptions of Proposition 1, assume that attention carries most of the bytes and most of the FLOPs of both passes, i.e., \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx \class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\), \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}} \approx \class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\) and \(\Pi \approx \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\), and that \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C) \lt I^\star\). Then

\[ \Gamma = \min\Bigl(\frac{\mathrm{TPF}}{\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C)},\ \frac{I^\star}{\omega\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C)}\Bigr), \]

which does not depend on the batch. Moreover, for a model whose attention is of one kind, with the windows full and \(C \ge m\):

  1. Dense or sliding-window layers: \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = 1\), and \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}\) if \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \lt I^\star\), and \(I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{attn}})\) otherwise, with \(I_{\mathrm{win}}\) in place of \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) for windows.
  2. Sparse layers: \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = \bigl(\nu_IC + \nu_S\,U_{C,m}(b)\bigr)/\bigl(\nu_IC + \nu_Sm\bigr)\). As \(C \to \infty\), \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \to 1\) and \(\Gamma \to I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{idx}})\) whenever \(I_{\mathrm{idx}} \lt I^\star \lt b\,I_{\mathrm{idx}}\).
Proof

The attention terms of Definition 2 are per sequence, so neither the two intensities nor \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) depend on \(B\), and the formula is Corollary 1(1) with these intensities. (1) On a global layer, Definition 2 gives \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \nu_GC\), so \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = 1\), \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \alpha_G/\nu_G = I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\). Then \(\mathrm{TPF} = b/\omega \le I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{attn}})\) exactly when \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \le I^\star\). Windows are the same with \(\alpha_Ww\) and \(\nu_Ww\). (2) For \(C \ge m\), Definition 2 gives \(\class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \nu_IC + \nu_Sm\) and \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \nu_IC + \nu_S\,U_{C,m}(b)\), hence \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\). Since \(U_{C,m}(b) \le bm\) stays bounded while \(\nu_IC\) grows, \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \to 1\), \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} \to \alpha_I/\nu_I = I_{\mathrm{idx}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} \to b\,I_{\mathrm{idx}}\). When \(I_{\mathrm{idx}} \lt I^\star \lt b\,I_{\mathrm{idx}}\), AR is memory-bound and the second term of the minimum is the smaller. \(\blacksquare\)

Corollary 3 asks attention to carry most of the FLOPs as well as most of the bytes. By Proposition 2, \(I^X = \theta^X_{\mathrm{lin}} I^X_{\mathrm{lin}} + \theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}} I^X_{\mathrm{attn}}\): the byte shares are only the weights of the average, and \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}\) grows with the batch while \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\) stays at \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 8\). Hence \(I^X \approx I^X_{\mathrm{attn}}\) needs \(\theta^X_{\mathrm{lin}} I^X_{\mathrm{lin}} \ll \theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}} I^X_{\mathrm{attn}}\), i.e., attention must also carry most of the FLOPs. This matters only when TD is compute-bound, where the gain \(I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}})\) depends on \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}}\); when TD is memory-bound, \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi\) and the bytes suffice. With dense attention and full windows, attention carries at least a share \(x\) of a position's FLOPs, for AR and TD alike, exactly when

\[ C \ge C_F\Bigl(\frac{x}{1-x} - \sigma\Bigr),\qquad C_F = \frac{2N_{\mathrm{act}}}{\alpha_G},\qquad \sigma = \frac{\alpha_Ww}{2N_{\mathrm{act}}}. \]

For DiffusionGemma, \(C_F = 37.6\)k and \(\sigma = 6.8\%\), so attention carries half of the FLOPs from 35k, while at batch 64 it carries half of an AR step's bytes from 27k (Figure 12). For instance, at batch 96 and context 16k, attention carries 52% of an AR step's bytes but 33% of its FLOPs, and Proposition 1 gives 1.56 where Corollary 3 gives 2.41.

Optimal batch size

Finally, in this last paragraph, specifically in Proposition 9, we show that the minimal batch size at which the model becomes compute bound can have two extremely different behaviors for TD and AR at long context. In particular, it becomes clear that TD is beneficial for any serving setting at large context.

Proposition 9The batch needed to saturate compute

Under (A2) and (A3), with dense attention and the windows full (\(C \ge w\)), let \(\class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}(C)\) and \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}(C)\) be the smallest batches at which an AR step and a TD pass are compute-bound at context \(C\), or \(\infty\) if there is none. Then:

  1. AR: if \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} \lt I^\star\), \(\class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}(C)\) is non-decreasing in \(C\), and infinite beyond some finite context.
  2. TD: if \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \gt I^\star\), \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}(C)\) is non-increasing in \(C\), and equal to 1 beyond some finite context.
Proof

Write \(n = 1\) for AR and \(n = b\) for TD, \(W_n\) and \(I_n\) for the weights and the intensity of the pass, and \(D_n(C) = n\,\class{mj-ar}{F^{\mathrm{AR}}}(C) - I^\star \class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C)\), the FLOPs per sequence left for the weights once its cache reads are compute-bound. Multiplying out \(I_n \ge I^\star\) gives \(B\,D_n(C) \ge I^\star s_wW_n(B)\). The right side is positive, so no batch works when \(D_n \le 0\). For \(C \ge w\), \(D_n\) is affine in \(C\) with slope \((nI_{\mathrm{attn}} - I^\star)\,\nu_G\). A smaller \(D_n\) can only remove batches from the feasible set, so a negative slope makes the smallest compute-bound batch non-decreasing and eventually infinite; a positive slope makes the set grow until batch 1 works, once \(D_n \ge I^\star s_wW_n(1)\). The same argument shows that a TD pass with \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \lt I^\star\) behaves like AR. Nothing needs a closed form for \(W_n(B)\), so the argument covers MoE models; the inequality holds at every context, and only the slope of \(D_n\) needs \(C \ge w\). \(\blacksquare\)
