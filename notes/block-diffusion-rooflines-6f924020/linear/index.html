Weights and cache

Part 2 of Block Diffusion Rooflines: the expert and linear penalties and the gain when the linear layers dominate; dense, windowed and sparse attention, the selection penalty as the twin of the expert penalty, and the gain when attention dominates; the batch that saturates compute at any context.

Part 2 of Block Diffusion RooflinesPrevious: 1 · Setup and the gainNext: 3 · Maps and calculator

A pass reads two things: the weights, which the batch shares, and the KV cache, which each sequence brings. In this chapter, we treat each in turn. At short context the weights dominate, and the batch \(B\) sets both intensities: we compute the linear penalty \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\), the ratio of the weight bytes a TD pass reads to those an AR step reads (7.9 at batch 1 for DiffusionGemma, falling to 1 as the batch grows), state up to which context the linear layers dominate, and derive the gain there, whose best batch is the one at which a TD pass just becomes compute-bound. At long context the cache dominates: a larger batch no longer raises either intensity, but a canvas still does, since its \(b\) queries share one read of the cache. We give the costs of dense, sliding-window and sparse attention, show that the selection penalty of a sparse layer is the expert penalty \(\Pi_E\) at batch 1 with the cached tokens in the role of the experts, and derive the gain when attention dominates. Finally, we follow, across every context, the batch at which each decoder saturates compute: the canvas does for the KV cache what the batch does for the weights.

We start with short contexts, where the attention terms are negligible. Hence, the bytes of a pass are those of the weights it reads: \(s_w\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)\) for an AR step and \(s_w\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}(B)\) for a TD pass. The TD pass computes \(b\) times as many positions while reading \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\) times the weight bytes, so its linear intensity is \(b/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\) times AR's (Proposition 3 below).

The expert and linear penalties

Proposition 3The expert penalty and the linear penalty

Under (A2) and (A3), \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{lin}}} = b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\), with \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1 = \theta_E\,(\Pi_E - 1)\), where \(\theta_E = L\,U_{E,k}(B)\,W^e/\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)\) is the expert share of the weights an AR step reads and \(\Pi_E = U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B)\). Moreover:

  1. \(1 \le \Pi_E \le \min(b,\ E/k)\), and \(\Pi_E\) is non-increasing in \(B\), with \(\Pi_E - 1 \le (E/k)\,e^{-kB/E}\), so \(\Pi_E \to 1\) as \(B \to \infty\).
  2. As \(b \to \infty\), a canvas touches every expert: \(\Pi_E \to E/U_{E,k}(B)\), which at batch 1 is \(E/k\), and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) \to W_{\mathrm{tot}}/N_{\mathrm{act}}\), total over active parameters, with \(W_{\mathrm{tot}} = W_{\mathrm{ne}} + L\,E\,W^e\).
  3. For a dense model (\(k = E\)), \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} \equiv 1\).
  4. \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} \le b\), and with Propositions 2 and 5, \(\Pi \le b\).
Proof

The first identity is Definition 4 restricted to the linear part; the split of \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1\) is Proposition 2's argument applied to the dense and expert parts of the weights, the dense part having penalty 1. (1) \(U_{E,k}\) is increasing and concave with \(U_{E,k}(0) = 0\) and \(U_{E,k}(N) \le \min(kN, E)\), so \(U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B) \le b\) by concavity and \(\le E/U_{E,k}(B) \le E/k\). With \(q = (1-k/E)^B\), \(\Pi_E = (1-q^b)/(1-q) = 1 + q + \dots + q^{b-1}\), which is increasing in \(q\), and \(q\) decreases with \(B\); and \(U_{E,k}(Bb) - U_{E,k}(B) \le E(1-k/E)^B \le Ee^{-kB/E}\) with \(U_{E,k}(B) \ge k\). (2) \(E \ge U_{E,k}(Bb) \ge E(1 - e^{-kb/E}) \to E\) as \(b \to \infty\), and \(U_{E,k}(1) = k\); \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(1) = W_{\mathrm{ne}} + LkW^e = N_{\mathrm{act}}\), while \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}(1) \to W_{\mathrm{ne}} + LEW^e = W_{\mathrm{tot}}\). (3) \(U_{E,k}(N) = E\) for all \(N \ge 1\). (4) \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1 = \theta_E(\Pi_E - 1) \le b - 1\) by (1) and \(\theta_E \le 1\). By Proposition 2, \(\Pi\) is a weighted mean of \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\), and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \le b\): by Proposition 5(1) for \(C \ge m\), and since its identity gives \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = 1\) for \(C \le m\). \(\blacksquare\)

When the linear layers dominate

Proposition 4When the linear layers dominate

Under (A2) and (A3), with the windows full (\(C \ge w\)) and dense attention, an AR step spends at least a share \(x\) of its bytes on weights exactly when

\[ C \;\le\; C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, x) = \frac{1}{\nu_G}\Bigl(\frac{1-x}{x}\cdot\frac{s_w\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)}{B} - \nu_W\,w\Bigr). \]
\[ \begin{gathered} C \le C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, x)\\[2pt] = \frac{1}{\nu_G}\Bigl(\frac{1-x}{x}\,\frac{s_w\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)}{B} - \nu_W w\Bigr). \end{gathered} \]

Moreover:

  1. \(C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B,x)\) is non-increasing in \(B\): the weights are shared across the batch, the cache is not. Once the batch touches every expert (\(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B) \to W_{\mathrm{tot}}\), as \(B \to \infty\) under (A3)), and for dense models at every batch, it is affine in \(1/B\), a multiple of \(1/B\) minus the constant window term; below that it falls more slowly, since \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)\) still grows with \(B\).
  2. For a TD pass, replace \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}\) by \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}\): its threshold is \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\) times further out, up to the window term.
  3. With sparse layers, replace \(\nu_G C\) by \(\nu_S\min(C,m) + \nu_I C\) for an AR step and by \(\nu_S U_{C,m}(b) + \nu_I C\) for a TD pass.
Proof

\(\class{mj-ar}{\theta^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}} \ge x \iff s_w\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}/B \ge \tfrac{x}{1-x}\,(\nu_GC + \nu_Ww)\); solve for \(C\). (1) \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)/B\) is non-increasing because \(U_{E,k}\) is concave with \(U_{E,k}(0) = 0\). When \(U_{E,k}(B) = E\) (at every \(B\) for a dense model, and as \(B \to \infty\) for an MoE under (A3)), \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B) = W_{\mathrm{tot}}\) is constant, so \(C_{\mathrm{lin}}\) is affine in \(1/B\). (2) and (3) substitute the TD and sparse byte counts of Definition 2. \(\blacksquare\)

DiffusionGemma's cache is small: 2 KV heads in 5 global layers, i.e., \(\nu_G = 20\) KiB per token, plus a fixed 210 MB per sequence for the windows. Therefore, its linear layers dominate for a long way. On the other hand, a dense model with grouped-query attention (GQA) such as Llama 3.1 8B has 128 KiB per token and 14 GB of weights without the vocabulary. It reaches equal weight and cache bytes at \(BC \approx 107\)k.

The gain when the linear layers dominate

When the weights are most of the bytes of both passes, the two terms of Corollary 1 depend only on the batch, and their crossing gives the best batch in closed form (Corollary 2).

Corollary 2The gain when the linear layers dominate

Under the assumptions of Proposition 1, assume that the linear layers dominate both passes, i.e., \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx \class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}\) and \(\Pi \approx \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\), and that \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) is non-increasing in the batch2. Define the batch at which a TD pass reaches the ridge, the batch at which an AR step does, and the parity batch,

\[ \class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} = \frac{I^\star s_w\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}}{2b\,N_{\mathrm{act}}},\qquad \class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star} = \frac{I^\star s_wW_{\mathrm{tot}}}{2N_{\mathrm{act}}},\qquad B_\times = \frac{\class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}}{\omega}, \]
\[ \begin{gathered} \class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} = \frac{I^\star s_w\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}}{2b\,N_{\mathrm{act}}},\qquad \class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star} = \frac{I^\star s_wW_{\mathrm{tot}}}{2N_{\mathrm{act}}},\\[4pt] B_\times = \frac{\class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}}{\omega}, \end{gathered} \]

with \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}\) taken at \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\), and \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}} = W_{\mathrm{tot}}\) in the last two, i.e., an AR step that touches every expert. Then:

  1. Both memory-bound, \(B \lt \class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\): \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\), which rises with the batch.
  2. TD compute-bound, \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} \le B \lt \class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}\): \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(B))\), which falls with the batch, as \(1/B\) once an AR step touches every expert, and equals 1 at \(B_\times\) if \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} \le B_\times\), i.e., if the best gain is at least 1.
  3. Best batch: if \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} \ge 1\) and a batch of that size fits, \(\Gamma\) is maximal at \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\), where it equals \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star})\); otherwise it is maximal at \(B = 1\), or at the largest batch that fits.
Proof

By Definition 2 and Proposition 3, \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(B) = 2BN_{\mathrm{act}}/(s_w\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B))\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(B) = 2BbN_{\mathrm{act}}/(s_w\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}(B))\). Setting \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{lin}}} = I^\star\) gives \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\); setting \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}} = I^\star\) with \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}} = W_{\mathrm{tot}}\) gives \(\class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}\); and \(I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}) = 1\) gives \(B_\times = \class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}/\omega\). Below \(\class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}\) AR is memory-bound (up to this approximation), so Corollary 1(1) applies, and its first term is the smaller exactly when \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{lin}}} \lt I^\star\), i.e., \(B \lt \class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\); this gives (1) and (2). The first term rises because \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) is non-increasing; the second falls because \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)/B\) is non-increasing (proof of Proposition 4), and is proportional to \(1/B\) when \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}} = W_{\mathrm{tot}}\). Since \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}(B)/B\) is decreasing, \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}\) is increasing in \(B\), so \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\) is unique. On the falling branch (2), \(\Gamma = 1\) exactly at \(B_\times\), which lies on that branch when \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} \le B_\times\); otherwise \(\Gamma\) at \(B_\times\) is \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B_\times) \lt 1\). (3) is Corollary 1(2): case (c) when \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} \ge 1\) fits, case (a) when \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} \lt 1\), where case (1) above is empty, and case (b) when no batch that fits reaches the ridge. The thresholds take \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}} = W_{\mathrm{tot}}\); since \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B) \le W_{\mathrm{tot}}\), the batch at which AR actually reaches the ridge is at most \(\class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}\), and equal to it within rounding for DiffusionGemma, where \(U_{E,k}(1{,}640)\) differs from \(E\) by a relative amount \((15/16)^{1640}\). \(\blacksquare\)

We now turn to long contexts, where the attention terms dominate: a pass costs what it reads of each sequence's KV cache. Three kinds of attention layer appear in current models: dense, sliding-window, and sparse in the style of DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA; DeepSeek-AI, 2025). We summarise how DSA works in Remark 1 below. Table 1 gives, per sequence, what one query costs on each kind of layer and what an AR step and a TD pass of \(b\) queries read. As a sparse example we use the sparse variant of DiffusionGemma: its five global layers become DSA layers with \(m = 2{,}048\) and an indexer of \(n_I = 8\) heads of dimension \(h_I = 128\) in FP8. Then \(\nu_S = \nu_G = 20\) KiB and \(\nu_I = 640\) bytes per token, so \(\nu_I = \nu_S/32\).

Dense, windowed and sparse attention

Remark 1How DSA works

For a query at position \(i\), with hidden state \(x_i\) and \(C\) cached tokens \(j\), a DSA layer does three things.

  1. Scan. A small indexer with \(n_I\) heads of dimension \(h_I\) (\(n_{I,\ell}\) and \(h_{I,\ell}\) of Definition 1, for the layer at hand), in FP8, scores every cached token:
    \[ z_{i,j} = \sum_{a=1}^{n_I} \gamma_{i,a}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(q^I_{i,a}\cdot k^I_j\bigr),\qquad q^I_{i,a} = W^I_{q,a}\,x_i,\quad k^I_j = W^I_k\,x_j, \]
    \[ \begin{gathered} z_{i,j} = \sum_{a=1}^{n_I} \gamma_{i,a}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(q^I_{i,a}\cdot k^I_j\bigr),\\[4pt] q^I_{i,a} = W^I_{q,a}\,x_i,\qquad k^I_j = W^I_k\,x_j, \end{gathered} \]
    with query-dependent weights \(\gamma_{i,a}\).3 The \(n_I\) heads share one index key per token, so the cache of one sparse layer holds \(k^I_j\) at \(h_Is_I\) bytes per token, with \(s_I\) bytes per element (1 in FP8), while its scan costs \(2n_Ih_I\) FLOPs per query and per cached token; \(\nu_I\) and \(\alpha_I\) of Definition 1 sum these over the sparse layers. Hence, when all sparse layers have the same indexer, the scan's intensity for one query is \(I_{\mathrm{idx}} = \alpha_I/\nu_I = 2n_I/s_I\): 16 FLOP/byte for the 8 FP8 heads used here, and about 124 for DeepSeek-V3.2's 64.4
  2. Select. \(\mathcal M_i = \) the \(m\) cached tokens with the largest \(z_{i,j}\) (\(m = 2{,}048\) in DeepSeek-V3.2).5
  3. Attend. Ordinary attention over the selected set, \(u_i = \mathrm{Attn}\bigl(q_i, \{k_j, v_j : j \in \mathcal M_i\}\bigr)\): \(\alpha_S = 4n_q\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal S}h_\ell\) FLOPs and \(\nu_S\) KV bytes per selected token, summed over the sparse layers: the same constants as a dense layer, over \(m\) tokens instead of \(C\).

For the AR decoder the scan is the only attention cost that still grows with \(C\); for a canvas, the scan's cache is read once for all \(b\) queries, while each query's selected tokens are its own.

The penalty of a sparse layer

Dense and windowed layers behave alike: the canvas reads the cache once, and every query uses it. In a sparse layer, the scan behaves the same way. On the other hand, the selected tokens do not. Each query attends to its own \(m\) tokens, hence the canvas reads the union of \(b\) selections, which lies between \(m\) and the whole cache, while the FLOPs stay at \(m\) per query.

Proposition 5The penalty of a sparse layer

Under (A2) and (A3), \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}/\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) with

\[ \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C) - 1 = \frac{\nu_S\,[\,U_{C,m}(b) - \min(C, m)\,]}{\class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C)}. \]
  1. For \(C \ge m\), \(1 \le \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \le 1 + \dfrac{\nu_S\,[\min(bm, C) - m]}{\nu_I C + \nu_S m} \le b\): the penalty comes only from the selected tokens, and is bounded by what the canvas can read of them beyond one query.
  2. For a model whose attention is a sparse layer, the context falls into three stages:
    • \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = 1\) for \(C \le m\);
    • for \(C \ge m\), \(1 - e^{-bm/C} \le \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C)\,\dfrac{\nu_I C + \nu_S m}{(\nu_I + \nu_S)\,C} \le 1\), so that for \(C \ll bm\) the canvas's selections cover the cache;
    • if \(\nu_I \gt 0\), \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \to 1\) as \(C \to \infty\), when the union stops growing at \(bm\) and the scan's bytes dominate.
  3. With a free scan (\(\alpha_I = \nu_I = 0\)) and no other attention layers, \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = \Pi_{\mathrm{sel}}\), which for \(C \ge m\) is \(U_{C,m}(b)/m\), between 1 and \(\min(b, C/m)\), and non-decreasing in \(b\) and \(C\): the exact twin of the expert penalty \(\Pi_E = U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B)\) at batch 1, with the \(C\) cached tokens in the role of the \(E\) experts and \(m\) in the role of \(k\), since a cache belongs to one sequence.
Proof

The identity is Definitions 2 and 4 on the attention part: \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} - \class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \nu_S[U_{C,m}(b) - \min(C,m)]\), and \(\min(C,m) = m\) for \(C \ge m\). (1) \(m \le U_{C,m}(b) \le \min(bm, C)\), and \(\class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} \ge \nu_I C + \nu_S m\). (2) For \(C \le m\), \(U_{C,m}(b) = C = \min(C,m)\). For \(C \ge m\), \(0 \le (1-m/C)^b \le e^{-bm/C}\), so \(C(1 - e^{-bm/C}) \le U_{C,m}(b) \le C\) and \((\nu_I + \nu_S)C(1 - e^{-bm/C}) \le \class{mj-td}{Q^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} \le (\nu_I + \nu_S)C\), while \(\class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \nu_I C + \nu_S m\). As \(C \to \infty\), \(C\,[1-(1-m/C)^b] \to bm\), a constant, while \(\nu_I C\) grows. (3) \(U_{C,m}\) is increasing and concave in its argument with \(U_{C,m}(0) = 0\) and \(U_{C,m}(1) = m\), so \(U_{C,m}(b)/m \le b\), and \(U_{C,m}(b) \le C\). It is non-decreasing in \(C\), since \(C[1 - (1-m/C)^b] = m\,\varphi(m/C)\) with \(\varphi(x) = [1-(1-x)^b]/x\) non-increasing on \((0, 1]\), the chord slope of a concave function vanishing at 0. \(\blacksquare\)

When attention dominates

Proposition 6When attention dominates

Under (A2) and (A3), with the windows full (\(C \ge w\)):

  1. With dense attention, attention carries at least a share \(x\) of an AR step's bytes exactly when \(C \ge C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, 1-x)\), the threshold of Proposition 4.
  2. With dense attention, attention carries at least a share \(x\) of a position's FLOPs exactly when \(C \ge C_F\bigl(\tfrac{x}{1-x} - \sigma\bigr)\), with \(C_F = 2N_{\mathrm{act}}/\alpha_G\) and \(\sigma = \alpha_Ww/(2N_{\mathrm{act}})\); the same for AR and TD.
  3. With dense attention, \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}}\) are monotone in \(C\) on \(C \ge w\), and tend to \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\). With sparse attention in place of the global layers, \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}}\) is monotone on \(C \ge m\) and tends to \(I_{\mathrm{idx}}\), and \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}} \to b\,I_{\mathrm{idx}}\) as \(C \to \infty\).
Proof

(1) is Proposition 4 with the complementary share. (2) With dense attention \(\class{mj-ar}{F^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}/\class{mj-ar}{F^{\mathrm{AR}}} \ge x \iff (1-x)(\alpha_GC + \alpha_Ww) \ge 2xN_{\mathrm{act}}\). (3) For \(C \ge w\), and \(C \ge m\) with sparse layers, \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}}\), and \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}}\) with dense attention, are ratios of affine functions of \(C\) with positive denominators, hence monotone, with the stated limits. For \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}}\) with sparse attention, \(U_{C,m}(b) \to bm\), so the \(\nu_IC\) and \(\alpha_IC\) terms dominate as \(C \to \infty\). \(\blacksquare\)

Note that \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}}\) need not be monotone with sparse attention. For \(m \lt C \lesssim \nu_S\,bm/\nu_I\), the penalty \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) rises and falls (Figure 11), and \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}/\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) moves the opposite way.

The context \(C_F\) is the one at which global attention alone matches the linear FLOPs.6 For DiffusionGemma, \(C_F = 37.6\)k and \(\sigma = 6.8\%\). Note that attention FLOPs and attention bytes reach a given share at very different contexts:

  • FLOPs: attention carries a tenth of the FLOPs at 1.6k tokens, and half at 35k.
  • Bytes: attention carries half of an AR step's bytes at 291k tokens for batch 1, and at 27k for batch 64.

The gain when attention dominates

When attention carries most of the bytes and FLOPs of both passes, the batch drops out of Corollary 1: a cache belongs to one sequence, so both of its terms depend only on the context (Corollary 3).

Corollary 3The gain when attention dominates

Under the assumptions of Proposition 1, assume that attention carries most of the bytes and most of the FLOPs of both passes, i.e., \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx \class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\), \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}} \approx \class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\) and \(\Pi \approx \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\), and that \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C) \lt I^\star\). Then

\[ \Gamma = \min\Bigl(\frac{\mathrm{TPF}}{\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C)},\ \frac{I^\star}{\omega\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C)}\Bigr), \]

which does not depend on the batch. Moreover, for a model whose attention is of one kind, with the windows full and \(C \ge m\):

  1. Dense or sliding-window layers: \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = 1\), and \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}\) if \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \lt I^\star\), and \(I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{attn}})\) otherwise, with \(I_{\mathrm{win}}\) in place of \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) for windows.
  2. Sparse layers: \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = \bigl(\nu_IC + \nu_S\,U_{C,m}(b)\bigr)/\bigl(\nu_IC + \nu_Sm\bigr)\). As \(C \to \infty\), \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \to 1\) and \(\Gamma \to I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{idx}})\) whenever \(I_{\mathrm{idx}} \lt I^\star \lt b\,I_{\mathrm{idx}}\).
Proof

The attention terms of Definition 2 are per sequence, so neither the two intensities nor \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) depend on \(B\), and the formula is Corollary 1(1) with these intensities. (1) On a global layer, Definition 2 gives \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \nu_GC\), so \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = 1\), \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \alpha_G/\nu_G = I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\). Then \(\mathrm{TPF} = b/\omega \le I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{attn}})\) exactly when \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \le I^\star\). Windows are the same with \(\alpha_Ww\) and \(\nu_Ww\). (2) For \(C \ge m\), Definition 2 gives \(\class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \nu_IC + \nu_Sm\) and \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \nu_IC + \nu_S\,U_{C,m}(b)\), hence \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\). Since \(U_{C,m}(b) \le bm\) stays bounded while \(\nu_IC\) grows, \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \to 1\), \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} \to \alpha_I/\nu_I = I_{\mathrm{idx}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}_{\mathrm{attn}}} \to b\,I_{\mathrm{idx}}\). When \(I_{\mathrm{idx}} \lt I^\star \lt b\,I_{\mathrm{idx}}\), AR is memory-bound and the second term of the minimum is the smaller. \(\blacksquare\)

When TD is compute-bound, both conditions of Corollary 3, on the bytes and on the FLOPs, are needed. Indeed, at large batch, attention dominates the bytes long before it dominates the FLOPs, which takes \(C \ge C_F(1-\sigma) = 35\)k. For instance, at batch 96 and context 16k, a point that fits in memory, attention carries 52% of an AR step's bytes but 33% of its FLOPs. There, Proposition 1 gives 1.56 where this formula gives 2.41.

We now look at the sparse row more closely. Its limit as \(C \to \infty\) is Corollary 3(2). Between \(m\) and \(bm\), on the other hand, the canvas reads nearly the whole cache on the selected tokens. This is the only attention term on which it reads more than an AR step. Therefore, a TD pass can be memory-bound there even though it is compute-bound on the scan.

For the sparse variant of DiffusionGemma, the index cache is small: \(\nu_I = \nu_S/32\). Hence, the selected tokens carry most of a canvas's attention bytes up to \(C \approx \nu_S\,bm/\nu_I \approx 17\)M, where the two terms are equal. As a consequence, the penalty is large between about 32k and 1M: 11 at 32k, 22 at 128k, 13 at 1M. In the limit, an AR step scans at \(I_{\mathrm{idx}} = 16\), twice the dense \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\). Therefore, the limit gain 0.86 is half the dense 1.72, and below 1.

In particular, under independent selections, sparse attention removes TD's long-context advantage once attention dominates both decoders. With DeepSeek-V3.2's indexer instead (64 heads, \(I_{\mathrm{idx}} \approx 124\)), the limit is \(206/(15 \times 124) = 0.11\). Finally, overlapping selections lower the penalty, but not the limit.7

The batch needed to saturate compute

The sections above treat the two extremes, short and long context. The last proposition of this chapter follows, across every context, the batch at which each decoder saturates compute.

Proposition 7The batch needed to saturate compute

Let \(B^\star_n(C)\) be the smallest batch at which a pass with \(n\) queries per sequence (\(n = 1\) for AR, \(n = b\) for TD) is compute-bound, or \(\infty\) if there is none, with dense attention and the windows full (\(C \ge w\)). Write \(I_n\) and \(W_n\) for the intensity and the weights of that pass (\(I_1 = \class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}}\), \(W_1 = \class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}\), \(I_b = \class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}}\), \(W_b = \class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}\)), and \(D_n(C) = n\,\class{mj-ar}{F^{\mathrm{AR}}}(C) - I^\star \class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C)\), the FLOPs per sequence left for the weights once its cache reads are compute-bound.

  1. If \(n\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \lt I^\star\), then \(B^\star_n(C)\) is non-decreasing in \(C\), and infinite beyond some finite context.
  2. If \(n\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \gt I^\star\), then \(B^\star_n(C)\) is non-increasing in \(C\), and equal to 1 beyond some finite context.

AR is in the first case whenever \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} \lt I^\star\), which holds for every preset model and accelerator of the calculator (\(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) is at most 16 there, against a lowest ridge point of 37, on the H20). TD is in the second case exactly when \(b \gt b_c = I^\star/I_{\mathrm{attn}}\), the critical canvas length.

Proof

Multiplying out \(I_n \ge I^\star\) gives \(B\,D_n(C) \ge I^\star s_wW_n(B)\): each sequence contributes \(n\class{mj-ar}{F^{\mathrm{AR}}}\) FLOPs, of which \(I^\star \class{mj-ar}{Q^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\) are needed to make its own cache reads compute-bound, and what is left, summed over the batch, has to cover the weights. The right side is positive, so no batch works when \(D_n \le 0\). For \(C \ge w\), \(D_n\) is affine in \(C\) with slope \((nI_{\mathrm{attn}} - I^\star)\,\nu_G\). A smaller \(D_n\) can only remove batches from the feasible set, so a negative slope makes \(B^\star_n\) non-decreasing and eventually infinite; a positive slope makes the set grow until batch 1 works, once \(D_n \ge I^\star s_wW_n(1)\). Nothing needs a closed form for \(W_n(B)\), so the argument covers MoE models. \(\blacksquare\)

The canvas does for the KV cache what batch does for the weights.

Indeed, a larger batch does not help with cache bytes, because every sequence brings its own cache. AR uses each cache byte for one query. Hence, at long context, its intensity tends to \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\), far below the ridge, whatever the batch. On the other hand, a canvas uses each cache byte for \(b\) queries. Once \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \gt I^\star\), a longer context makes compute easier to saturate, not harder.

When \(W_n\) does not depend on \(B\), solving the inequality gives \(B \ge B^\star_n(C) = I^\star s_wW_n/D_n(C)\) for \(D_n \gt 0\). At zero context, this is the \(\class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\) of Corollary 28; an integer batch rounds it up.
