Five rules turn these numbers into gains:

  • Halvings. \((1-x)^n \approx 2^{-1.5nx}\), accurate for \(x\) near \(1/16\); for small \(x\), use \(1.44\) in place of \(1.5\). For DiffusionGemma, \(1 - k/E = 15/16\), so the fraction of experts that \(N\) tokens leave untouched halves every 11 tokens, and \(U_{E,k}(N) = E\,[1 - (15/16)^N]\).
  • Shares. An intensity is the byte-weighted average of its parts (Proposition 2), and the expert share \(\theta_E\) is a ratio of bytes. Both can be computed per layer and in units of \(d\), since every weight matrix has \(d\) as a dimension.
  • Linear intensity. In BF16, \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(B) = BN_{\mathrm{act}}/\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)\), with \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B) = W_{\mathrm{ne}} + L\,U_{E,k}(B)\,W^e\).
  • TD's intensity. \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}} = b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}}/\Pi\) (Definition 4).
  • The gain. \(\Gamma = \min(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}}, I^\star)/\bigl(\omega\min(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}}, I^\star)\bigr)\) (Proposition 1): \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi\) when both decoders are memory-bound, and \(I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}})\) when only TD is compute-bound.

Exercise 1: reading the roofline

We place DiffusionGemma's AR step and TD pass on the roofline of the H200, as in Figure 6, with \(b = 256\), \(S = 14\) and \(r = 1\). On the slope of the roofline, below the ridge, a pass runs at \(I\beta\) FLOP/s, so heights are proportional to intensities; above the ridge, it runs at the peak. Figure 15 repeats Figure 6: set its batch and context to check each answer.

Figure 15 · interactive

TD wins when its point is more than \(\omega\) times higher than AR's.

The roofline of Figure 6: the gain is TD's height over AR's, divided by \(\omega\).

  1. Batch 1, 1k context. Place both points, and read the gain off the plot.

    Hint

    At 1k tokens the cache is small next to the weights. Then use \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}} = b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}}/\Pi\) and \(\Pi(1) \approx W_{\mathrm{tot}}/N_{\mathrm{act}}\).

    Solution

    A sequence's cache is 0.2 GB of window cache and 0.02 GB of global cache, against 6.2 GB of weights, so \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx I_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) = 1\). TD's intensity is \(b/\Pi \approx 256/8 = 32\) times AR's. Both points are below the ridge at 206, on the slope, so TD's point is 32 times higher than AR's: TD runs its FLOPs 32 times faster. A canvas costs \(\omega = 15\) passes, so \(\Gamma \approx 32/15 \approx 2.1\). In general, TD wins exactly when its point is more than \(\omega = 15\) times higher than AR's, a vertical gap of \(\log_{10} 15 \approx 1.2\) decades on the plot.

  2. The best batch. As the batch grows, at which batch does TD's point reach the ridge, and how large is the gap there?

    Hint

    From batch 2 on, a TD pass touches every expert. Write \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}}\) with \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}} = W_{\mathrm{tot}}\).

    Solution

    With \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}} = W_{\mathrm{tot}}\), \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}} = bBN_{\mathrm{act}}/W_{\mathrm{tot}} \approx 256B/8 = 32B\), which reaches 206 at \(B \approx 6\): this is \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\) of Corollary 2. At batch 6, AR leaves \(2^{-0.56} \approx 0.68\) of the experts untouched, touches about 41 per layer and reads \(1.7 + 30 \times 41 \times 6\text{M} \approx 9\)B parameters, so \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx 6 \times 3.1/9 \approx 2\). The gap is \(206/2 \approx 100\), and \(\Gamma \approx 100/15 \approx 7\), the largest gain at 1k: below this batch TD's point climbs faster than AR's, and above it TD's point stays at the peak.

  3. Parity. Past the ridge, how do the points move, and at which batch is the gap down to 15?

    Hint

    TD's height is fixed at the peak, so the gap is \(I^\star/\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}}\). Count the window cache in AR's bytes.

    Solution

    TD's point stays at the peak while AR's climbs the slope, and the gap \(I^\star/\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}}\) is 15 when \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} = 206/15 \approx 14\). Without a cache, \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx B/8\) once AR touches every expert, and parity would be at batch 110 (Corollary 2). At 1k tokens, a sequence also reads 0.2 GB of window cache at intensity 2 and 0.02 GB of global cache at intensity 8, so its step costs \(6.2 + 0.4 + 0.16 \approx 6.8\) GFLOPs for \(49/B + 0.22\) GB. Setting the ratio to 14 gives \(49/B \approx 0.27\), so parity is at about batch 180: the window cache, which has a lower intensity than the weights at this batch, holds AR down.

  4. Context at batch 1. Move the context from 1k to 1M. Why does the gain first rise and then fall?

    Hint

    Both decoders read the same cache. Compute \(\Pi\) and both intensities at 128k and at 1M tokens.

    Solution

    At 128k tokens, a sequence's global cache is 2.6 GB and AR's step reads \(6.2 + 2.8 = 9\) GB for \(6.2 + 2.6 \times 8 + 0.2 \times 2 \approx 27\) GFLOPs, so \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx 3\). TD reads the same cache next to its 49 GB of weights, so \(\Pi \approx 52/9 \approx 5.8\): the cache dilutes the penalty. TD's intensity, \(256 \times 3/5.8 \approx 130\), is still below the ridge, so the gap is \(b/\Pi \approx 44\) and \(\Gamma \approx 2.9\), up from 2.1. At 1M tokens the cache is 21 GB, \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx (6.2 + 21 \times 8)/27 \approx 6.4\) and \(\Pi \approx 70/27 \approx 2.6\), so TD's intensity, \(256 \times 6.4/2.6 \approx 640\), is past the ridge. The gap is then \(206/6.4 \approx 32\), and \(\Gamma \approx 2.1\) again. The gain rises while the cache lowers \(\Pi\) and TD stays below the ridge, and falls once TD reaches the peak and AR keeps climbing towards \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 8\).

Exercise 2: a smaller canvas

We consider DiffusionGemma on the H200 with \(S = 14\) and \(r = 1\), so that \(\omega = 15\), but we shrink the canvas from 256 to \(b = 32\).

Figure 16

  1. Batch 1, short context. Compute TPF and \(\Pi(1)\). Who wins?

    Hint

    Count the experts a 32-token canvas touches with the halving rule, then apply Proposition 3 with the expert share \(\theta_E(1)\).

    Solution

    \(\mathrm{TPF} = 32/15 \approx 2\). A canvas of 32 tokens leaves \((15/16)^{32} \approx 2^{-3} = 1/8\) of the experts untouched, so \(\Pi_E \approx 16 \times 7/8 = 14\). At batch 1 the active experts are \(LkW^e \approx 30 \times 8 \times 6\text{M} \approx 1.4\)B of the 3.1B parameters an AR step reads, so \(\theta_E(1) \approx 1/2\) and \(\Pi(1) \approx 1 + \tfrac12 \times 13 = 7.5\). AR's intensity is about 1 and TD's is \(b/\Pi \approx 4\) times that, so both are memory-bound and \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi \approx 2/7.5 \approx 0.3\): AR wins by a factor of 3 to 4. At \(b = 256\) the penalty is barely larger, \(\Pi \approx W_{\mathrm{tot}}/N_{\mathrm{act}} \approx 8\), but TPF is eight times larger. On the map: the blue bottom-left corner.

  2. Where does TD overtake AR? Find the batch at which \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi(B) = 1\).

    Hint

    From a few sequences on, a TD pass touches every expert. Write \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi(B) = 1\) as an equation for the number of experts AR touches, \(U_{E,k}(B)\).

    Solution

    From batch 2 on, a TD pass covers at least 64 tokens, which leave \(2^{-6}\) of the experts untouched, so \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}} \approx W_{\mathrm{tot}} = 24.5\)B. Parity needs \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B) = W_{\mathrm{tot}}/\mathrm{TPF} \approx 24.5/2.1 \approx 11.5\)B, that is, \(30 \times U_{E,k}(B) \times 6\text{M} \approx 11.5 - 1.7 \approx 10\)B and \(U_{E,k}(B) \approx 55\). The untouched fraction is then \(1 - 55/128 \approx 0.57 \approx 2^{-0.8}\), so \(1.5B/16 \approx 0.8\) and \(B \approx 9\). On the map: the lower dashed line, \(\Gamma = 1\).

  3. Where is TD's best batch, and how large is the gain there?

    Hint

    The gain is largest where a TD pass becomes compute-bound, at \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\) of Corollary 2. How does \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\) scale with \(b\)?

    Solution

    \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} = I^\star s_w\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}/(2bN_{\mathrm{act}}) \approx 206 \times (49\,\text{GB}/6.2\,\text{GB})/32 \approx 206 \times 8/32 \approx 51\), eight times the batch at \(b = 256\), since \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} \propto 1/b\). At batch 51, AR leaves \(2^{-1.5 \times 51/16} \approx 2^{-5} \approx 3\%\) of the experts untouched, so \(\Pi \approx 1\) and the peak is \(\Gamma \approx \mathrm{TPF} \approx 2.1\). On the map: the solid line labelled "TD compute-bound above" and the darkest orange just below it.

  4. When does AR catch up again?

    Hint

    Above \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\), which term of the minimum in Proposition 1 is active, and does it involve \(b\)?

    Solution

    With TD compute-bound and AR memory-bound, \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}})\), and the canvas drops out. Where the cache is negligible and AR touches every expert, \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} = BN_{\mathrm{act}}/W_{\mathrm{tot}} \approx B/8\), so \(\Gamma = 1\) at \(B \approx 8 \times 206/15 \approx 110\), as with \(b = 256\). AR is still memory-bound there: parity only needs \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} = I^\star/\omega \approx 14\). At a few hundred tokens the sliding-window cache, 200 kB per token at intensity 2, already lowers AR's intensity and moves parity to a larger batch. On the map: the upper dashed line and the blue top-left.

  5. Long context. What is the limit of the gain, and what happens at 1M tokens, given the memory?

    Hint

    For the limit, compare \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) with \(I^\star\) (Corollary 3). At 1M tokens, count the sequences that fit next to the weights, then compute TD's intensity \(b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}}/\Pi\) at that batch.

    Solution

    \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 32 \times 8 = 256 \gt 206\), so as \(C \to \infty\) AR is memory-bound and TD compute-bound, and \(\Gamma \to I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{attn}}) = 206/120 \approx 1.7\), whatever \(b\). Memory keeps this limit out of reach. At 1M tokens a sequence's cache is \(1\text{M} \times 20\,\text{kB} \approx 21\) GB, and the \(141 - 50 = 91\) GB left next to the weights hold 4 sequences. At batch 4, AR leaves \(2^{-0.4} \approx 3/4\) of the experts untouched, touches about 30 per layer, and reads \(1.7 + 30 \times 30 \times 6\text{M} \approx 7\)B parameters, 14 GB, plus 84 GB of cache; TD reads \(49 + 84\) GB. Hence \(\Pi \approx 133/98 \approx 1.35\). Attention is \(84/98 \approx 0.86\) of AR's bytes, so \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx 0.86 \times 8 \approx 7\), and TD's intensity is \(32 \times 7/1.35 \approx 170 \lt 206\): both decoders are memory-bound, and \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi \approx 2.1/1.35 \approx 1.6\). At batch 1, \(\Pi \approx (49 + 21)/(6 + 21) \approx 2.6\) and \(\Gamma \approx 0.8\): AR wins. On the map: along the right edge, only the faded cells, which do not fit in memory, approach the limit.

  6. What changes at \(b = 16\)?

    Hint

    Redo the comparison of \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) with \(I^\star\).

    Solution

    \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 128 \lt 206\): a TD pass stays memory-bound at every context, as AR does, and with dense attention \(\Gamma \to \mathrm{TPF} = 16/15 \approx 1.1\). At 1M tokens and batch 4, a TD pass still covers 64 tokens and touches nearly every expert, so \(\Pi \approx 1.35\) as at \(b = 32\), and \(\Gamma \approx 1.07/1.35 \approx 0.8\): AR wins at every batch that fits.

Exercise 3: a dense model

We take a dense model, Llama 3.1 8B, on the same H200 with \(b = 256\), \(S = 14\) and \(r = 1\), and pair it with a TD twin that has no self-conditioning. A dense model has no expert penalty, \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} = 1\), and in BF16 (\(s_w = 2\)) its linear intensity is the batch: \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(B) = 2BN_{\mathrm{act}}/(s_wN_{\mathrm{act}}) = B\).

Figure 17

For a dense model, the roofline bound at context 1k is about 13 at batch 1 and falls like 1/B, to parity at batch 15.

  1. Batch 1, short context. Which regime, and what gain?

    Hint

    Compute TD's linear intensity at batch 1 and compare it with \(I^\star\) (Corollary 1).

    Solution

    \(b\,I_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) = 256 \gt 206\): a TD pass is compute-bound already at batch 1, while AR runs at intensity 1. Hence \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{lin}}(1)) = 206/15 \approx 14\). Nothing is lost to \(\Pi\), which is why dense models have the largest batch-1 gains. On the map: the dark orange bottom row.

  2. Where does AR catch up?

    Hint

    Which term of the minimum in Proposition 1 is active from batch 1 on?

    Solution

    With TD compute-bound and AR memory-bound, \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega B)\), which equals 1 at \(B = 206/15 \approx 14\). TD is compute-bound from batch 1, so batch 1 is also its best batch, and the gain only falls from there. On the map: the dashed line \(\Gamma = 1\), which bends upward at long context, where TD wins at every batch (last question).

  3. Does AR ever become compute-bound at 1k context?

    Hint

    As \(B \to \infty\), the weights drop out of an AR step's bytes per sequence, but the cache does not (Proposition 9).

    Solution

    Without a cache, AR would turn compute-bound at \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) = B = 206\). At 1k tokens, a sequence reads \(1\text{k} \times 131\,\text{kB} \approx 0.13\) GB of cache, which carries \(4 \times 0.13 \approx 0.5\) GFLOPs, on top of the \(2N_{\mathrm{act}} = 14\) GFLOPs of its linear layers. As \(B \to \infty\), AR's intensity therefore tends to \(14.5/0.13 \approx 110 \lt 206\), and no batch makes it compute-bound; the threshold is the context at which \(2N_{\mathrm{act}}/(131\,\text{kB} \times C) \approx 202\), about 530 tokens. AR stays memory-bound, and the gain keeps falling: at batch 256 a sequence reads \(14/256 \approx 0.055\) GB of weights and 0.13 GB of cache for 14.5 GFLOPs, so \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx 78\) and \(\Gamma \approx 206/(15 \times 78) \approx 0.18\).

  4. Long context. What gain, and which contexts fit?

    Hint

    Corollary 3 gives the limit. For the contexts that fit, divide the memory left next to the weights by the cache bytes per token.

    Solution

    \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 1024 \gt I^\star\), so at long context AR is memory-bound and TD compute-bound, with \(\Gamma \to 206/(15 \times 4) \approx 3.4\), twice DiffusionGemma's 1.7, because Llama has half as many query heads per KV head. Memory decides which of these contexts exist: the \(141 - 16 = 125\) GB next to the weights hold about \(125\,\text{GB}/131\,\text{kB} \approx 950\)k tokens for one sequence, and only 15k per sequence at batch 64. At batch 1 and 930k tokens, AR reads 14 GB of weights and 122 GB of cache, so the weights are still 10% of its bytes, \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx 0.9 \times 4 + 0.1 \times 1 \approx 3.7\), and \(\Gamma \approx 206/(15 \times 3.7) \approx 3.7\), slightly above the limit. On the map: the large grey region in the top right.

Exercise 4: a low-ridge accelerator

We go back to DiffusionGemma with \(b = 256\) and \(S = 14\), and run it on an H20, whose ridge point is \(I^\star = 148/4.0 = 37\), against 206 on the H200.

Figure 18

On an H20, whose ridge point is 37, the roofline bound puts TD ahead of AR only up to batch 4 at short context.

  1. Batch 1, short context. Where is TD on the roofline, and what gain?

    Hint

    Evaluate \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\) of Corollary 2 with \(I^\star = 37\), then both terms of the minimum in Proposition 1 at batch 1.

    Solution

    \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} \approx 37 \times 8/256 \approx 1.2\): a TD pass sits at the ridge at batch 1. With \(\Pi(1) \approx W_{\mathrm{tot}}/N_{\mathrm{act}} \approx 8\) and \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) = 1\), \(\Gamma = \min\bigl(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi,\ I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{lin}}(1))\bigr) \approx \min(17/8,\ 37/15) \approx \min(2.1,\ 2.5) = 2.1\).

  2. Where does AR catch up?

    Hint

    Parity is where \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) = I^\star/\omega\). Check whether AR touches every expert at that batch.

    Solution

    \(\Gamma = 1\) when \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) = 37/15 \approx 2.5\). If AR touched every expert, \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) = B/8\) and parity would be at batch 20; but 20 tokens leave \(2^{-1.9} \approx 1/4\) of the experts untouched. At batch 10, the untouched fraction is \(2^{-0.9} \approx 1/2\), so AR touches about 64 experts per layer, reads \(1.7 + 30 \times 64 \times 6\text{M} \approx 13\)B parameters, and \(I_{\mathrm{lin}} \approx 10 \times 3.1/13 \approx 2.4\): parity is at about batch 10. On the map: the dashed line sits lower, close to the bottom; the next question explains why.

  3. What Proposition 1 leaves out. Proposition 1 ignores the vocabulary matrix. By how much does it lower the gain at a few sequences, and where does parity move?

    Hint

    TD is compute-bound and AR memory-bound: compare the FLOPs the matrix adds to a TD position with the bytes it adds to an AR step.

    Solution

    Applying the matrix to one position costs \(2Vd \approx 1.5\) GFLOPs, 24% of \(2N_{\mathrm{act}} \approx 6.2\) GFLOPs. A TD position goes through \(\omega = 15\) passes but meets the matrix \(2S - 1 = 27\) times: as the head of the 14 denoising passes, and as the soft embedding of self-conditioning in 13 of them. TD's FLOPs grow by a factor \(1 + 27 \times 0.24/15 \approx 1.43\), and since TD is compute-bound, so does its time. AR is memory-bound, and at batch 5 the matrix adds 0.74B parameters to the 8B its step reads, a factor 1.1. The gain falls by about \(1.1/1.43 \approx 0.77\), and parity moves to \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) \approx 0.77 \times 2.5 \approx 1.9\). At batch 5, \(2^{-0.5} \approx 0.7\) of the experts are untouched, so AR touches about 38, reads \(1.7 + 30 \times 38 \times 6\text{M} \approx 8.5\)B parameters, and \(I_{\mathrm{lin}} \approx 15.5/8.5 \approx 1.8\): parity is near batch 5. On the H20, TD is compute-bound from batch 1, so every FLOP that Proposition 1 omits lowers the gain; on the H200, the same FLOPs count only above \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star}\).

  4. Long context. Who wins?

    Hint

    Place \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) relative to \(I^\star = 37\) and apply Corollary 3.

    Solution

    \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 8 \lt 37 \lt b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\), so at long context TD is compute-bound and AR memory-bound, and \(\Gamma \to 37/(15 \times 8) \approx 0.31\): in the limit, AR wins at every batch. With such a low ridge, AR's attention runs at \(8/37\), about a fifth of peak, while TD runs at peak but does 15 times the work. The \(96 - 50 = 46\) GB next to the weights hold one sequence of about \(46\,\text{GB}/20\,\text{kB} \approx 2.2\)M tokens; there AR reads 6 GB of weights and 45 GB of cache, \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx 0.9 \times 8 \approx 7\), and \(\Gamma \approx 37/(15 \times 7) \approx 0.35\). At batch 1, TD keeps the lead while \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \lt 2.5\), that is, while the cache is at most a fifth of AR's bytes, about 1.5 GB of global cache, or 75k tokens. On the map: the right half is blue, except its bottom rows.

Exercise 5: fewer denoising passes

We go back to the H200, with DiffusionGemma, dense attention and \(b = 256\), and vary the number of denoising passes \(S\), with \(r = 1\).

  1. From \(S = 14\) to \(S = 7\). How does the gain of Proposition 1 change?

    Hint

    Which quantities in Proposition 1 depend on \(S\)?

    Solution

    Only \(\omega = Sr + 1\) does: the FLOPs and bytes of one pass do not depend on \(S\), and neither do the intensities or the regimes. Hence \(\Gamma\) is multiplied by \(15/8 \approx 1.9\) at every batch and context. The boundaries between regimes stay where they are, and the parity lines move to where \(\Gamma = 8/15\) at \(S = 14\).

  2. The largest \(S\). For which \(S\) does TD keep a gain above 1 at long context?

    Hint

    Write the limit of Corollary 3 as a function of \(\omega\).

    Solution

    \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 2048 \gt 206\), so \(\Gamma \to I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{attn}}) = 206/(8\omega)\), which is above 1 when \(\omega \lt 26\): \(S \le 24\).

Exercise 6: sparse attention

We keep DiffusionGemma on the H200 with \(b = 256\), \(S = 14\) and \(r = 1\), but replace its five global layers with the sparse layers of Remark 1 (Table 3), as in Figure 11.

  1. Long context. Does TD win in the limit?

    Hint

    As \(C \to \infty\), the index scan dominates both passes. Place \(I_{\mathrm{idx}}\) and \(b\,I_{\mathrm{idx}}\) relative to \(I^\star\) (Corollary 3).

    Solution

    \(I_{\mathrm{idx}} = 16 \lt 206 \lt b\,I_{\mathrm{idx}} = 4096\): TD is compute-bound and AR memory-bound, and \(\Gamma \to 206/(15 \times 16) \approx 0.86\), so AR wins. The index scan has twice the intensity of dense attention, which halves the limit: AR runs its scan at \(16/206 \approx 8\%\) of peak, while TD runs at peak but does 15 times the work, and \(15 \times 8\% \gt 1\). In Figure 11, the shading reaches the right edge.

  2. The largest \(S\). For which \(S\) does TD win in the limit?

    Hint

    Write the same limit as a function of \(\omega\).

    Solution

    \(206/(16\omega) \gt 1\) needs \(\omega \lt 13\): \(S \le 11\), against \(S \le 24\) with dense attention (Exercise 5).

  3. Batch 1, 4M tokens. Who wins, and why does the limit not apply yet?

    Hint

    Compare the index cache of a 4M-token sequence with the weights an AR step reads at batch 1. For TD, count the distinct tokens its 256 queries select, \(U_{C,m}(b)\).

    Solution

    The index cache holds 640 B per token, so 4M tokens are only 2.7 GB, against 6.2 GB of weights at batch 1 and 0.2 GB of window cache. Attention is therefore about 0.3 of AR's bytes, and \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx 0.7 \times 1 + 0.3 \times 16 \approx 5.5\). TD's 256 queries select \(U_{C,m}(b) \approx bm \approx 520\)k distinct tokens, since \(bm \ll C\), and read them from the global cache at 20 kB each, about 10.5 GB. TD reads about \(49 + 2.7 + 10.5 \approx 62\) GB against AR's 9 GB, so \(\Pi \approx 7\) and \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi \approx 2.4\); its intensity, \(256 \times 5.5/7 \approx 200\), puts it at the ridge, so both terms of Proposition 1 give \(\Gamma \approx 2.5\): TD wins. The limit needs the index cache to outweigh the weights, which happens only beyond \(6.2\,\text{GB}/640\,\text{B} \approx 10\)M tokens.

Exercise 7: other MoE models

We compare four MoE models, each paired with a TD twin, on the H200 with \(b = 256\), \(S = 14\) and \(r = 1\). None of the three below has a dense FFN, and none shares K and V:

  • Qwen3-30B-A3B: \(E = 128\), \(k = 8\), \(f_E = 768\); 32 query heads and 4 KV heads of dimension 128.
  • gpt-oss-120b: \(E = 128\), \(k = 4\), \(f_E = 2{,}880\); 64 query heads and 8 KV heads of dimension 64.
  • Mixtral 8x7B: \(E = 8\), \(k = 2\), \(f_E = 14{,}336\); 32 query heads and 8 KV heads of dimension 128.

  1. Batch 1, short context. Which model pays the largest penalty for the canvas, and which has the largest gain?

    Hint

    A canvas of 256 tokens touches every expert of each model, so \(\Pi_E = E/k\) (Proposition 4). Compute \(\theta_E(1)\) per layer and in units of \(d\): \(k\) experts weigh \(3kf_E\), and attention weighs \(h\) times twice the number of query and KV heads.

    Solution

    With \(\Pi(1) \approx 1 + \theta_E(1)\,(E/k - 1)\), per layer and in units of \(d\):

    • Qwen3-30B-A3B: experts \(8 \times 3 \times 768 \approx 18\)k, attention \(128 \times 72 \approx 9\)k, so \(\theta_E \approx 2/3\) and \(\Pi \approx 1 + \tfrac23 \times 15 = 11\).
    • gpt-oss-120b: experts \(4 \times 3 \times 2{,}880 \approx 35\)k, attention \(64 \times 144 \approx 9\)k, so \(\theta_E \approx 0.8\) and \(\Pi \approx 1 + 0.8 \times 31 \approx 25\).
    • Mixtral 8x7B: experts \(2 \times 3 \times 14{,}336 \approx 86\)k, attention \(128 \times 80 \approx 10\)k, so \(\theta_E \approx 0.9\) and \(\Pi \approx 1 + 0.9 \times 3 \approx 3.7\).
    • DiffusionGemma: \(\theta_E \approx 1/2\) (Exercise 2) and \(\Pi \approx W_{\mathrm{tot}}/N_{\mathrm{act}} \approx 8\).

    TD's intensity is \(256/\Pi\), at most 70, so both decoders are memory-bound and \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi \approx 17/\Pi\): about 4.6 for Mixtral, 2.1 for DiffusionGemma, 1.5 for Qwen3 and 0.7 for gpt-oss-120b, the only one where AR wins. Many fine-grained experts (a large \(E/k\)) and a large expert share make a long canvas expensive at batch 1; Mixtral's few large experts make it cheap.

Exercise 8: number formats

We keep DiffusionGemma on the H200 with \(b = 256\), \(S = 14\), \(r = 1\) and a 1k context, and change the format of the weights, of the KV cache and of the matrix multiplications. A format of \(s\) bytes per value multiplies the intensity of what it stores by \(2/s\): \(s_w\) acts on \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and \(s_{\mathrm{kv}}\) on \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 4g/(\kappa^c s_{\mathrm{kv}})\) (Definitions 2 and 3). The format of a multiplication sets the peak, and hence the ridge, at which its FLOPs run. The questions follow the roofline chapter of How to Scale Your Model, which compares int8 weights with BF16 and with int8 multiplications.

  1. FP8 everywhere. Weights and cache are in FP8, and every multiplication, attention included, runs at the FP8 peak. How do the points of Figure 15 move, and what happens to \(\Gamma\)?

    Hint

    Multiply \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}}\), \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}}\) and \(I^\star\) by the same factor in Proposition 1.

    Solution

    Every byte count halves, so every intensity doubles, and the ridge doubles to \(1{,}979/4.8 \approx 412\). Each point moves right by a factor 2 and the roof moves up by 2, so each decoder runs twice as fast wherever it is. Both terms of \(\Gamma = \min(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}}, I^\star)/\bigl(\omega\min(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}}, I^\star)\bigr)\) are multiplied by 2, so \(\Gamma\) does not change at any batch or context: 2.1 at batch 1, 7 at batch 6, parity at batch 180. A format changes \(\Gamma\) only when it speeds up the bytes and the FLOPs by different factors.

  2. FP8 weights, BF16 multiplications. The weights are stored in FP8 and converted to BF16 inside the kernel, as on an A100, which has no FP8 rate; the cache stays in BF16. Give \(\Gamma\) at batch 1, the best batch and the gain there, and the parity batch.

    Hint

    Only \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}\) doubles; the ridge stays at 206. Redo Questions 1–3 of Exercise 1 with \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) = 2BN_{\mathrm{act}}/\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)\).

    Solution

    At batch 1, \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx 2\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}} \approx 64\), both below the ridge: each decoder runs twice as fast, and \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi \approx 2.1\) as in BF16. TD's intensity, \(64B\), reaches 206 at batch 3, half the batch of BF16: storing the weights in fewer bytes halves the batch at which a matrix multiplication becomes compute-bound, as in How to Scale Your Model. At batch 3, AR leaves \(2^{-1.5 \times 3/16} \approx 0.82\) of the experts untouched, so it touches 23 per layer and reads \(1.7 + 30 \times 23 \times 6\text{M} \approx 5.8\)B parameters, and \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx 2 \times 3 \times 3.1/5.8 \approx 3.2\). The best gain is \(\Gamma \approx 206/(15 \times 3.2) \approx 4.3\), against 7 in BF16. At parity, \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} = 14\) with 6.8 GFLOPs for \(24.5/B + 0.22\) GB, so \(24.5/B \approx 0.27\) and parity is at about batch 90, half of 180. Halving the weight bytes alone thus acts on the linear layers as halving the ridge: the H200 with FP8 weights behaves like a BF16 accelerator with \(I^\star \approx 103\), and, as on the H20 (Exercise 4), TD loses its advantage at a smaller batch.

  3. NVFP4. NVFP4 stores 4-bit values with one FP8 scale per block of 16 (Table 3). The H200 has no FP4 multiplication and converts the weights to BF16; the B200 multiplies them at its FP4 peak. For each, give the BF16 ridge it is equivalent to, the best batch and the gain there; attention is negligible at 1k tokens.

    Hint

    Count the bytes per weight, scale included, and use the equivalence of Question 2: the ridge divided by \(2/s_w\).

    Solution

    A weight costs \(4 + 8/16 = 4.5\) bits, so \(s_w \approx 0.56\) and \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}\) is multiplied by \(2/0.56 \approx 3.6\).

    • H200: the ridge stays at 206, which is equivalent to \(206/3.6 \approx 57\) in BF16, between the H20 and the H200. TD's intensity, \(32 \times 3.6\,B \approx 115B\), reaches the ridge at batch 2. There AR leaves \(2^{-1.5 \times 2/16} \approx 0.88\) of the experts untouched, touches 15 per layer and reads \(1.7 + 30 \times 15 \times 6\text{M} \approx 4.4\)B parameters, so \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \approx 3.6 \times 2 \times 3.1/4.4 \approx 5\) and \(\Gamma \approx 206/(15 \times 5) \approx 2.7\). Parity, with 14 GB of weights, is at \(14/B \approx 0.27\): about batch 50.
    • B200: the FP4 ridge is \(9{,}000/8 \approx 1{,}125\), equivalent to \(1{,}125/3.6 \approx 310\) in BF16. TD reaches it at \(B \approx 310/32 \approx\) 10, where AR leaves \(2^{-0.94} \approx 0.52\) of the experts untouched, touches 61 per layer and reads \(1.7 + 30 \times 61 \times 6\text{M} \approx 12.7\)B parameters, so the BF16 intensity is \(10 \times 3.1/12.7 \approx 2.4\) and \(\Gamma \approx 310/(15 \times 2.4) \approx 9\). In BF16 on the same B200, the ridge is 281 and the same steps give \(\Gamma \approx 8\) at batch 9.

    A format therefore enters \(\Gamma\) only through the ratio \(\rho\) of the speed-up of the multiplications to the reduction of the weight bytes, which multiplies the effective ridge: \(\rho = 1\) for FP8 everywhere, \(1/2\) for FP8 weights alone, \(1/3.6\) for NVFP4 on the H200 and \(4/3.6 \approx 1.1\) for NVFP4 on the B200. At batch 1 both decoders stay memory-bound and \(\Gamma \approx 2.1\) in every case.

  4. FP8 cache, long context. The weights are back in BF16, the cache is in FP8 and attention runs at the BF16 peak. Does TD win in the limit of long context? What changes if attention runs at the FP8 peak?

    Hint

    Compute \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) with \(s_{\mathrm{kv}} = 1\), and use the limit \(I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{attn}})\) of Corollary 3.

    Solution

    With \(s_{\mathrm{kv}} = 1\), \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 16\), and the limit is \(206/(15 \times 16) \approx 0.86\): AR wins, as with the index scan of Exercise 6, whose intensity is also 16. The FP8 cache halves AR's attention bytes and leaves TD's attention FLOPs, which already run at the peak, unchanged. If attention also runs at the FP8 peak, its ridge doubles to 412, and the limit is \(412/(15 \times 16) \approx 1.7\), the BF16 value: by Question 1, the cache and the attention change by the same factor. In both cases a sequence's cache is half as large, so twice the context fits in the same memory.
