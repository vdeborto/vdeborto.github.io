Exercises

Part 4 of Block Diffusion Rooflines: three exercises with folded solutions: a smaller canvas, a dense model, a low-ridge accelerator.

Part 4 of Block Diffusion RooflinesPrevious: 3 · Maps and calculatorNext: 5 · Empirical rooflines

This chapter is a set of exercises. The propositions are meant to be used: from a few quantities, they should tell us what the exact map looks like before we compute it. Each exercise changes one thing in the running example (the canvas, the model or the accelerator) and asks a few questions about the result. Try to answer each question with the propositions first; its solution is folded below it and compares the prediction with the exact map, which the calculator of Part 3 also reproduces.

Exercise 1: a smaller canvas

We keep DiffusionGemma, the H200, \(S = 14\) and \(r = 1\), so that \(\omega = 15\), but we shrink the canvas from 256 to \(b = 32\). The exact map below changes completely. We encourage the reader to predict each feature from the propositions before opening its solution. Every solution also says where to look on the map.

Figure 16

With a 32-token canvas, the roofline bound puts TD ahead of AR only between batch 9 and batch 110 at context 1k.

  1. Batch 1, short context. Compute TPF and \(\Pi(1)\). Who wins?

    Solution

    \(\mathrm{TPF} = 32/15 = 2.13\). A 32-token canvas touches \(U_{E,k}(32) = 128\,[1 - (15/16)^{32}] = 112\) experts per layer, so \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}(1) = 1.66 + 30 \times 112 \times 0.00595 = 21.6\)B against \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(1) = 3.08\)B: \(\Pi(1) = 7.0\). Both are memory-bound, so \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi = 0.30\): AR wins by about 3×. The penalty is only slightly smaller than at \(b = 256\) (7.0 against 7.9), but TPF has shrunk eightfold. On the map: the blue bottom-left corner.

  2. Where does TD overtake AR? Find the batch at which \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi(B) = 1\).

    Solution

    From batch ≈ 4 the canvas touches every expert, so \(\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}} \approx W_{\mathrm{tot}} = 24.5\)B. Parity needs \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B) = W_{\mathrm{tot}}/\mathrm{TPF} = 11.5\)B, that is, \(U_{E,k}(B) = 55\), that is, \(1 - (15/16)^B = 0.43\): \(B \approx 8.7\) (exact: 8.3, so TD is ahead from batch 9). On the map: the lower dashed line, \(\Gamma = 1\).

  3. Where is TD's best batch, and how large is the gain there?

    Solution

    \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} = I^\star s_w\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}/(2bN_{\mathrm{act}}) = 206 \times 49.0\,\text{GB}/(2 \times 32 \times 3.08\text{B}) \approx 51\): at a batch eight times larger than at \(b = 256\), since \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} \propto 1/b\). There \(\Pi(51) = 1.04\), so the peak is \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi \approx 2.1\). The exact accounting gives 1.8 at batch 46: it includes the vocabulary matrix multiplications, which add to TD's FLOPs, so TD becomes compute-bound at a slightly smaller batch and a lower gain. On the map: the solid line labelled "TD compute-bound above" and the darkest orange just below it.

  4. When does AR catch up again?

    Solution

    With AR memory-bound and TD compute-bound, the canvas drops out: \(B_\times = \class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}/\omega = 1640/15 \approx 109\), as with \(b = 256\). AR is still memory-bound there; it only needs \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}} \gt I^\star/\omega \approx 14\). On the map: the upper dashed line and the blue top-left.

  5. Long context. Which regime, and what gain? What changes at \(b = 16\)?

    Solution

    \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 256 \gt 206\), so in the limit \(C \to \infty\) AR is memory-bound and TD compute-bound, with \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{attn}}) = 1.72\), independent of \(b\). This limit is not reached in memory: at 1M context a TD pass with \(b = 32\) is compute-bound only from batch 8.1 by Proposition 8 (Figure 14), or 8.2 in the exact accounting, while at most 4 sequences fit. At batch 1 the weights still count in an AR step's bytes, \(\Pi = 2.3\) and \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi = 0.92\), so AR wins; at batch 4, \(\Gamma = 1.58\) (1.56 in the exact accounting). At \(b = 16\), \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 128 \lt 206\): TD stays memory-bound, as AR, and \(\Gamma \to \mathrm{TPF} = 16/15 = 1.07\). On the map: the right edge is between 1.6 and 1.9 only in the faded cells, which do not fit in memory; the cells that fit go from 0.9 at batch 1 to 1.6 at batch 4.

Exercise 2: a dense model

We now change the model and take a dense one, Llama 3.1 8B. We keep the same H200 and the same canvas (\(b = 256\), \(S = 14\), \(r = 1\)), and we pair the model with a TD twin that has no self-conditioning. A dense model has no expert penalty, i.e., \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} = 1\). In addition, in BF16 (\(s_w = 2\)), its linear intensity is the batch: \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(B) = 2BN_{\mathrm{act}}/(s_wN_{\mathrm{act}}) = B\).

Figure 17

For a dense model, the roofline bound at context 1k is about 13 at batch 1 and falls like 1/B, to parity at batch 15.

  1. Batch 1, short context. Which regime, and what gain?

    Solution

    \(b\,I_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) = 256\) is above \(I^\star = 206\), so a TD pass is already compute-bound at batch 1, while AR runs at intensity 1. Hence, AR is memory-bound and TD compute-bound: \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{lin}}(1)) = 206/15 = 13.7\) (exact: 13.3). Nothing is lost to \(\Pi\), which is why dense models have the largest batch-1 gains. On the map: the dark orange bottom row.

  2. Where does AR catch up?

    Solution

    With TD compute-bound and AR memory-bound, \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega B)\), so \(\Gamma = 1\) at \(B_\times = I^\star/\omega \approx 14\) (exact: 15). TD is compute-bound from batch 1, so batch 1 is also its best batch, and the gain only falls from there. On the map: the dashed line \(\Gamma = 1\). It starts near batch 15 at short context and turns sharply upward around 11k–12k tokens, because at long context TD wins at every batch (last question).

  3. Does AR ever become compute-bound at 1k context?

    Solution

    Without a cache it would at \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) = I^\star\), batch 206. But Llama's cache holds 128 KiB per token (32 layers, 8 KV heads, head dimension 128, K and V, 2 bytes), and, with \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 4\), Proposition 8 says no batch makes an AR step compute-bound once the context exceeds about 530 tokens. So AR stays memory-bound, and the gain keeps falling instead: 0.17 at batch 256.

  4. Long context. What gain, and which contexts actually fit?

    Solution

    \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 1024 \gt I^\star\), so at long context AR is memory-bound and TD compute-bound, with \(\Gamma = 206/(15 \times 4) = 3.4\), twice DiffusionGemma's 1.7 because Llama has half as many query heads per KV head. Memory decides which of these contexts exist: next to 16 GB of weights, one sequence fits up to about 950k tokens and a batch of 64 only up to 14k. At batch 1 and 930k tokens the exact gain is 3.7, a little above the limit because the weights are still 11% of AR's bytes and hold its intensity below 4. On the map: the large grey region in the top right.

Exercise 3: a low-ridge accelerator

Finally, we change the accelerator. We go back to DiffusionGemma with \(b = 256\) and \(S = 14\), but we run it on an H20, which has 148 TFLOP/s of BF16, 4.0 TB/s and 96 GB. Its ridge point is \(I^\star = 148/4.0 = 37\), against 206 on the H200.

Figure 18

On an H20, whose ridge point is 37, the roofline bound puts TD ahead of AR only up to batch 4 at short context.

  1. Batch 1, short context. Where is TD on the roofline, and what gain?

    Solution

    \(\class{mj-td}{B^{\mathrm{TD}}_\star} = I^\star s_w\class{mj-td}{W^{\mathrm{TD}}}/(2bN_{\mathrm{act}}) = 37 \times 49.0\,\text{GB}/(2 \times 256 \times 3.08\text{B}) = 1.15\): a TD pass sits just below the ridge at batch 1. Corollary 1 gives \(\Gamma = \min(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi,\ I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{lin}})) = \min(2.15,\ 2.47) = 2.15\). The exact gain is 2.03, with TD already compute-bound because the vocabulary adds to its FLOPs.

  2. Where does AR catch up?

    Solution

    With TD compute-bound and AR memory-bound, \(\Gamma = 1\) when \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) = I^\star/\omega = 2.47\). The closed form \(B_\times = \class{mj-ar}{B^{\mathrm{AR}}_\star}/\omega = 19.6\) does not apply here, because at such small batches AR does not yet touch every expert. Solving with the full \(\class{mj-ar}{W^{\mathrm{AR}}}(B)\) gives \(B \approx 10\). The exact parity is at 4.5: once TD is compute-bound its vocabulary FLOPs count in full and lower its gain by about a fifth (a factor 0.79 at batches 4–5), so parity comes much earlier. On the map: the dashed line, close to the bottom.

  3. Long context. Who wins?

    Solution

    \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 8 \lt 37 \lt b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\), so at long context TD is still compute-bound and AR memory-bound, but now \(\Gamma = 37/(15 \times 8) = 0.31\). In the limit of long context, AR wins at every batch. With such a low ridge, AR's attention already runs at \(8/37\), about a fifth of peak, while TD runs at peak but does 15 times the work. The exact gain is 0.62 at batch 1 and 256k tokens, and 0.35 at 2.2M tokens, the longest single sequence that fits next to the weights. On the map: the right half is blue, except its bottom rows, where TD still wins at batch 1 up to about 90k tokens and at batch 2 up to about 34k.
