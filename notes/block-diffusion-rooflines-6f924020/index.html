Block Diffusion Rooflines

Block text diffusion (TD) writes a block of tokens at once, but runs the model many times to do it. Autoregressive decoding (AR) runs the model once per token. This note works out, with a roofline model, when TD produces more tokens per second than AR for the same model on the same accelerator. The running example is DiffusionGemma 26B-A4B and its autoregressive twin, which runs the same weights one token at a time.

\[ \Gamma \;=\; \frac{1}{\omega}\cdot\frac{\min\bigl(I_{TD},\,I^\star\bigr)}{\min\bigl(I_{AR},\,I^\star\bigr)},\qquad \omega = S\,r + 1 \]

\(\Gamma\) is the multiplicative throughput gain of TD over AR. \(\omega = Sr + 1\) is the cost of one canvas in forward passes: \(S\) denoising passes at \(r\) forward passes each, plus one commit pass. \(I_{AR}\) and \(I_{TD}\) are the arithmetic intensities of an AR step and of a TD pass, in FLOPs per byte read from memory. \(I^\star\) is the accelerator's ridge point, its peak FLOP/s divided by its memory bandwidth.

Four things to remember
  1. TD trades work for reuse. A canvas of \(b\) tokens costs \(\omega\) forward passes instead of one, so TD does \(\omega\) times more FLOPs per token than AR. In return, every byte it reads from memory serves \(b\) positions instead of one.
  2. The best batch is the one at which a TD pass just becomes compute-bound. At smaller batches TD is still memory-bound, and the gain grows as a larger batch dilutes the MoE penalty. At larger batches TD already runs at peak while AR keeps speeding up, so the gain falls like \(1/B\).
  3. At long context the batch stops mattering. Both decoders are then reading the KV cache, which belongs to one sequence, so no batch can share it. What still separates them is when they become compute-bound: an AR step reads the cache for one query and never reaches the ridge, while a TD pass reads it once for \(b\) queries and does.
  4. A higher ridge point means more to gain. Whenever TD is compute-bound and AR is not, the gain scales with \(I^\star\), because \(I^\star\) measures the compute AR leaves idle. The price is that TD needs more to reach the ridge: a larger batch at short context, and a canvas longer than \(I^\star/I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) at long context. On a DGX Spark, 273 GB/s against a model that reads 3 GB per token caps AR at 90 tokens per second and 0.1% of the chip's compute; the other 99.9% is what TD spends.

What drives the gain

Canvas cost, \(\omega\)

A canvas costs \(\omega = Sr+1\) forward passes. This divides the gain in every regime.

Canvas length, \(b\)

Every byte a TD pass reads serves \(b\) positions. While TD is memory-bound this sets the gain, through \(\mathrm{TPF} = b/\omega\). It also sets the batch at which TD becomes compute-bound, which scales like \(1/b\).

Linear penalty, \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\)

A canvas of 256 positions routes to almost every expert, while an AR step at batch \(B\) reaches about \(kB\). At batch 1 the penalty is close to total over active parameters (7.9 for DiffusionGemma); it falls to 1 as the batch grows.

Attention penalty, \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\)

1 for dense and windowed attention: the canvas reads the cache once for \(b\) queries. Above 1 for sparse attention, where each query selects its own tokens and the canvas reads the union. Same formula as the linear penalty, with the canvas size in the role of the batch size.

Batch, \(B\)

The weights are shared by the whole batch, so a larger batch raises AR's intensity. It helps TD first, by shrinking \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\), and AR after that. It does nothing for the cache, which is per sequence.

Context, \(C\)

As the context grows, attention bytes overtake the weights, and the intensities move from their linear values to their attention values: \(I_{AR} \to I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(I_{TD} \to b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}/\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\).

Three regimes matter

AR compute-bound
\(\Gamma = 1/\omega\)

TD is then compute-bound as well. Both run at peak, and TD does \(\omega\) times the work. Rare for MoE decoding: DiffusionGemma on an H200 would need a batch of about 1,600.

Both memory-bound
\(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi\)

TD's extra FLOPs cost nothing. Each read serves TPF tokens, minus the penalty of whichever bytes dominate: 17.1 / 7.9 ≈ 2.2 at batch 1.

AR memory-, TD compute-bound
\(\Gamma = \dfrac{I^\star}{\omega\,I_{AR}}\)

TD uses the compute that AR leaves idle. At short context this falls like \(1/B\); at long context it tends to \(I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{attn}})\), 1.72 on an H200, at any batch.

While AR is memory-bound, \(\Gamma = \min\bigl(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi,\ I^\star/(\omega I_{AR})\bigr)\). The first term grows with the batch and the second shrinks, which is why the best batch is where TD turns compute-bound.

What the formulas leave out

  • Vocabulary and self-conditioning. Per token, TD runs \(2S-1\) vocabulary-sized matmuls against AR's one, and a smaller decoder does not shrink them. The maps and the calculator include them.
  • Kernels run one after another. Assumption (A1) gives a pass the time of a single kernel in which all of its compute and all of its memory traffic overlap. Adding up the roofline times of its kernels instead is never faster and at most twice slower, and it moves DiffusionGemma's best batch from 5 to 9 (§5). The calculator offers both.
  • Small effects. Activations (they stay on-chip once kernels are fused), KV writes, and the at most \(b\) keys inside the canvas.
  • Cross-layer KV sharing. It divides the KV cache's size, not the bytes read per pass, since each layer still reads the shared cache. It changes the largest batch that fits in memory, not any intensity.
  • Beyond the roofline. Kernel efficiency (vLLM reaches 84% of the bound at batch 1 and 34% at batch 64), adaptive step counts, uneven expert routing, correlated block selections, the wait for the first canvas, and quality per step.

2 The gain

Proposition 1TD/AR throughput gain
A1 · Roofline
A pass with \(F\) FLOPs and \(Q\) HBM bytes takes \(\max(F/\pi,\,Q/\beta)\).
A2 · Costs
Per sequence, an AR step costs \(F^{AR}\) FLOPs and \(Q^{AR}\) bytes, and a TD pass \(F^{TD}\) and \(Q^{TD}\), as in Definition 2. Vocabulary, activations, KV writes and in-canvas keys are neglected. Expert routing and token selection are independent across positions.
A3 · Canvas
A canvas commits \(b\) tokens per sequence after \(S\) denoising passes and one commit pass. A denoising pass costs \(r\) times a commit pass, in FLOPs and in bytes.
A4 · Lockstep
\(B\) sequences share every pass, at a context \(C\) held fixed over a canvas.

Then TD's throughput over AR's is

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}\Gamma = \frac{1}{\omega}\cdot\frac{\min(I_{TD},\,I^\star)}{\min(I_{AR},\,I^\star)},\\[4pt] \omega = S\,r+1,\qquad I_{TD} = \frac{b}{\Pi}\,I_{AR}.\end{gathered}} \]
Proof
  1. For a pass with \(I = F/Q\): \(\max(F/\pi,\,Q/\beta) = \dfrac{F}{\beta}\max\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{I^\star},\dfrac{1}{I}\Bigr) = \dfrac{F}{\beta\,\min(I, I^\star)}\).
  2. AR. A step costs \(BF^{AR}\) FLOPs at intensity \(I_{AR}\) and yields \(B\) tokens: \(t_{AR} = F^{AR}\,/\,\bigl(\beta\min(I_{AR}, I^\star)\bigr)\) per token.
  3. TD. A commit pass costs \(BF^{TD} = BbF^{AR}\) FLOPs at intensity \(I_{TD}\). A denoising pass multiplies both \(F^{TD}\) and \(Q^{TD}\) by \(r\), so it has the same intensity and takes \(r\) times as long. A canvas therefore takes \(\omega\) times one commit pass and yields \(Bb\) tokens: \(t_{TD} = \omega F^{AR}\,/\,\bigl(\beta\min(I_{TD}, I^\star)\bigr)\) per token.
  4. \(\Gamma = t_{AR}/t_{TD}\), and \(I_{TD} = F^{TD}/Q^{TD} = bF^{AR}/(\Pi Q^{AR}) = bI_{AR}/\Pi\) by Definition 4. \(\blacksquare\)
Corollary 1Which side wins

If AR is compute-bound (\(I_{AR} \ge I^\star\)), then \(\Gamma \le 1/\omega\). Otherwise

\[ \Gamma = \min\Bigl(\frac{\mathrm{TPF}}{\Pi},\ \frac{I^\star}{\omega\,I_{AR}}\Bigr),\quad \mathrm{TPF} = \frac{b}{\omega}. \]
Proof

If \(I_{AR} \ge I^\star\) the denominator of Proposition 1 is \(I^\star\) and the numerator at most \(I^\star\). Otherwise \(\min(bI_{AR}/\Pi,\,I^\star)/I_{AR} = \min(b/\Pi,\,I^\star/I_{AR})\); divide by \(\omega\). \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 2Each intensity is a byte-weighted average of its linear and attention parts

Under (A2), for \(X \in \{AR, TD\}\), let \(\theta^X_{\mathrm{lin}} = Q^X_{\mathrm{lin}}/Q^X\) and \(\theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}} = Q^X_{\mathrm{attn}}/Q^X\) be the shares of the two parts in the bytes of Definition 2. Then

\[ I_X = \theta^X_{\mathrm{lin}}\,I^X_{\mathrm{lin}} + \theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}}\,I^X_{\mathrm{attn}},\qquad \Pi - 1 = \theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1) + \theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} - 1). \]
\[ \begin{gathered} I_X = \theta^X_{\mathrm{lin}}\,I^X_{\mathrm{lin}} + \theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}}\,I^X_{\mathrm{attn}},\\[4pt] \Pi - 1 = \theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1)\\ \qquad + \theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} - 1). \end{gathered} \]

Consequently each intensity lies between its two parts, and \(I_{TD}/I_{AR}\) lies between \(b/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and \(b/\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\), nearer to whichever part carries more of the bytes.

Proof

\(I_X = F^X/Q^X = \sum_c (Q^X_c/Q^X)(F^X_c/Q^X_c)\) over the two parts \(c\). For the penalty, \(Q^{TD}_c = \Pi_c\,Q^{AR}_c\) by Definition 4, so \(\Pi - 1 = \sum_c (Q^{TD}_c - Q^{AR}_c)/Q^{AR} = \sum_c \theta^{AR}_c(\Pi_c - 1)\). A weighted average with positive weights lies between its terms; \(I_{TD} = (b/\Pi)I_{AR}\) and \(\Pi\) lies between \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 3The MoE penalty

Under (A2), \(I^{TD}_{\mathrm{lin}} = b\,I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\), with \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1 = \theta_E\,(\Pi_E - 1)\), where \(\theta_E = L\,U_{E,k}(B)\,W^e/W_{AR}(B)\) is the expert share of the weights an AR step reads and \(\Pi_E = U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B)\). Moreover:

  1. \(1 \le \Pi_E \le \min(b,\ E/k)\), and \(\Pi_E\) is non-increasing in \(B\): \(\Pi_E - 1 \le (E/k)\,e^{-kB/E}\), so the penalty disappears at large batch.
  2. At \(b \gg E/k\), a canvas touches every expert and \(\Pi_E \approx E/U_{E,k}(B)\), which at batch 1 is \(E/k\); then \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) \approx W_{\mathrm{tot}}/N_{\mathrm{act}}\), total over active parameters.
  3. For a dense model (\(k = E\)), \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} \equiv 1\).
Proof

The first identity is Definition 4 restricted to the linear part; the split of \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1\) is Proposition 2's argument applied to the dense and expert parts of the weights, the dense part having penalty 1. (1) \(U_{E,k}\) is increasing and concave with \(U_{E,k}(0) = 0\) and \(U_{E,k}(N) \le \min(kN, E)\), so \(U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B) \le b\) by concavity and \(\le E/U_{E,k}(B) \le E/k\); and \(U_{E,k}(Bb) - U_{E,k}(B) \le E(1-k/E)^B \le Ee^{-kB/E}\) with \(U_{E,k}(B) \ge k\). (2) \(U_{E,k}(Bb) \ge E(1 - e^{-kb/E}) \approx E\), and \(U_{E,k}(1) = k\); \(W_{AR}(1) = W_{\mathrm{ne}} + LkW^e = N_{\mathrm{act}}\). (3) \(U_{E,k}(N) = E\) for all \(N \ge 1\). \(\blacksquare\)

For DiffusionGemma, \(E/k = 16\) and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) = 24.5/3.08 = 7.9\): a canvas streams all 24.5B parameters where a token streams the 3.08B it activates, and the dense 1.66B, shared by both, dilute the ratio from 16 to 7.9. By batch 48 (three times \(E/k\)) an AR step touches every expert too, and the penalty is gone.

Proposition 4When the linear layers dominate

Under (A2), with the windows full (\(C \ge w\)) and dense attention, an AR step spends at least a share \(x\) of its bytes on weights exactly when

\[ C \;\le\; C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, x) = \frac{1}{\nu_G}\Bigl(\frac{1-x}{x}\cdot\frac{s_wW_{AR}(B)}{B} - \nu_W\,w\Bigr). \]
\[ \begin{gathered} C \le C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, x)\\[2pt] = \frac{1}{\nu_G}\Bigl(\frac{1-x}{x}\,\frac{s_wW_{AR}(B)}{B} - \nu_W w\Bigr). \end{gathered} \]

Moreover:

  1. \(C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B,x)\) is non-increasing in \(B\): the weights are shared across the batch, the cache is not. Once the batch touches every expert (\(W_{AR}(B) = W_{\mathrm{tot}}\)), and for dense models at every batch, it falls exactly like \(1/B\); below that it falls more slowly, since \(W_{AR}(B)\) still grows with \(B\).
  2. For a TD pass, replace \(W_{AR}\) by \(W_{TD}\): its threshold is \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\) times further out, up to the window term.
  3. With sparse layers, replace \(\nu_G C\) by \(\nu_S\min(C,m) + \nu_I C\) for an AR step and by \(\nu_S U_{C,m}(b) + \nu_I C\) for a TD pass.
Proof

\(\theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}} \ge x \iff s_wW_{AR}/B \ge \tfrac{x}{1-x}\,(\nu_GC + \nu_Ww)\); solve for \(C\). (1) \(W_{AR}(B)/B\) is non-increasing because \(U_{E,k}\) is concave with \(U_{E,k}(0) = 0\), and constant over \(B\) once \(U_{E,k}(B) = E\). (2) and (3) substitute the TD and sparse byte counts of Definition 2. \(\blacksquare\)

DiffusionGemma's cache is small (2 KV heads in 5 global layers, \(\nu_G = 20\) kB per token, plus a fixed 210 MB per sequence for the windows), so its linear layers dominate for a long way. A dense GQA model such as Llama-3-8B, with 131 kB per token and 16 GB of weights, reaches equal weight and cache bytes at \(BC \approx 122\)k.

The gain when the linear layers dominate

With \(I_{AR} \approx I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and \(\Pi \approx \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\), Corollary 1 reads \(\Gamma = \min\bigl(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}},\ I^\star/(\omega I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}})\bigr)\) as long as AR is memory-bound. \[ B^\star_{TD} = \frac{I^\star s_wW_{TD}}{2b\,N_{\mathrm{act}}},\qquad B^\star_{AR} = \frac{I^\star s_wW_{\mathrm{tot}}}{2N_{\mathrm{act}}},\qquad B_\times = \frac{B^\star_{AR}}{\omega}. \]

  • Both memory-bound, \(B \lt B^\star_{TD}\): \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\). The gain rises with the batch as \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) falls.
  • TD compute-bound, \(B^\star_{TD} \le B \lt B^\star_{AR}\): \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}(B))\).   • The best batch is \(B^\star_{TD}\), where the two expressions meet: \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B^\star_{TD})\), i.e. 6.5 at batch 6.4 under the propositions and 5.1 at batch 5 in the exact model. Since \(B^\star_{TD} \approx B^\star_{AR}/b\), a longer canvas moves it earlier and raises it.

4 The attention part

At long context the attention terms dominate: a pass costs what it reads of each sequence's KV cache. Three kinds of attention layer appear in current models: dense, sliding-window, and sparse in the style of DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA), summarised in the remark below. Table 4 gives, per sequence, what one query costs on each and what a canvas of \(b\) queries costs.

RemarkHow DSA works

For a query at position \(t\), with \(C\) cached tokens \(s\), a DSA layer does three things.

  1. Scan. A small indexer with \(H_I\) heads of dimension \(h_I\), in FP8, scores every cached token:
    \[ I_{t,s} = \sum_{j=1}^{H_I} w_{t,j}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(q^I_{t,j}\cdot k^I_s\bigr),\qquad q^I_{t,j} = W^I_{q,j}\,h_t,\quad k^I_s = W^I_k\,h_s, \]
    \[ \begin{gathered} I_{t,s} = \sum_{j=1}^{H_I} w_{t,j}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(q^I_{t,j}\cdot k^I_s\bigr),\\[4pt] q^I_{t,j} = W^I_{q,j}\,h_t,\qquad k^I_s = W^I_k\,h_s, \end{gathered} \]
    with query-dependent weights \(w_{t,j}\). The index keys \(k^I_s\) are cached, \(\nu_I = H_Ih_Is_I\) bytes per token, and the scan costs \(\alpha_I = 2H_Ih_I\) FLOPs per query and per cached token.
  2. Select. \(S_t = \) the \(m\) cached tokens with the largest \(I_{t,s}\) (\(m = 2048\) in DeepSeek-V3.2).
  3. Attend. Ordinary attention over the selected set, \(u_t = \mathrm{Attn}\bigl(q_t, \{k_s, v_s : s \in S_t\}\bigr)\): \(\alpha_S = 4n_q\sum h_\ell\) FLOPs and \(\nu_S\) KV bytes per selected token, the same constants as a dense layer, over \(m\) tokens instead of \(C\).

The indexer is trained to match the dense attention distribution (KL divergence, during a short dense warm-up and then during sparse training) and receives no gradient from the language-modelling loss. For the AR decoder the scan is the only attention cost that still grows with \(C\); for a canvas, the scan's cache is read once for all \(b\) queries, while each query's selected tokens are its own.

Proposition 5The penalty of a sparse layer

Under (A2), with independent selections, \(I^{TD}_{\mathrm{attn}} = b\,I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}/\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) with

\[ \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C) - 1 = \frac{\nu_S\,[\,U_{C,m}(b) - m\,]}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}(C)}. \]
  1. \(1 \le \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \le 1 + \dfrac{\nu_S\min(bm, C)}{\nu_I C + \nu_S m}\): the penalty comes only from the selected tokens, and is bounded by what the canvas can read of them beyond one query.
  2. Three stages of context, for a model whose attention is a sparse layer: \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = 1\) for \(C \le m\); \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \approx \dfrac{(\nu_I + \nu_S)\,C}{\nu_I C + \nu_S m}\) for \(m \lt C \lesssim bm\), when the canvas's selections cover the cache; \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \to 1\) as \(C \to \infty\), when the union stops growing at \(bm\) and the scan's bytes dominate.
  3. With a free scan (\(\alpha_I = \nu_I = 0\)) and no other attention layers, \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = \Pi_{\mathrm{sel}} = U_{C,m}(b)/m\), between 1 and \(\min(b, C/m)\), and non-decreasing in \(b\) and \(C\): the exact twin of the expert penalty \(\Pi_E = U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B)\), with the canvas length in the role of the batch size.
Proof

The identity is Definition 4 on the attention part: \(Q^{TD}_{\mathrm{attn}} - Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}} = \nu_S[U_{C,m}(b) - \min(C,m)]\), and \(\min(C,m) = m\) for \(C \ge m\). (1) \(m \le U_{C,m}(b) \le \min(bm, C)\), and \(Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}} \ge \nu_I C + \nu_S m\). (2) For \(C \le m\), \(U_{C,m}(b) = C = \min(C,m)\). For \(bm \gg C\), \((1-m/C)^b \approx e^{-bm/C} \to 0\), so \(U_{C,m}(b) \to C\). As \(C \to \infty\), \((1-m/C)^b \to 1 - bm/C\), so \(U_{C,m}(b) \to bm\), a constant, while \(\nu_I C\) grows. (3) \(U_{C,m}\) is increasing and concave in its argument with \(U_{C,m}(1) = m\), so \(U_{C,m}(b)/m \le b\), and \(U_{C,m}(b) \le C\); monotonicity in \(C\) is that of \(C[1 - (1-m/C)^b]\). \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 6When attention dominates

Under (A2), with the windows full (\(C \ge w\)):

  1. Attention carries at least a share \(x\) of an AR step's bytes exactly when \(C \ge C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, 1-x)\), the threshold of Proposition 4.
  2. Attention carries at least a share \(x\) of a position's FLOPs exactly when \(C \ge C_F\bigl(\tfrac{x}{1-x} - \sigma\bigr)\), with \(C_F = 2N_{\mathrm{act}}/\alpha_G\) and \(\sigma = \alpha_Ww/(2N_{\mathrm{act}})\); the same for AR and TD.
  3. As \(C\) grows, \(I_{AR}\) and \(I_{TD}\) move monotonically toward \(I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(I^{TD}_{\mathrm{attn}}\). With dense attention these are \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\); with sparse attention they are \(I_{\mathrm{idx}}\) and \(b\,I_{\mathrm{idx}}\), the index cache dominating the attention part once \(\nu_IC \gg \nu_S\,bm\).
Proof

(1) is Proposition 4 with the complementary share. (2) With dense attention \(F^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}/F^{AR} \ge x \iff (1-x)(\alpha_GC + \alpha_Ww) \ge 2xN_{\mathrm{act}}\). (3) For \(C \ge w\) each intensity is a ratio of affine functions of \(C\) with positive denominator once \(U_{C,m}\) has saturated at \(bm\), hence monotone, with the stated limits; in the sparse case the \(\nu_IC\) and \(\alpha_IC\) terms dominate. \(\blacksquare\)

\(C_F\) is the context at which global attention alone matches the linear FLOPs; for a dense model with attention in every layer and a 4× MLP it is about \(6d\). For DiffusionGemma \(C_F = 37.6\)k and \(\sigma = 6.8\%\). Attention FLOPs and attention bytes become the majority at very different contexts: a tenth of the FLOPs at 1.6k tokens, half at 35k; half of an AR step's bytes at 291k tokens for batch 1 and 27k for batch 64.

5 The whole picture

Proposition 2 makes each intensity a byte-weighted average of the two parts of §3 and §4. Putting them back into Proposition 1 gives the general answer.

Proposition 7When TD wins

Under (A1) to (A4), with \(I_{AR} = \theta_{\mathrm{lin}}I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}} + \theta_{\mathrm{attn}}I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(\Pi - 1 = \theta_{\mathrm{lin}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1) + \theta_{\mathrm{attn}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} - 1)\) (shares of an AR step's bytes):

  1. If AR is compute-bound, \(I_{AR} \ge I^\star\), TD loses: \(\Gamma \le 1/\omega\).
  2. Otherwise \(\Gamma \ge 1\) exactly when both \(\mathrm{TPF} \ge \Pi\) and \(I_{AR} \le I^\star/\omega\): a TD pass must serve more tokens per read than it pays in penalty, and AR must use at most a fraction \(1/\omega\) of the compute, so that TD's \(\omega\) times more FLOPs fit in what AR leaves idle.
  3. The gain is \(\Gamma = \min\bigl(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi,\ I^\star/(\omega I_{AR})\bigr)\), largest where the two are equal, at the batch where a TD pass reaches the ridge.
Proof

(1) and (3) are Corollary 1 with Proposition 2's expressions. (2) A minimum of two numbers is at least 1 exactly when both are. \(\blacksquare\)

Read with §3 and §4: at short context \(\Pi = \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and \(I_{AR} = I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\), so the first condition is a lower bound on the batch (the MoE penalty must fall below TPF) and the second an upper bound (\(B_\times\)); at long context \(\Pi = \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(I_{AR} = I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}\), and neither depends on the batch. For DiffusionGemma on an H200, both conditions hold at every batch up to 109 at short context, and at every batch at long context.

Proposition 8The batch needed to saturate compute

Let \(B^\star_n(C)\) be the smallest batch at which a pass with \(n\) queries per sequence (\(n = 1\) for AR, \(n = b\) for TD) is compute-bound, or \(\infty\) if there is none, with dense attention and the windows full (\(C \ge w\)).

  1. If \(n\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \lt I^\star\), then \(B^\star_n(C)\) is non-decreasing in \(C\), and infinite beyond some finite context.
  2. If \(n\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \gt I^\star\), then \(B^\star_n(C)\) is non-increasing in \(C\), and equal to 1 beyond some finite context.

AR is in the first case whenever \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} \lt I^\star\), which holds for every model and accelerator here. TD is in the second case exactly when \(b \gt T_c = I^\star/I_{\mathrm{attn}}\).

Proof

Write \(D_n(C) = n\,F^{AR}(C) - I^\star Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}(C)\). Multiplying out \(I_n \ge I^\star\) gives \(B\,D_n(C) \ge I^\star s_wW_n(B)\): each sequence contributes \(nF^{AR}\) FLOPs, of which \(I^\star Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}\) are needed to make its own cache reads compute-bound, and what is left, summed over the batch, has to cover the weights. The right side is positive, so no batch works when \(D_n \le 0\). For \(C \ge w\), \(D_n\) is affine in \(C\) with slope \((nI_{\mathrm{attn}} - I^\star)\,\nu_G\). A smaller \(D_n\) can only remove batches from the feasible set, so a negative slope makes \(B^\star_n\) non-decreasing and eventually infinite; a positive slope makes the set grow until batch 1 works, once \(D_n \ge I^\star s_wW_n(1)\). Nothing needs a closed form for \(W_n(B)\), so the argument covers MoE models. \(\blacksquare\)

The canvas does for the KV cache what batch does for the weights.

A larger batch does not help with cache bytes, because every sequence brings its own cache. AR uses each cache byte for one query, so at long context its intensity drifts toward \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\), far below the ridge, whatever the batch. A canvas uses each cache byte for \(b\) queries, and once \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \gt I^\star\) a longer context makes compute easier to saturate, not harder. When \(W_n\) does not depend on \(B\), solving the inequality gives \(B^\star_n(C) = \lceil I^\star s_wW_n/D_n(C)\rceil\) for \(D_n \gt 0\), which at zero context is the \(B^\star_{AR}\) and \(B^\star_{TD}\) of §3.

Here \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 8\) and \(T_c = 206/8 \approx 26\). AR needs a batch of 1,640 at zero context, and past 135 tokens no batch is enough. TD with \(b = 256\) needs 6.4 at zero context, and batch 1 is enough from about 224k tokens. TD with \(b = 32\) first needs a slightly larger batch while the windows fill (\(b\,I_{\mathrm{win}} = 64 \lt I^\star\)), then a smaller one once global attention dominates (\(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 256 \gt I^\star\)). TD with \(b = 16\) is below \(T_c\) and behaves like AR. The grey region is where the weights and the batch's cache exceed the H200's 141 GB: at 1k tokens about 390 sequences fit, far below the 1,640 that AR would need, so for DiffusionGemma on an H200 AR decoding is memory-bound at every batch that fits.

RemarkKernels run one after another

For an AR step at moderate batch every class is memory-bound and nothing changes. 7.1 A smaller canvas

  
Keep DiffusionGemma, the H200, \(S = 14\) and \(r = 1\) (so \(\omega = 15\)), but shrink the canvas from 256 to \(b = 32\). The exact map below changes completely. Predict each feature from the propositions before opening its answer; every answer says where to look on the map.

  
  
    
    
  1. Batch 1, short context. Compute TPF and \(\Pi(1)\). Who wins?
    
      
    Answer
        
    \(\mathrm{TPF} = 32/15 = 2.13\). A 32-token canvas touches \(U(32) = 128\,[1 - (15/16)^{32}] = 112\) experts per layer, so \(W_{TD}(1) = 1.66 + 30 \times 112 \times 0.00595 = 21.6\)B against \(W_{AR}(1) = 3.08\)B: \(\Pi(1) = 7.0\). Both are memory-bound (R1), so \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi = 0.30\): AR wins by about 3×. The penalty is barely smaller than at \(b = 256\), but TPF has shrunk eightfold. On the map: the blue bottom-left corner.
    2. 
    
  2. Where does TD overtake AR? Find the batch at which \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi(B) = 1\).
    
      
    Answer
        
    From batch ≈ 4 the canvas touches every expert, so \(W_{TD} \approx W_{\mathrm{tot}} = 24.5\)B. Parity needs \(W_{AR}(B) = W_{\mathrm{tot}}/\mathrm{TPF} = 11.5\)B, i.e. \(U(B) = 55\), i.e. \(1 - (15/16)^B = 0.43\): \(B \approx 8.7\) (exact: 8.3). On the map: the lower dashed line, \(\Gamma = 1\).
    3. 
    
  3. Where is TD's best batch, and how large is the gain there?
    
      
    Answer
        
    \(B^\star_{TD} = I^\star s_wW_{TD}/(2bN_{\mathrm{act}}) = 206 \times 49.0\,\text{GB}/(2 \times 32 \times 3.08\text{B}) \approx 51\): eight times later than at \(b = 256\), since \(B^\star_{TD} \propto 1/b\). There \(\Pi(51) = 1.04\), so the peak is \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi \approx 2.1\). The exact model gives 1.8 at batch 45: its vocabulary matmuls add to TD's FLOPs, so TD saturates a little earlier and lower. On the map: the solid line labelled "TD compute-bound above" and the darkest orange just above it.
    4. 
    
  4. When does AR catch up again?
    
      
    Answer
        
    In R2 the canvas drops out: \(B_\times = B^\star_{AR}/\omega = 1640/15 \approx 109\), as with \(b = 256\). AR is still memory-bound there; it only needs \(I_{AR} \gt I^\star/\omega \approx 14\). On the map: the upper dashed line and the blue top-left.
    5. 
    
  5. Long context. Which regime, and what gain? What changes at \(b = 16\)?
    
      
    Answer
        
    \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 256 \gt 206\): TD stays compute-bound, R6, \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{attn}}) = 1.72\), independent of \(b\). At \(b = 16\), \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 128 \lt 206\): TD stays memory-bound (R5) and \(\Gamma \to \mathrm{TPF} = 16/15 = 1.07\). On the map: the colour on the right edge, about 1.7 at every batch.

7.2 A dense model

  
Now take a dense model, Llama 3.1 8B, on the same H200 with the same canvas (\(b = 256\), \(S = 14\), \(r = 1\)), and pair it with a TD twin that has no self-conditioning. A dense model has no MoE penalty, \(\Pi = 1\), and in BF16 its linear intensity is simply the batch: \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) = 2BN/(2N) = B\).

  
  
    
    
  1. Batch 1, short context. Which regime, and what gain?
    
      
    Answer
        
    \(b\,I_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) = 256\) is above \(I^\star = 206\), so a TD pass is already compute-bound at batch 1, while AR runs at intensity 1. That is R2: \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{lin}}(1)) = 206/15 = 13.7\) (exact: 13.3). Nothing is lost to \(\Pi\), which is why dense models have the largest batch-1 gains in Table 9. On the map: the dark orange bottom row.
    2. 
    
  2. Where does AR catch up?
    
      
    Answer
        
    In R2, \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega B)\), so \(\Gamma = 1\) at \(B_\times = I^\star/\omega \approx 14\) (exact: 15). TD is compute-bound from batch 1, so batch 1 is also its best batch, and the gain only falls from there. On the map: the dashed line \(\Gamma = 1\). It starts near batch 15 at short context and turns sharply upward around 16k tokens, because at long context TD wins at every batch (next question).
    3. 
    
  3. Does AR ever become compute-bound at 1k context?
    
      
    Answer
        
    Without a cache it would at \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) = I^\star\), batch 206. But Llama's cache holds 128 KiB per token (32 layers, 8 KV heads, head dimension 128, K and V, 2 bytes), and with \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 4\) Proposition 4 says no batch makes an AR step compute-bound once the context exceeds about 530 tokens. So AR stays memory-bound, and the gain keeps falling instead: 0.17 at batch 256.
    4. 
    
  4. Long context. What gain, and which contexts actually fit?
    
      
    Answer
        
    \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 1024 \gt I^\star\), so this is R6 with \(\Gamma = 206/(15 \times 4) = 3.4\), twice DiffusionGemma's 1.7 because Llama has half as many query heads per KV head. Memory decides which of these contexts exist: next to 16 GB of weights, one sequence fits up to about 950k tokens and a batch of 64 only up to 14k. At batch 1 and 930k tokens the exact gain is 3.7, a little above the limit because the weights are still 12% of AR's bytes and hold its intensity below 4. On the map: the large grey region in the top right.
    5. 
  


  
7.3 A low-ridge accelerator

  
Back to DiffusionGemma with \(b = 256\) and \(S = 14\), but on an H20: 148 TFLOP/s of BF16, 4.0 TB/s and 96 GB. Its ridge point is \(I^\star = 148/4.0 = 37\), against 206 on the H200.

  
  
    
    
  1. Batch 1, short context. Where is TD on the roofline, and what gain?
    
      
    Answer
        
    \(B^\star_{TD} = I^\star s_wW_{TD}/(2bN_{\mathrm{act}}) = 37 \times 49.0\,\text{GB}/(2 \times 256 \times 3.08\text{B}) = 1.15\): a TD pass sits just below the ridge at batch 1. Corollary 1 gives \(\Gamma = \min(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi,\ I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{lin}})) = \min(2.15,\ 2.47) = 2.15\). The exact gain is 2.03, with TD already compute-bound because the vocabulary adds to its FLOPs.
    2. 
    
  2. Where does AR catch up?
    
      
    Answer
        
    In R2, \(\Gamma = 1\) when \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) = I^\star/\omega = 2.47\). The closed form \(B_\times = B^\star_{AR}/\omega = 19.6\) does not apply here, because at such small batches AR does not yet touch every expert. Solving with the full \(W_{AR}(B)\) gives \(B \approx 10\). The exact parity is at 4.5: once TD is compute-bound its vocabulary FLOPs count in full and lower its gain by about a quarter, so parity needs only \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) \approx 2.47 \times 0.74 \approx 1.8\), which happens near batch 4. On the map: the dashed line, close to the bottom.
    3. 
    
  3. Long context. Who wins?
    
      
    Answer
        
    \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 8 \lt 37 \lt b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\), so this is still R6, but now \(\Gamma = 37/(15 \times 8) = 0.31\). AR wins at long context, at every batch. With such a low ridge, AR's attention already runs at \(8/37\), about a fifth of peak, while TD runs at peak but does 15 times the work. The exact gain is 0.62 at batch 1 and 256k tokens, and 0.35 at 2.2M tokens, the longest single sequence that fits next to the weights. On the map: the blue right half.

A Appendix

  
Exact accounting

  
The maps, headline numbers and calculator do not use Proposition 1. They evaluate every pass of a generation: AR prefill and \(G-1\) decode steps; TD encoder prefill, then per canvas \(S\) bidirectional denoising passes and one causal commit. Each pass counts:

  
    
    
  • FLOPs: linear layers; exact (query, key) pairs under causal, bidirectional and sliding-window masks, in-canvas keys included; the LM head on every logit position; for DiffusionGemma, the self-conditioning soft embedding and MLP.
    • 
    
  • Bytes: weights with \(U(N)\) experts; the vocabulary matrix once per use; KV reads and writes; activations under a fused kernel schedule (or none, when on-chip); logits and probabilities, \(\lambda\) times per output position.
    • 
    
  • Decoder ratio: a denoising pass multiplies every per-layer term (FLOPs, weights, KV reads, activations) by \(r\); its vocabulary and self-conditioning terms are unchanged. For \(r \gt 1\) the memory footprint adds \((r-1)L\) layers of weights.
    • 
    
  • Time: the per-pass roofline, \(\max(F/\pi, Q/\beta)\), or, under the calculator's kernel-by-kernel rule, the sum of that roofline over the kernel classes (dense layers, experts, global, window and sparse attention, indexer, vocabulary head). The class sums equal the totals exactly.
    • 
  

  
The closed forms for masked key counts match brute-force counting. Proposition 1 matches this accounting to within 1.2% without vocabulary (Table 3). Model shapes come from each checkpoint's config.json; for DiffusionGemma also from its safetensors headers (25.25B text parameters, 3.08B active per token plus a 0.74B head).

  
Sanity check against measurement

  
dgbench is our harness for timing DiffusionGemma generation in vLLM. Its runs (vLLM 0.28.0, one H200, BF16, \(S = 14\)) measured 1,295 tok/s per request at batch 1 and 2,160 tok/s at batch 64. The exact bounds are 1,535 and 6,440 tok/s: 84% and 34% of the roofline. At batch 64 the denoising pass is compute-bound; the gap is matmul efficiency (128 small experts) and vocabulary-sized sampling work, which a pass-level roofline overlaps with compute and a real sampler does not.
