Block Diffusion Rooflines

when block text diffusion beats autoregressive decoding · inference cost model

Block text diffusion (TD) writes a block of tokens at once, but runs the model many times to do it. Autoregressive decoding (AR) runs the model once per token. This note works out, with a roofline model, when TD produces more tokens per second than AR for the same model on the same accelerator. The running example is DiffusionGemma 26B-A4B and its autoregressive twin, which runs the same weights one token at a time.

Before reading on

Which decoder does the roofline model make faster?

Six settings of model, accelerator, batch and context. For each, decide whether block diffusion (TD) or autoregressive decoding (AR) has the higher throughput under the roofline model. Each answer comes with the roofline that decides it, and the rest of the page explains why.

\[ \Gamma \;=\; \frac{1}{\omega}\cdot\frac{\min\bigl(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}},\,I^\star\bigr)}{\min\bigl(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}},\,I^\star\bigr)},\qquad \omega = S\,r + 1 \]

\(\Gamma\) is the multiplicative throughput gain of TD over AR. \(\omega = Sr + 1\) is the cost of one canvas in forward passes: \(S\) denoising passes at \(r\) forward passes each, plus one commit pass. \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}\) are the arithmetic intensities of an AR step and of a TD pass, in FLOPs per byte read from memory. \(I^\star\) is the accelerator's ridge point, its peak FLOP/s divided by its memory bandwidth.

How the numbers are counted

Every gain on this page is a ratio of roofline bounds, counted in one of three ways.

A roofline time is a lower bound on the time of a pass, so each throughput is an upper bound, and \(\Gamma\) is the ratio of two such bounds, not a measurement. In vLLM, TD reaches 84% of its bound at batch 1 and 34% at batch 64 (appendix). The three accountings below differ in what they include, and each number on the page says which one it uses. Four things to remember
  1. TD trades work for reuse. A canvas of \(b\) tokens costs \(\omega\) forward passes instead of one, so TD does \(\omega\) times more FLOPs per token than AR. In return, every byte it reads from memory serves \(b\) positions instead of one. Try it in Figure 1
  2. The best batch is the one at which a TD pass just becomes compute-bound. At smaller batches TD is still memory-bound, and the gain grows as a larger batch dilutes the MoE penalty. At larger batches TD already runs at peak while AR keeps speeding up, so the gain falls like \(1/B\). Try it in Figure 3
  3. At long context the batch stops mattering. Both decoders are then reading the KV cache, which belongs to one sequence, so no batch can share it. What still separates them is when they become compute-bound: an AR step reads the cache for one query and never reaches the ridge, while a TD pass reads it once for \(b\) queries and does. Try it in Figure 4
  4. A higher ridge point means more to gain. Whenever TD is compute-bound and AR is not, the gain scales with \(I^\star\), because \(I^\star\) measures the compute AR leaves idle. The price is that TD needs more to reach the ridge: a larger batch at short context, and a canvas longer than \(I^\star/I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) at long context. On a DGX Spark, 273 GB/s against a model that reads 3 GB per token caps AR at 90 tokens per second and 0.1% of the chip's compute; the other 99.9% is what TD spends. Try it in Figure 6

What drives the gain

Canvas cost, \(\omega\)

A canvas costs \(\omega = Sr+1\) forward passes. This divides the gain in every regime.

\[ \begin{gathered} \omega = S\,r + 1,\\[4pt] \Gamma = \frac{1}{\omega}\cdot\frac{\min(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}},\,I^\star)}{\min(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}},\,I^\star)}. \end{gathered} \]

\(S\) denoising passes, each costing \(r\) forward passes (\(r \lt 1\) for a smaller denoiser), and one commit pass that writes the canvas to the KV cache. The intensity of a pass does not depend on \(S\), so under Proposition 1 the gain is exactly proportional to \(1/\omega\): halving \(S\) from 14 to 7 takes \(\omega\) from 15 to 8 and multiplies \(\Gamma\) by \(15/8\) in every regime, whatever it costs in quality.

Proposition 1 · Figure 1

Canvas length, \(b\)

Every byte a TD pass reads serves \(b\) positions. While TD is memory-bound this sets the gain, through \(\mathrm{TPF} = b/\omega\). It also sets the batch at which TD becomes compute-bound, which scales like \(1/b\).

\[ \begin{gathered} \mathrm{TPF} = \frac{b}{\omega},\qquad \class{mj-td}{I_{TD}} = \frac{b}{\Pi}\,\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}},\\[4pt] \class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}} \approx \frac{I^\star s_w\class{mj-td}{W_{TD}}}{2b\,N_{\mathrm{act}}}. \end{gathered} \]

While a TD pass is memory-bound, \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi\) grows with \(b\). Once it is compute-bound, \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}})\) no longer depends on \(b\): a longer canvas reaches that regime at a smaller batch, and so raises the best gain, which sits at \(\class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}}\) or one batch below it.

The gain when the linear layers dominate · Figure 3

Linear penalty, \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\)

A canvas of 256 positions routes to almost every expert, while an AR step at batch \(B\) reaches about \(kB\). At batch 1 the penalty is close to total over active parameters (7.9 for DiffusionGemma); it falls to 1 as the batch grows.

\[ \begin{gathered} \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) = \frac{\class{mj-td}{W_{TD}}(B)}{\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B)},\\[4pt] \class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B) = W_{\mathrm{ne}} + L\,U_{E,k}(B)\,W^e,\\[2pt] \class{mj-td}{W_{TD}}(B) = W_{\mathrm{ne}} + L\,U_{E,k}(Bb)\,W^e,\\[4pt] U_{E,k}(N) = E\Bigl[1 - \bigl(1 - \tfrac{k}{E}\bigr)^{N}\Bigr]. \end{gathered} \]

\(U_{E,k}(N)\) is the expected number of distinct experts that \(N\) positions touch in one layer. A canvas routes \(Bb\) positions and touches almost all \(E = 128\); an AR step routes \(B\) and touches about \(kB\), with \(k = 8\). The dense part \(W_{\mathrm{ne}} = 1.66\)B is read by both and dilutes the ratio from \(E/k = 16\) to 7.9. Dense models have \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} = 1\).

Proposition 3 · Definition 4

Attention penalty, \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\)

1 for dense and windowed attention: the canvas reads the cache once for \(b\) queries. Above 1 for sparse attention, where each query selects its own tokens and the canvas reads the union. Same formula as the linear penalty, with the canvas size in the role of the batch size.

\[ \begin{gathered} \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C) - 1 = \frac{\nu_S\,\bigl[\,U_{C,m}(b) - m\,\bigr]}{\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C)},\\[4pt] U_{C,m}(b) = C\Bigl[1 - \bigl(1 - \tfrac{m}{C}\bigr)^{b}\Bigr]. \end{gathered} \]

Only sparse layers contribute: each query selects its own \(m\) cached tokens, and the canvas reads the union, \(U_{C,m}(b)\) tokens, up to \(\min(bm, C)\). This is the MoE penalty again, with cached tokens in the role of experts and the canvas length in the role of the batch. It is 1 while \(C \le m\), largest for \(m \lt C \lesssim bm\), and returns to 1 as \(C \to \infty\), when the union stops growing and the indexer's scan carries the bytes.

Proposition 5 · sparse attention

Batch, \(B\)

The weights are shared by the whole batch, so a larger batch raises AR's intensity. It helps TD first, by shrinking \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\), and AR after that. It does nothing for the cache, which is per sequence.

\[ Q^{X}(B, C) = \underbrace{\frac{s_wW_X(B)}{B}}_{\text{weights}} + \underbrace{Q^{X}_{\mathrm{attn}}(C)}_{\text{cache}}. \]

For \(X \in \{AR, TD\}\), the weights are shared by the batch and each sequence brings its own cache. A larger batch divides the weight bytes per sequence and leaves the cache bytes unchanged, so it raises both intensities at short context. At first it helps TD more, because \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) falls; once a TD pass is compute-bound it only raises \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\), and \(\Gamma = I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}})\) falls, down to parity near batch 109.

Proposition 4 · Figure 3

Context, \(C\)

As the context grows, attention bytes overtake the weights, and the intensities move from their linear values to their attention values: \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \to I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}} \to b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}/\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\).

\[ \begin{gathered} I_X = \theta^X_{\mathrm{lin}}\,I^X_{\mathrm{lin}} + \theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}}\,I^X_{\mathrm{attn}},\\[4pt] \theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}} = \frac{Q^X_{\mathrm{attn}}}{Q^X}. \end{gathered} \]

Each intensity is the byte-weighted average of its two parts (Proposition 2). As \(C\) grows, the cache's share \(\theta_{\mathrm{attn}}\) goes to 1 and the intensities go to their attention values: \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \to I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 8\) on DiffusionGemma's global layers, far below every ridge, and \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}} \to b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 2{,}048\), above every ridge in this note.

Proposition 2 · Figure 4

Three regimes matter

AR compute-bound
\(\Gamma = 1/\omega\)

TD is then compute-bound as well. Both run at peak, and TD does \(\omega\) times the work. Rare for MoE decoding: DiffusionGemma on an H200 would need a batch of about 1,600.

Both passes run at the peak, so time is FLOPs, and a canvas spends \(\omega\) times AR's FLOPs per token: \(\Gamma \le 1/\omega\), equal to it under Proposition 1. AR reaches the ridge at

\[ \begin{gathered} \class{mj-ar}{B^\star_{AR}} = \frac{I^\star s_wW_{\mathrm{tot}}}{2N_{\mathrm{act}}}\\[4pt] = \frac{206 \times 2 \times 24.5\text{B}}{2 \times 3.08\text{B}} \approx 1{,}640, \end{gathered} \]

This closed form takes zero context. Holding 1,640 sequences of 9k tokens, the 1k prompt and 8k generated tokens of the running example, would take about 650 GB of KV cache, more than four H200s hold; and by Proposition 8, at a long enough context no batch makes an AR step compute-bound.

Corollary 1 · Proposition 8

Both memory-bound
\(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi\)

TD's extra FLOPs cost nothing. Each read serves TPF tokens, minus the penalty of whichever bytes dominate: 17.1 / 7.9 ≈ 2.2 at batch 1.

Time is bytes. Per sequence, an AR step reads \(\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}}\) for one token; a canvas reads \(\omega\) times \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{TD}} = \Pi\,\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}}\) for \(b\) tokens. Hence

\[ \Gamma = \frac{\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}}}{\omega\,\Pi\,\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}}/b} = \frac{\mathrm{TPF}}{\Pi}. \]

TD's extra FLOPs are hidden under the memory time. At batch 1, \(\mathrm{TPF} = 256/15 = 17.1\) and \(\Pi \approx \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) = 7.9\), so \(\Gamma \approx 2.2\); the exact accounting gives 2.53.

Corollary 1 · Figure 1

AR memory-, TD compute-bound
\(\Gamma = \dfrac{I^\star}{\omega\,\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}}\)

TD uses the compute that AR leaves idle. At short context this falls like \(1/B\); at long context it tends to \(I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{attn}})\), 1.72 on an H200, at any batch.

TD runs at the peak \(\pi\) and AR at \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\,\beta\), so TD's FLOPs run \(I^\star/\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\) times faster and it does \(\omega\) times as many. The gain is the compute that AR leaves idle, divided by TD's extra work. At short context \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\) grows with the batch and \(\Gamma\) reaches 1 near batch 109. At long context \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \to I_{\mathrm{attn}}\), and at any batch

\[ \Gamma \to \frac{I^\star}{\omega\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \frac{206}{15 \times 8} = 1.72. \]

Proposition 7 · Figure 6

While AR is memory-bound, \(\Gamma = \min\bigl(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi,\ I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}})\bigr)\). The first term grows with the batch and the second shrinks, which is why the best batch is where TD turns compute-bound.

What the formulas leave out

  • Vocabulary and self-conditioning. Per token, TD runs \(2S-1\) vocabulary-sized matmuls against AR's one, and a smaller decoder does not shrink them. The maps and the calculator include them.
  • Kernels run one after another. Assumption (A1) gives a pass the time of a single kernel in which all of its compute and all of its memory traffic overlap. Adding up the roofline times of its kernels instead is never faster and at most twice slower, and it moves DiffusionGemma's best batch from 5 to 9 (§5). The calculator offers both.
  • Small effects. Activations (they stay on-chip once kernels are fused), KV writes, and the at most \(b\) keys inside the canvas.
  • Cross-layer KV sharing. It divides the KV cache's size, not the bytes read per pass, since each layer still reads the shared cache. It changes the largest batch that fits in memory, not any intensity.
  • Beyond the roofline. Kernel efficiency (vLLM reaches 84% of the bound at batch 1 and 34% at batch 64), adaptive step counts, uneven expert routing, correlated block selections, the wait for the first canvas, and quality per step.

1 Setup

One forward pass advances a batch of \(B\) sequences, each with \(C\) tokens in its KV cache: by one token for AR, by a canvas of \(b\) tokens for TD, which takes \(S\) denoising passes and one commit pass.

Figure 1 · interactive

TD does more work per token, but reads each byte for more tokens.

Each slot is one forward pass, and every pass reads the weights once. Press play, change the canvas length \(b\) and the number of denoising passes \(S\), then switch the model from dense to MoE.

One sequence, batch 1, short context, and both decoders assumed memory-bound, so that a pass takes the time to read its weights; this fails for a dense model once the canvas is longer than about \(I^\star\) positions, where Corollary 1 caps the gain. A canvas costs \(\omega = S + 1\) passes (here \(r = 1\)) and commits \(b\) tokens; in the same \(\omega\) passes AR commits \(\omega\) tokens. For a dense model both passes read the same weights, and per committed token TD reads \(\omega/b = 1/\mathrm{TPF}\) times the bytes of AR: \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}\). For an MoE model a canvas touches more experts than one token does, a TD pass reads \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) times the weights of an AR step (the two bars, Proposition 3), and \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\).

Definition 4Selected resources and the penalties

In short. A penalty is how many more bytes per sequence a TD pass reads than an AR step: \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) from the experts its \(b\) positions touch, \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) from the cached tokens that sparse layers select.

Two resources are selected by each position rather than read in full. A pass reads the union of its positions' selections, \(U_{n,k}(N)\) units for \(N\) independent selections of \(k\) out of \(n\). The penalty of a resource is the ratio of what a TD pass reads to what an AR step reads.

2 The gain

Proposition 1TD/AR throughput gain

In short. The gain is the ratio of the two decoders' attainable FLOP rates, divided by the \(\omega\) passes a canvas costs.

A1 · Roofline
A pass with \(F\) FLOPs and \(Q\) HBM bytes takes \(\max(F/\pi,\,Q/\beta)\).
A2 · Costs
Per sequence, an AR step costs \(\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}\) FLOPs and \(\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}}\) bytes, and a TD pass \(\class{mj-td}{F^{TD}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{TD}}\), as in Definition 2. Vocabulary, activations, KV writes and in-canvas keys are neglected. Expert routing and token selection are independent across positions.
A3 · Canvas
A canvas commits \(b\) tokens per sequence after \(S\) denoising passes and one commit pass. A denoising pass costs \(r\) times a commit pass, in FLOPs and in bytes.
A4 · Lockstep
\(B\) sequences share every pass, at a context \(C\) held fixed over a canvas.

Then TD's throughput over AR's is

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}\Gamma = \frac{1}{\omega}\cdot\frac{\min(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}},\,I^\star)}{\min(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}},\,I^\star)},\\[4pt] \omega = S\,r+1,\qquad \class{mj-td}{I_{TD}} = \frac{b}{\Pi}\,\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}.\end{gathered}} \]
Proof
  1. For a pass with \(I = F/Q\): \(\max(F/\pi,\,Q/\beta) = \dfrac{F}{\beta}\max\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{I^\star},\dfrac{1}{I}\Bigr) = \dfrac{F}{\beta\,\min(I, I^\star)}\).
  2. AR. A step costs \(B\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}\) FLOPs at intensity \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\) and yields \(B\) tokens: \(\class{mj-ar}{t_{AR}} = \class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}\,/\,\bigl(\beta\min(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}, I^\star)\bigr)\) per token.
  3. TD. A commit pass costs \(B\class{mj-td}{F^{TD}} = Bb\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}\) FLOPs at intensity \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}\). A denoising pass multiplies both \(\class{mj-td}{F^{TD}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{TD}}\) by \(r\), so it has the same intensity and takes \(r\) times as long. A canvas therefore takes \(\omega\) times one commit pass and yields \(Bb\) tokens: \(\class{mj-td}{t_{TD}} = \omega \class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}\,/\,\bigl(\beta\min(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}, I^\star)\bigr)\) per token.
  4. \(\Gamma = \class{mj-ar}{t_{AR}}/\class{mj-td}{t_{TD}}\), and \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}} = \class{mj-td}{F^{TD}}/\class{mj-td}{Q^{TD}} = b\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}/(\Pi \class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}}) = b\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}/\Pi\) by Definition 4. \(\blacksquare\)

Corollary 1Which side wins

In short. If AR is compute-bound TD loses; otherwise the gain is the smaller of \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi\), set by bytes, and \(I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}})\), set by the compute AR leaves idle.

If AR is compute-bound (\(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \ge I^\star\)), then \(\Gamma \le 1/\omega\). Otherwise

\[ \Gamma = \min\Bigl(\frac{\mathrm{TPF}}{\Pi},\ \frac{I^\star}{\omega\,\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}}\Bigr),\quad \mathrm{TPF} = \frac{b}{\omega}. \]
Proof

If \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \ge I^\star\) the denominator of Proposition 1 is \(I^\star\) and the numerator at most \(I^\star\). Otherwise \(\min(b\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}/\Pi,\,I^\star)/\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} = \min(b/\Pi,\,I^\star/\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}})\); divide by \(\omega\). \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 2Each intensity is a byte-weighted average of its linear and attention parts

In short. Each intensity is the byte-weighted average of its linear and attention parts, so it lies between them, nearer to the part that carries more of the bytes.

Under (A2), for \(X \in \{AR, TD\}\), let \(\theta^X_{\mathrm{lin}} = Q^X_{\mathrm{lin}}/Q^X\) and \(\theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}} = Q^X_{\mathrm{attn}}/Q^X\) be the shares of the two parts in the bytes of Definition 2. Then

\[ I_X = \theta^X_{\mathrm{lin}}\,I^X_{\mathrm{lin}} + \theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}}\,I^X_{\mathrm{attn}},\qquad \Pi - 1 = \class{mj-ar}{\theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1) + \class{mj-ar}{\theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} - 1). \]
\[ \begin{gathered} I_X = \theta^X_{\mathrm{lin}}\,I^X_{\mathrm{lin}} + \theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}}\,I^X_{\mathrm{attn}},\\[4pt] \Pi - 1 = \class{mj-ar}{\theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1)\\ \qquad + \class{mj-ar}{\theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} - 1). \end{gathered} \]

Consequently each intensity lies between its two parts, and \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}/\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\) lies between \(b/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and \(b/\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\), nearer to whichever part carries more of the bytes.

Proof

\(I_X = F^X/Q^X = \sum_c (Q^X_c/Q^X)(F^X_c/Q^X_c)\) over the two parts \(c\). For the penalty, \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{TD}_c} = \Pi_c\,\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_c}\) by Definition 4, so \(\Pi - 1 = \sum_c (\class{mj-td}{Q^{TD}_c} - \class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_c})/\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}} = \sum_c \class{mj-ar}{\theta^{AR}_c}(\Pi_c - 1)\). A weighted average with positive weights lies between its terms; \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}} = (b/\Pi)\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\) and \(\Pi\) lies between \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\). \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 3The MoE penalty

In short. A canvas touches almost every expert, so at batch 1 TD streams the total parameters where AR streams the active ones (a penalty of 7.9), and the penalty vanishes once the batch alone touches every expert.

Under (A2), \(\class{mj-td}{I^{TD}_{\mathrm{lin}}} = b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\), with \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1 = \theta_E\,(\Pi_E - 1)\), where \(\theta_E = L\,U_{E,k}(B)\,W^e/\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B)\) is the expert share of the weights an AR step reads and \(\Pi_E = U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B)\). Moreover:

  1. \(1 \le \Pi_E \le \min(b,\ E/k)\), and \(\Pi_E\) is non-increasing in \(B\): \(\Pi_E - 1 \le (E/k)\,e^{-kB/E}\), so the penalty disappears at large batch.
  2. At \(b \gg E/k\), a canvas touches every expert and \(\Pi_E \approx E/U_{E,k}(B)\), which at batch 1 is \(E/k\); then \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) \approx W_{\mathrm{tot}}/N_{\mathrm{act}}\), total over active parameters.
  3. For a dense model (\(k = E\)), \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} \equiv 1\).
Proof

The first identity is Definition 4 restricted to the linear part; the split of \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1\) is Proposition 2's argument applied to the dense and expert parts of the weights, the dense part having penalty 1. (1) \(U_{E,k}\) is increasing and concave with \(U_{E,k}(0) = 0\) and \(U_{E,k}(N) \le \min(kN, E)\), so \(U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B) \le b\) by concavity and \(\le E/U_{E,k}(B) \le E/k\); and \(U_{E,k}(Bb) - U_{E,k}(B) \le E(1-k/E)^B \le Ee^{-kB/E}\) with \(U_{E,k}(B) \ge k\). (2) \(U_{E,k}(Bb) \ge E(1 - e^{-kb/E}) \approx E\), and \(U_{E,k}(1) = k\); \(\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(1) = W_{\mathrm{ne}} + LkW^e = N_{\mathrm{act}}\). (3) \(U_{E,k}(N) = E\) for all \(N \ge 1\). \(\blacksquare\)

For DiffusionGemma, \(E/k = 16\) and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(1) = 24.5/3.08 = 7.9\): a canvas streams all 24.5B parameters where a token streams the 3.08B it activates, and the dense 1.66B, shared by both, dilute the ratio from 16 to 7.9. By batch 48 (three times \(E/k\)) an AR step touches every expert too, and the penalty is gone.

Proposition 4When the linear layers dominate

In short. With dense attention and full windows, the weights dominate an AR step's bytes up to a context that shrinks as the batch grows, like \(1/B\) once the batch touches every expert, because the batch shares the weights but each sequence brings its own cache.

Under (A2), with the windows full (\(C \ge w\)) and dense attention, an AR step spends at least a share \(x\) of its bytes on weights exactly when

\[ C \;\le\; C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, x) = \frac{1}{\nu_G}\Bigl(\frac{1-x}{x}\cdot\frac{s_w\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B)}{B} - \nu_W\,w\Bigr). \]
\[ \begin{gathered} C \le C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, x)\\[2pt] = \frac{1}{\nu_G}\Bigl(\frac{1-x}{x}\,\frac{s_w\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B)}{B} - \nu_W w\Bigr). \end{gathered} \]

Moreover:

  1. \(C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B,x)\) is non-increasing in \(B\): the weights are shared across the batch, the cache is not. Once the batch touches every expert (\(\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B) = W_{\mathrm{tot}}\)), and for dense models at every batch, it falls exactly like \(1/B\); below that it falls more slowly, since \(\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B)\) still grows with \(B\).
  2. For a TD pass, replace \(\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}\) by \(\class{mj-td}{W_{TD}}\): its threshold is \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}(B)\) times further out, up to the window term.
  3. With sparse layers, replace \(\nu_G C\) by \(\nu_S\min(C,m) + \nu_I C\) for an AR step and by \(\nu_S U_{C,m}(b) + \nu_I C\) for a TD pass.
Proof

\(\class{mj-ar}{\theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}} \ge x \iff s_w\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}/B \ge \tfrac{x}{1-x}\,(\nu_GC + \nu_Ww)\); solve for \(C\). (1) \(\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B)/B\) is non-increasing because \(U_{E,k}\) is concave with \(U_{E,k}(0) = 0\), and constant over \(B\) once \(U_{E,k}(B) = E\). (2) and (3) substitute the TD and sparse byte counts of Definition 2. \(\blacksquare\)

DiffusionGemma's cache is small (2 KV heads in 5 global layers, \(\nu_G = 20\) kB per token, plus a fixed 210 MB per sequence for the windows), so its linear layers dominate for a long way. A dense GQA model such as Llama-3-8B, with 131 kB per token and 16 GB of weights, reaches equal weight and cache bytes at \(BC \approx 122\)k.

4 The attention part

At long context the attention terms dominate: a pass costs what it reads of each sequence's KV cache. Three kinds of attention layer appear in current models: dense, sliding-window, and sparse in the style of DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA), summarised in the remark below. Table 4 gives, per sequence, what one query costs on each and what a canvas of \(b\) queries costs.

RemarkHow DSA works

For a query at position \(t\), with \(C\) cached tokens \(s\), a DSA layer does three things.

  1. Scan. A small indexer with \(H_I\) heads of dimension \(h_I\), in FP8, scores every cached token:
    \[ I_{t,s} = \sum_{j=1}^{H_I} w_{t,j}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(q^I_{t,j}\cdot k^I_s\bigr),\qquad q^I_{t,j} = W^I_{q,j}\,h_t,\quad k^I_s = W^I_k\,h_s, \]
    \[ \begin{gathered} I_{t,s} = \sum_{j=1}^{H_I} w_{t,j}\,\mathrm{ReLU}\bigl(q^I_{t,j}\cdot k^I_s\bigr),\\[4pt] q^I_{t,j} = W^I_{q,j}\,h_t,\qquad k^I_s = W^I_k\,h_s, \end{gathered} \]
    with query-dependent weights \(w_{t,j}\). The index keys \(k^I_s\) are cached, \(\nu_I = H_Ih_Is_I\) bytes per token, and the scan costs \(\alpha_I = 2H_Ih_I\) FLOPs per query and per cached token.
  2. Select. \(S_t = \) the \(m\) cached tokens with the largest \(I_{t,s}\) (\(m = 2048\) in DeepSeek-V3.2).
  3. Attend. Ordinary attention over the selected set, \(u_t = \mathrm{Attn}\bigl(q_t, \{k_s, v_s : s \in S_t\}\bigr)\): \(\alpha_S = 4n_q\sum h_\ell\) FLOPs and \(\nu_S\) KV bytes per selected token, the same constants as a dense layer, over \(m\) tokens instead of \(C\).

The indexer is trained to match the dense attention distribution (KL divergence, during a short dense warm-up and then during sparse training) and receives no gradient from the language-modelling loss. For the AR decoder the scan is the only attention cost that still grows with \(C\); for a canvas, the scan's cache is read once for all \(b\) queries, while each query's selected tokens are its own.

Proposition 5The penalty of a sparse layer

In short. With sparse attention each query selects its own cached tokens and a canvas reads their union: the MoE penalty again, with the canvas length in the role of the batch.

Under (A2), with independent selections, \(\class{mj-td}{I^{TD}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = b\,\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}/\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) with

\[ \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}(C) - 1 = \frac{\nu_S\,[\,U_{C,m}(b) - m\,]}{\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C)}. \]
  1. \(1 \le \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \le 1 + \dfrac{\nu_S\min(bm, C)}{\nu_I C + \nu_S m}\): the penalty comes only from the selected tokens, and is bounded by what the canvas can read of them beyond one query.
  2. Three stages of context, for a model whose attention is a sparse layer: \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = 1\) for \(C \le m\); \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \approx \dfrac{(\nu_I + \nu_S)\,C}{\nu_I C + \nu_S m}\) for \(m \lt C \lesssim bm\), when the canvas's selections cover the cache; \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} \to 1\) as \(C \to \infty\), when the union stops growing at \(bm\) and the scan's bytes dominate.
  3. With a free scan (\(\alpha_I = \nu_I = 0\)) and no other attention layers, \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} = \Pi_{\mathrm{sel}} = U_{C,m}(b)/m\), between 1 and \(\min(b, C/m)\), and non-decreasing in \(b\) and \(C\): the exact twin of the expert penalty \(\Pi_E = U_{E,k}(Bb)/U_{E,k}(B)\), with the canvas length in the role of the batch size.
Proof

The identity is Definition 4 on the attention part: \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{TD}_{\mathrm{attn}}} - \class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}} = \nu_S[U_{C,m}(b) - \min(C,m)]\), and \(\min(C,m) = m\) for \(C \ge m\). (1) \(m \le U_{C,m}(b) \le \min(bm, C)\), and \(\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}} \ge \nu_I C + \nu_S m\). (2) For \(C \le m\), \(U_{C,m}(b) = C = \min(C,m)\). For \(bm \gg C\), \((1-m/C)^b \approx e^{-bm/C} \to 0\), so \(U_{C,m}(b) \to C\). As \(C \to \infty\), \((1-m/C)^b \to 1 - bm/C\), so \(U_{C,m}(b) \to bm\), a constant, while \(\nu_I C\) grows. (3) \(U_{C,m}\) is increasing and concave in its argument with \(U_{C,m}(1) = m\), so \(U_{C,m}(b)/m \le b\), and \(U_{C,m}(b) \le C\); monotonicity in \(C\) is that of \(C[1 - (1-m/C)^b]\). \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 6When attention dominates

In short. With full windows, attention overtakes the weights in an AR step's bytes at the context of Proposition 4, and in a position's FLOPs at \(C_F(1-\sigma)\), which does not depend on the batch; as the context grows both intensities move to their attention values, \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) for AR and \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) for TD with dense attention.

Under (A2), with the windows full (\(C \ge w\)):

  1. Attention carries at least a share \(x\) of an AR step's bytes exactly when \(C \ge C_{\mathrm{lin}}(B, 1-x)\), the threshold of Proposition 4.
  2. Attention carries at least a share \(x\) of a position's FLOPs exactly when \(C \ge C_F\bigl(\tfrac{x}{1-x} - \sigma\bigr)\), with \(C_F = 2N_{\mathrm{act}}/\alpha_G\) and \(\sigma = \alpha_Ww/(2N_{\mathrm{act}})\); the same for AR and TD.
  3. As \(C\) grows, \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}\) move monotonically toward \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I^{TD}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\). With dense attention these are \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\); with sparse attention they are \(I_{\mathrm{idx}}\) and \(b\,I_{\mathrm{idx}}\), the index cache dominating the attention part once \(\nu_IC \gg \nu_S\,bm\).
Proof

(1) is Proposition 4 with the complementary share. (2) With dense attention \(\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}/\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}} \ge x \iff (1-x)(\alpha_GC + \alpha_Ww) \ge 2xN_{\mathrm{act}}\). (3) For \(C \ge w\) each intensity is a ratio of affine functions of \(C\) with positive denominator once \(U_{C,m}\) has saturated at \(bm\), hence monotone, with the stated limits; in the sparse case the \(\nu_IC\) and \(\alpha_IC\) terms dominate. \(\blacksquare\)

\(C_F\) is the context at which global attention alone matches the linear FLOPs; for a dense model with attention in every layer and a 4× MLP it is about \(6d\). For DiffusionGemma \(C_F = 37.6\)k and \(\sigma = 6.8\%\). Attention FLOPs and attention bytes become the majority at very different contexts: a tenth of the FLOPs at 1.6k tokens, half at 35k; half of an AR step's bytes at 291k tokens for batch 1 and 27k for batch 64.

5 The whole picture

Proposition 2 makes each intensity a byte-weighted average of the two parts of §3 and §4. Putting them back into Proposition 1 gives the general answer.

Proposition 7When TD wins

In short. TD wins exactly when a pass serves more tokens per read than it pays in penalty (\(\mathrm{TPF} \ge \Pi\)) and AR uses at most a fraction \(1/\omega\) of the compute (\(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \le I^\star/\omega\)).

Under (A1) to (A4), with \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} = \theta_{\mathrm{lin}}\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}} + \theta_{\mathrm{attn}}\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\) and \(\Pi - 1 = \theta_{\mathrm{lin}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1) + \theta_{\mathrm{attn}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} - 1)\) (shares of an AR step's bytes):

  1. If AR is compute-bound, \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \ge I^\star\), TD loses: \(\Gamma \le 1/\omega\).
  2. Otherwise \(\Gamma \ge 1\) exactly when both \(\mathrm{TPF} \ge \Pi\) and \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \le I^\star/\omega\): a TD pass must serve more tokens per read than it pays in penalty, and AR must use at most a fraction \(1/\omega\) of the compute, so that TD's \(\omega\) times more FLOPs fit in what AR leaves idle.
  3. The gain is \(\Gamma = \min\bigl(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi,\ I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}})\bigr)\), largest where the two are equal, at the batch where a TD pass reaches the ridge.
Proof

(1) and (3) are Corollary 1 with Proposition 2's expressions. (2) A minimum of two numbers is at least 1 exactly when both are. \(\blacksquare\)

Read with §3 and §4: at short context \(\Pi = \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} = \class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(B)\), so the first condition is a lower bound on the batch (the MoE penalty must fall below TPF) and the second an upper bound (\(B_\times\)); at long context \(\Pi = \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} = \class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\), and neither depends on the batch. For DiffusionGemma on an H200, both conditions hold at every batch up to 109 at short context, and at every batch at long context.

Proposition 8The batch needed to saturate compute

In short. With dense attention and full windows, a batch cannot saturate compute on cache bytes, since each sequence brings its own cache, but a canvas can: once \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \gt I^\star\), a longer context makes a TD pass compute-bound at a smaller batch, not a larger one.

Let \(B^\star_n(C)\) be the smallest batch at which a pass with \(n\) queries per sequence (\(n = 1\) for AR, \(n = b\) for TD) is compute-bound, or \(\infty\) if there is none, with dense attention and the windows full (\(C \ge w\)).

  1. If \(n\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \lt I^\star\), then \(B^\star_n(C)\) is non-decreasing in \(C\), and infinite beyond some finite context.
  2. If \(n\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \gt I^\star\), then \(B^\star_n(C)\) is non-increasing in \(C\), and equal to 1 beyond some finite context.

AR is in the first case whenever \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} \lt I^\star\), which holds for every model and accelerator here. TD is in the second case exactly when \(b \gt T_c = I^\star/I_{\mathrm{attn}}\).

Proof

Write \(D_n(C) = n\,\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}(C) - I^\star \class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C)\). Multiplying out \(I_n \ge I^\star\) gives \(B\,D_n(C) \ge I^\star s_wW_n(B)\): each sequence contributes \(n\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}\) FLOPs, of which \(I^\star \class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\) are needed to make its own cache reads compute-bound, and what is left, summed over the batch, has to cover the weights. The right side is positive, so no batch works when \(D_n \le 0\). For \(C \ge w\), \(D_n\) is affine in \(C\) with slope \((nI_{\mathrm{attn}} - I^\star)\,\nu_G\). A smaller \(D_n\) can only remove batches from the feasible set, so a negative slope makes \(B^\star_n\) non-decreasing and eventually infinite; a positive slope makes the set grow until batch 1 works, once \(D_n \ge I^\star s_wW_n(1)\). Nothing needs a closed form for \(W_n(B)\), so the argument covers MoE models. \(\blacksquare\)

The canvas does for the KV cache what batch does for the weights.

A larger batch does not help with cache bytes, because every sequence brings its own cache. AR uses each cache byte for one query, so at long context its intensity drifts toward \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\), far below the ridge, whatever the batch. A canvas uses each cache byte for \(b\) queries, and once \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \gt I^\star\) a longer context makes compute easier to saturate, not harder. When \(W_n\) does not depend on \(B\), solving the inequality gives \(B^\star_n(C) = \lceil I^\star s_wW_n/D_n(C)\rceil\) for \(D_n \gt 0\), which at zero context is the \(\class{mj-ar}{B^\star_{AR}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}}\) of §3.

Here \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 8\) and \(T_c = 206/8 \approx 26\). AR needs a batch of 1,640 at zero context, and past 135 tokens no batch is enough. TD with \(b = 256\) needs 6.4 at zero context, and batch 1 is enough from about 224k tokens. TD with \(b = 32\) first needs a slightly larger batch while the windows fill (\(b\,I_{\mathrm{win}} = 64 \lt I^\star\)), then a smaller one once global attention dominates (\(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 256 \gt I^\star\)). TD with \(b = 16\) is below \(T_c\) and behaves like AR. The grey region is where the weights and the batch's cache exceed the H200's 141 GB: at 1k tokens about 390 sequences fit, far below the 1,640 that AR would need, so for DiffusionGemma on an H200 AR decoding is memory-bound at every batch that fits.

RemarkKernels run one after another

In short. Timing a pass as a chain of kernels instead of one fully overlapped kernel is never faster and at most twice slower; it moves DiffusionGemma's best batch from 5 to 9.

(A1) gives a pass the time of one kernel in which all of its compute and memory traffic overlap. A pass is a chain of kernels, and adding their roofline times, \(\sum_c \max(F_c/\pi, Q_c/\beta)\) over kernel classes, is never faster and at most twice slower. For an AR step at moderate batch every class is memory-bound and nothing changes. For a TD pass the dense layers are compute-bound from batch 1 while the experts stay memory-bound until \(Bbk/E \approx I^\star\), batch 13 here; the best batch moves from 5 to 9 and the peak gain from 5.1× to 3.7×. The calculator offers both rules.

    \(\class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}} = I^\star s_w\class{mj-td}{W_{TD}}/(2bN_{\mathrm{act}}) = 37 \times 49.0\,\text{GB}/(2 \times 256 \times 3.08\text{B}) = 1.15\): a TD pass sits just below the ridge at batch 1. Corollary 1 gives \(\Gamma = \min(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi,\ I^\star/(\omega I_{\mathrm{lin}})) = \min(2.15,\ 2.47) = 2.15\). The exact gain is 2.03, with TD already compute-bound because the vocabulary adds to its FLOPs.

    Where does AR catch up?
    
      
    Answer
        
    In R2, \(\Gamma = 1\) when \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) = I^\star/\omega = 2.47\). The closed form \(B_\times = \class{mj-ar}{B^\star_{AR}}/\omega = 19.6\) does not apply here, because at such small batches AR does not yet touch every expert. Solving with the full \(\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B)\) gives \(B \approx 10\). The exact parity is at 4.5: once TD is compute-bound its vocabulary FLOPs count in full and lower its gain by about a quarter, so parity needs only \(I_{\mathrm{lin}}(B) \approx 2.47 \times 0.74 \approx 1.8\), which happens near batch 4. On the map: the dashed line, close to the bottom.

  3. 

    Long context. Who wins?
    
      
    Answer
        
    \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 8 \lt 37 \lt b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}}\), so this is still R6, but now \(\Gamma = 37/(15 \times 8) = 0.31\). AR wins at long context, at every batch. With such a low ridge, AR's attention already runs at \(8/37\), about a fifth of peak, while TD runs at peak but does 15 times the work. The exact gain is 0.62 at batch 1 and 256k tokens, and 0.35 at 2.2M tokens, the longest single sequence that fits next to the weights. On the map: the blue right half.

A Appendix

  
Exact accounting

  
The maps, headline numbers and calculator do not use Proposition 1. They evaluate every pass of a generation: AR prefill and \(G-1\) decode steps; TD encoder prefill, then per canvas \(S\) bidirectional denoising passes and one causal commit. Each pass counts:

  
    
    
  • 
FLOPs: linear layers; exact (query, key) pairs under causal, bidirectional and sliding-window masks, in-canvas keys included; the LM head on every logit position; for DiffusionGemma, the self-conditioning soft embedding and MLP.
    • 
    
  • 
Bytes: weights with \(U(N)\) experts; the vocabulary matrix once per use; KV reads and writes; activations under a fused kernel schedule (or none, when on-chip); logits and probabilities, \(\lambda\) times per output position.
    • 
    
  • 
Decoder ratio: a denoising pass multiplies every per-layer term (FLOPs, weights, KV reads, activations) by \(r\); its vocabulary and self-conditioning terms are unchanged. For \(r \gt 1\) the memory footprint adds \((r-1)L\) layers of weights.
    • 
    
  • 
Time: the per-pass roofline, \(\max(F/\pi, Q/\beta)\), or, under the calculator's kernel-by-kernel rule, the sum of that roofline over the kernel classes (dense layers, experts, global, window and sparse attention, indexer, vocabulary head). The class sums equal the totals exactly.
    • 
  

  
The closed forms for masked key counts match brute-force counting. Proposition 1 matches this accounting to within 1.2% without vocabulary (Table 3). Model shapes come from each checkpoint's config.json; for DiffusionGemma also from its safetensors headers (25.25B text parameters, 3.08B active per token plus a 0.74B head).

  
Sanity check against measurement

  
dgbench is our harness for timing DiffusionGemma generation in vLLM. Its runs (vLLM 0.28.0, one H200, BF16, \(S = 14\)) measured 1,295 tok/s per request at batch 1 and 2,160 tok/s at batch 64. The exact bounds are 1,535 and 6,440 tok/s: 84% and 34% of the roofline. At batch 64 the denoising pass is compute-bound; the gap is matmul efficiency (128 small experts) and vocabulary-sized sampling work, which a pass-level roofline overlaps with compute and a real sampler does not.
