The gain

Part 2 of Block Diffusion Rooflines (Previous: 1 · Setup | Next: 3 · The linear part)

With the costs of Part 1, the roofline model bounds the time of each pass, and hence the throughput of each decoder. Proposition 1 gives their ratio: the FLOP rates the two decoders attain, divided by the \(\omega = Sr + 1\) passes that a canvas costs. Corollary 1 reads off which decoder wins, and Proposition 2 splits each intensity into a linear part and an attention part, which Parts 3 and 4 treat separately.

Proposition 1TD/AR throughput gain

In short. The gain is the ratio of the two decoders' attainable FLOP rates, divided by the \(\omega\) passes a canvas costs.

A1 · Roofline
A pass with \(F\) FLOPs and \(Q\) HBM bytes takes \(\max(F/\pi,\,Q/\beta)\).
A2 · Costs
Per sequence, an AR step costs \(\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}\) FLOPs and \(\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}}\) bytes, and a TD pass \(\class{mj-td}{F^{TD}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{TD}}\), as in Definition 2. Vocabulary, activations, KV writes and in-canvas keys are neglected. Expert routing and token selection are independent across positions.
A3 · Canvas
A canvas commits \(b\) tokens per sequence after \(S\) denoising passes and one commit pass. A denoising pass costs \(r\) times a commit pass, in FLOPs and in bytes.
A4 · Lockstep
\(B\) sequences share every pass, at a context \(C\) held fixed over a canvas.

Then TD's throughput over AR's is

\[ \boxed{\begin{gathered}\Gamma = \frac{1}{\omega}\cdot\frac{\min(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}},\,I^\star)}{\min(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}},\,I^\star)},\\[4pt] \omega = S\,r+1,\qquad \class{mj-td}{I_{TD}} = \frac{b}{\Pi}\,\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}.\end{gathered}} \]
Proof
  1. For a pass with \(I = F/Q\): \(\max(F/\pi,\,Q/\beta) = \dfrac{F}{\beta}\max\Bigl(\dfrac{1}{I^\star},\dfrac{1}{I}\Bigr) = \dfrac{F}{\beta\,\min(I, I^\star)}\).
  2. AR. A step costs \(B\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}\) FLOPs at intensity \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\) and yields \(B\) tokens: \(\class{mj-ar}{t_{AR}} = \class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}\,/\,\bigl(\beta\min(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}, I^\star)\bigr)\) per token.
  3. TD. A commit pass costs \(B\class{mj-td}{F^{TD}} = Bb\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}\) FLOPs at intensity \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}\). A denoising pass multiplies both \(\class{mj-td}{F^{TD}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{TD}}\) by \(r\), so it has the same intensity and takes \(r\) times as long. A canvas therefore takes \(\omega\) times one commit pass and yields \(Bb\) tokens: \(\class{mj-td}{t_{TD}} = \omega \class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}\,/\,\bigl(\beta\min(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}, I^\star)\bigr)\) per token.
  4. \(\Gamma = \class{mj-ar}{t_{AR}}/\class{mj-td}{t_{TD}}\), and \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}} = \class{mj-td}{F^{TD}}/\class{mj-td}{Q^{TD}} = b\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}/(\Pi \class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}}) = b\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}/\Pi\) by Definition 4. \(\blacksquare\)

Corollary 1Which side wins

In short. If AR is compute-bound TD loses; otherwise the gain is the smaller of \(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi\), set by bytes, and \(I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}})\), set by the compute AR leaves idle.

If AR is compute-bound (\(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \ge I^\star\)), then \(\Gamma \le 1/\omega\). Otherwise

\[ \Gamma = \min\Bigl(\frac{\mathrm{TPF}}{\Pi},\ \frac{I^\star}{\omega\,\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}}\Bigr),\quad \mathrm{TPF} = \frac{b}{\omega}. \]
Proof

If \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \ge I^\star\) the denominator of Proposition 1 is \(I^\star\) and the numerator at most \(I^\star\). Otherwise \(\min(b\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}/\Pi,\,I^\star)/\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} = \min(b/\Pi,\,I^\star/\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}})\); divide by \(\omega\). \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 2Each intensity is a byte-weighted average of its linear and attention parts

In short. Each intensity is the byte-weighted average of its linear and attention parts, so it lies between them, nearer to the part that carries more of the bytes.

Under (A2), for \(X \in \{AR, TD\}\), let \(\theta^X_{\mathrm{lin}} = Q^X_{\mathrm{lin}}/Q^X\) and \(\theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}} = Q^X_{\mathrm{attn}}/Q^X\) be the shares of the two parts in the bytes of Definition 2. Then

\[ I_X = \theta^X_{\mathrm{lin}}\,I^X_{\mathrm{lin}} + \theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}}\,I^X_{\mathrm{attn}},\qquad \Pi - 1 = \class{mj-ar}{\theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1) + \class{mj-ar}{\theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} - 1). \]
\[ \begin{gathered} I_X = \theta^X_{\mathrm{lin}}\,I^X_{\mathrm{lin}} + \theta^X_{\mathrm{attn}}\,I^X_{\mathrm{attn}},\\[4pt] \Pi - 1 = \class{mj-ar}{\theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1)\\ \qquad + \class{mj-ar}{\theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} - 1). \end{gathered} \]

Consequently each intensity lies between its two parts, and \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}/\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\) lies between \(b/\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and \(b/\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\), nearer to whichever part carries more of the bytes.

Proof

\(I_X = F^X/Q^X = \sum_c (Q^X_c/Q^X)(F^X_c/Q^X_c)\) over the two parts \(c\). For the penalty, \(\class{mj-td}{Q^{TD}_c} = \Pi_c\,\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_c}\) by Definition 4, so \(\Pi - 1 = \sum_c (\class{mj-td}{Q^{TD}_c} - \class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_c})/\class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}} = \sum_c \class{mj-ar}{\theta^{AR}_c}(\Pi_c - 1)\). A weighted average with positive weights lies between its terms; \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}} = (b/\Pi)\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\) and \(\Pi\) lies between \(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and \(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\). \(\blacksquare\)
