Throughput calculator

Part 3 of Block Diffusion Rooflines: the exact maps over batch and context, the calculator and the exact accounting.

Part 3 of Block Diffusion RooflinesPrevious: 2 · Weights and cacheNext: 4 · Exercises

In the previous section, we have given the setup to obtain a closed simplified formula for the throughput gains of TD over AR in Proposition 1. Then we have turned to two different regimes which arise at different context length for both AR and TD. In this final section, before diving into the practice of AR and TD inference engines, we provide here a calculator to illustrate the throughput gains of TD over AR. In particular, our calculator accounts not only for model choices but also for hardware specifications.

The exact maps

We now drop the propositions' simplifications. The maps use the exact accounting described at the end of this chapter, vocabulary included, over batch × context. Every line is a contour of the computed grid. Faded cells do not fit in the accelerator's memory, which must hold the weights, the KV cache and one pass's logits, \(b\) times larger for a TD canvas than for an AR step. On the gain maps, faded colour marks where TD does not fit, and hatching where AR does not fit either. Hover or tap a cell for its values.

Sparse attention

A generation reads a prompt of \(P\) tokens, then generates \(G\) tokens, so the context grows from \(P\) to \(P + G\). Throughputs, latencies and the gain are averages over the whole generation; the regime label and the share-of-peak chart are evaluated at the middle context \(P + G/2\).

Exact accounting

The maps and the calculator above do not use Proposition 1. The maps compare one canvas (its \(S\) denoising passes and its commit) with \(b\) AR steps, at a fixed context \(C\); the calculator evaluates every pass of a generation. For a generation of \(G\) tokens, AR's passes are the prefill and \(G-1\) decode steps. For TD, they are the prefill, then, for each canvas, \(S\) bidirectional denoising passes and one causal commit. Expert routing and token selection are independent and uniform, as in (A3). For each pass, we count the following, where † marks what Proposition 1 (Definition 2) leaves out:

  • FLOPs:
    • the linear layers;
    • † the exact (query, key) pairs under causal, bidirectional and sliding-window masks, in-canvas keys included, where Definition 2 gives every query \(C\) keys;
    • for sparse layers, the indexer's scan of every cached token and attention over the \(m\) selected tokens;
    • † the vocabulary head on every logit position;
    • † for DiffusionGemma, the self-conditioning soft embedding and MLP.
  • Bytes:
    • the weights, with \(U_{E,k}(N)\) experts per MoE layer for a pass of \(N\) positions;
    • † the vocabulary matrix once per use;
    • † the embedding rows of the input positions, \(d\) values each;
    • KV reads, including the index cache of sparse layers, and † KV writes;
    • † activations under a fused kernel schedule, none when they stay on-chip (the calculator's setting);
    • † logits and probabilities, \(\lambda\) times per output position, with \(\class{mj-td}{\lambda^{\mathrm{TD}}} = 5\) and \(\class{mj-ar}{\lambda^{\mathrm{AR}}} = 2\), and \(\class{mj-td}{\lambda^{\mathrm{TD}}} = 2\) for a TD twin without self-conditioning.
  • Denoising-pass cost: a denoising pass multiplies every per-layer term (FLOPs, weights, KV reads, activations) by \(r\), while its vocabulary and self-conditioning terms are unchanged. For \(r \gt 1\), the memory footprint adds \((r-1)L\) layers of weights.

We compare the two on the grid of the maps: DiffusionGemma on an H200 at its BF16 datasheet rates, \(b = 256\), \(S = 14\), \(r = 1\), 40 batches from 1.1 to 939 and 44 contexts from 558 to 962k, both evenly spaced on a log scale. Without the vocabulary and self-conditioning, Proposition 1 matches the exact gain to within 1.32% at contexts of at least 1k, and to within 2.2% on the whole grid. With them, Proposition 1 is above the exact gain in 1,503 of the 1,760 cells, by up to 41% at the largest batch and the shortest context, since the vocabulary head runs on every position of every pass and so adds more FLOPs per generated token to TD than to AR. At batch 1 and context 1k it is below, 2.22 against 2.53, since the vocabulary matrix adds the same bytes to both passes and so lowers the penalty.
