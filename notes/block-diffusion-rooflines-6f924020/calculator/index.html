Regimes, maps and calculator

Part 4 of Block Diffusion Rooflines: when TD wins, the batch needed to saturate compute, the regimes, the exact maps over batch and context, the calculator and the exact accounting.

Part 4 of Block Diffusion RooflinesPrevious: 3 · The attention partNext: 5 · Exercises

Parts 2 and 3 treat the two extremes: a pass that reads only weights, and one that reads only KV cache. In this chapter, we cover every context in between. First, we state when TD wins (Proposition 7) and the batch each decoder needs to saturate compute (Proposition 8), and we sort the cases into regimes. We then drop the simplifications of the propositions: the exact maps show the gain over batch and context, and the calculator computes it for any accelerator, model and decoding setup. The chapter closes with the exact accounting behind both.

We now put the two extremes together. By Proposition 2, each intensity is a byte-weighted average of its linear part (Part 2) and its attention part (Part 3). Putting them back into Proposition 1 gives the general answer.

Proposition 7When TD wins

In short. TD wins exactly when a pass serves more tokens per read than it pays in penalty (\(\mathrm{TPF} \ge \Pi\)) and AR uses at most a fraction \(1/\omega\) of the compute (\(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \le I^\star/\omega\)).

Assume (A1) to (A5), and write \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\) and \(\Pi\) with the shares of an AR step's bytes, as in Proposition 2: \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} = \class{mj-ar}{\theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}} + \class{mj-ar}{\theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\) and \(\Pi - 1 = \class{mj-ar}{\theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{lin}} - 1) + \class{mj-ar}{\theta^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(\Pi_{\mathrm{attn}} - 1)\). By Corollary 1, TD loses if AR is compute-bound (\(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \ge I^\star\)), and otherwise \(\Gamma = \min\bigl(\mathrm{TPF}/\Pi,\ I^\star/(\omega \class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}})\bigr)\). In the second case:

  1. \(\Gamma \ge 1\) exactly when both \(\mathrm{TPF} \ge \Pi\) and \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} \le I^\star/\omega\): a TD pass must serve more tokens per read than it pays in penalty, and AR must use at most a fraction \(1/\omega\) of the compute, so that TD's \(\omega\) times more FLOPs fit in what AR leaves idle.
  2. Treat \(B\) as a real variable and assume that \(\Pi(\cdot, C)\) is non-increasing in \(B\). Since \(\class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}(B)/B\) is non-increasing (proof of Proposition 4), \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}(\cdot, C)\) is non-decreasing in \(B\), and \(\Gamma\) is maximal at the batch \(\class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}}(C)\) at which \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}} = I^\star\), or at \(B = 1\) if \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}(1, C) \ge I^\star\); if \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}(\cdot, C) \lt I^\star\) at every batch, \(\Gamma = \mathrm{TPF}/\Pi\) is non-decreasing in \(B\). Over integer batches the maximum is at \(\lfloor \class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}}(C)\rfloor\) or \(\lceil \class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}}(C)\rceil\). The assumption on \(\Pi\) can fail.1
Proof

(1) A minimum of two numbers is at least 1 exactly when both are. (2) The first term of the minimum is non-decreasing in \(B\) and the second is non-increasing. The minimum of two such functions is maximal where they cross, which is where \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}} = b\,\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}/\Pi = I^\star\), or at \(B = 1\) if the first is already the larger there. Over integer batches the minimum is non-decreasing up to \(\lfloor \class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}}(C)\rfloor\) and non-increasing from \(\lceil \class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}}(C)\rceil\) on. \(\blacksquare\)

Proposition 7 specialises to the two extremes as follows:

  • Short context: \(\Pi = \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\) and \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} = \class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}(B)\). Hence, the first condition of Proposition 7(1) is a lower bound on the batch (the linear penalty must fall below TPF), and the second is an upper bound (\(B_\times\)).
  • Long context: \(\Pi = \Pi_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} = \class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\), and neither condition depends on the batch.

For DiffusionGemma on an H200, both conditions hold at every batch up to 109 at short context, and at every batch at long context.2

Proposition 8The batch needed to saturate compute

In short. With dense attention and full windows, a batch cannot saturate compute on cache bytes, since each sequence brings its own cache, but a canvas can: once \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \gt I^\star\), a longer context makes a TD pass compute-bound at a smaller batch, not a larger one.

Let \(B^\star_n(C)\) be the smallest batch at which a pass with \(n\) queries per sequence (\(n = 1\) for AR, \(n = b\) for TD) is compute-bound, or \(\infty\) if there is none, with dense attention and the windows full (\(C \ge w\)). Write \(I_n\) and \(W_n\) for the intensity and the weights of that pass (\(I_1 = \class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\), \(W_1 = \class{mj-ar}{W_{AR}}\), \(I_b = \class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}\), \(W_b = \class{mj-td}{W_{TD}}\)), and \(D_n(C) = n\,\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}(C) - I^\star \class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}(C)\), the FLOPs per sequence left for the weights once its cache reads are compute-bound.

  1. If \(n\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \lt I^\star\), then \(B^\star_n(C)\) is non-decreasing in \(C\), and infinite beyond some finite context.
  2. If \(n\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \gt I^\star\), then \(B^\star_n(C)\) is non-increasing in \(C\), and equal to 1 beyond some finite context.

AR is in the first case whenever \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} \lt I^\star\), which holds for every model and accelerator of the calculator (\(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\) and \(I_{\mathrm{idx}}\) are at most 16 there, against a lowest ridge point of 37, on the H20). TD is in the second case exactly when \(b \gt b_c = I^\star/I_{\mathrm{attn}}\), the critical canvas length.

Proof

Multiplying out \(I_n \ge I^\star\) gives \(B\,D_n(C) \ge I^\star s_wW_n(B)\): each sequence contributes \(n\class{mj-ar}{F^{AR}}\) FLOPs, of which \(I^\star \class{mj-ar}{Q^{AR}_{\mathrm{attn}}}\) are needed to make its own cache reads compute-bound, and what is left, summed over the batch, has to cover the weights. The right side is positive, so no batch works when \(D_n \le 0\). For \(C \ge w\), \(D_n\) is affine in \(C\) with slope \((nI_{\mathrm{attn}} - I^\star)\,\nu_G\). A smaller \(D_n\) can only remove batches from the feasible set, so a negative slope makes \(B^\star_n\) non-decreasing and eventually infinite; a positive slope makes the set grow until batch 1 works, once \(D_n \ge I^\star s_wW_n(1)\). Nothing needs a closed form for \(W_n(B)\), so the argument covers MoE models. \(\blacksquare\)

The canvas does for the KV cache what batch does for the weights.

Indeed, a larger batch does not help with cache bytes, because every sequence brings its own cache. AR uses each cache byte for one query. Hence, at long context, its intensity tends to \(I_{\mathrm{attn}}\), far below the ridge, whatever the batch. On the other hand, a canvas uses each cache byte for \(b\) queries. Once \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} \gt I^\star\), a longer context makes compute easier to saturate, not harder.

When \(W_n\) does not depend on \(B\), solving the inequality gives \(B \ge B^\star_n(C) = I^\star s_wW_n/D_n(C)\) for \(D_n \gt 0\). At zero context, this is the \(\class{mj-ar}{B^\star_{AR}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{B^\star_{TD}}\) of Part 23; an integer batch rounds it up.

TD with \(b = 16\) is below \(b_c\) and behaves like AR. The grey region is where the weights and the batch's cache exceed the H200's 141 GB: at 1k tokens about 390 sequences fit, far below the 1,640 that AR would need, so for DiffusionGemma on an H200 AR decoding is memory-bound at every batch that fits, except at very small context: below about 90 tokens, the cache is small enough for a compute-bound batch to fit.

Eight regimes

Each minimum in Proposition 1 asks whether one decoder is compute- or memory-bound. Answering both questions at short context (\(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} = \class{mj-ar}{I^{AR}_{\mathrm{lin}}}\), \(\Pi = \Pi_{\mathrm{lin}}\)) and at long context with dense attention (\(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}} = I_{\mathrm{attn}}\), \(\Pi = 1\)) gives eight regimes. Four of them are the ones of Parts 2 and 3; the other four need AR compute-bound, which no batch that fits in memory achieves here, except at very small context (below about 90 tokens).

The exact maps

Finally, we drop the propositions' simplifications. The maps use the exact accounting described at the end of this chapter, vocabulary included, over batch × context. Every line is a contour of the computed grid. Faded cells do not fit in the accelerator's memory, which must hold the weights, the KV cache and one pass's logits, \(b\) times larger for a TD canvas than for an AR step. On the gain maps, faded colour marks where TD does not fit, and hatching where AR does not fit either. Hover or tap a cell for its values.

Figure 18 · interactive

In the roofline bound, AR catches up with TD at batch 110 at context 1k, and at a larger batch as the context grows, beyond 1,024 from 64k tokens on.An AR step never reaches the ridge, and at long context its intensity tends to 8 whatever the batch.A TD canvas crosses the ridge at a batch that falls as the context grows, from 5 at 1k to 1 at 256k, and its intensity tends to 2,048.With sparse global layers, the roofline bound has AR catch up with TD near batch 90–110 at every context up to 1M tokens.

As the context grows, \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\) tends to \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 8\) without reaching the ridge, and \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}\) tends to \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 2{,}048\), above it. On the gain map, the bottom-left is R1, the band above the TD-compute line is R2, and the colour flattens towards R6's 1.7 at long context. With sparse layers, the gain at large batch rises far less with the context than on the dense map: at batch 1,024 it goes from 0.41 at 1k to 0.65 at 1M (1.65 with dense layers), below the sparse limit 0.86 given after Table 5, which it approaches only as \(C \to \infty\). At batch 1, it stays between 2.3 and 2.5, where the weights still carry most of AR's bytes.

The calculator

The propositions give closed forms, but only under simplifying assumptions. The calculator below drops the simplifications of (A2): it evaluates every pass of a generation with the exact accounting at the end of this chapter, still under the roofline, for the whole pass (A1) or kernel by kernel (Part 1), and, unless measured routing is chosen, (A5), for any accelerator, model, attention layout and decoding setup. For the chosen setting, it reports both throughputs, the AR batch that matches TD, the memory each decoder needs, and the maps above. In addition, the page address records the setting, so it can be shared.

Sparse attention

\(\zeta\) is the fraction of a canvas's selections that are independent: 1 is the independent case of (A5), and \(1/b\) means every query selects the same tokens.

Throughput and gain cover the whole generation, during which the context grows from \(P\) to \(P + G\). The regime label, the per-pass and per-kernel tables and the share-of-peak chart use the context halfway through, \(P + G/2\).

How the numbers are counted

    
Every gain in Parts 1–5 is a ratio of roofline bounds, counted in one of three ways.

    
A roofline time is a lower bound on the time of a pass, so each throughput is an upper bound, and \(\Gamma\) is the ratio of two such bounds, not a measurement. Part 9 compares these bounds with throughput measured in vLLM. The three accountings below differ in what they include, and each number in the blogpost says which one it uses. For DiffusionGemma on an H200:

    

    
In the interactive figures and the cards, the intensities \(\class{mj-ar}{I_{AR}}\) and \(\class{mj-td}{I_{TD}}\) are those of Definition 3, without the vocabulary, except in the intensity tabs of Figure 18 and the calculator's maps, which use the exact accounting at the end of this chapter; next to an exact gain, the regime of a pass is given instead as the share of the peak compute that it uses. The cards quote the closed-form values of the propositions, at zero context where Part 2 does, unless a readout says exact. A fourth variant, the kernel-by-kernel timing rule of Part 1, can be switched on in Figure 11 and in the calculator.

  

  
Exact accounting

  
The maps and the calculator above do not use Proposition 1. The maps compare one canvas (its \(S\) denoising passes and its commit) with \(b\) AR steps, at a fixed context \(C\); the calculator evaluates every pass of a generation. For a generation of \(G\) tokens, AR's passes are the prefill and \(G-1\) decode steps. For TD, they are the prefill, then, for each canvas, \(S\) bidirectional denoising passes and one causal commit. For each pass, we count the following:
