Maps and calculator

Part 4 of Block Diffusion Rooflines: the exact maps over batch and context, the calculator and the exact accounting.

Part 4 of Block Diffusion RooflinesPrevious: 3 · The attention partNext: 5 · Exercises

Parts 2 and 3 treat the two extremes: a pass that reads only weights, and one that reads only KV cache. In this chapter, we cover every context in between and drop the simplifications of the propositions: the exact maps show the gain over batch and context, and the calculator computes it for any accelerator, model and decoding setup. The chapter closes with the exact accounting behind both.

The exact maps

We now drop the propositions' simplifications. The maps use the exact accounting described at the end of this chapter, vocabulary included, over batch × context. Every line is a contour of the computed grid. Faded cells do not fit in the accelerator's memory, which must hold the weights, the KV cache and one pass's logits, \(b\) times larger for a TD canvas than for an AR step. On the gain maps, faded colour marks where TD does not fit, and hatching where AR does not fit either. Hover or tap a cell for its values.

As the context grows, \(\class{mj-ar}{I^{\mathrm{AR}}}\) tends to \(I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 8\) without reaching the ridge, and \(\class{mj-td}{I^{\mathrm{TD}}}\) tends to \(b\,I_{\mathrm{attn}} = 2{,}048\), above it. On the gain map, the bottom-left is R1, the band above the TD-compute line is R2, and the colour flattens towards R6's 1.7 at long context. With sparse layers, the gain at large batch rises far less with the context than on the dense map: at batch 1,024 it goes from 0.41 at 1k to 0.65 at 1M (1.65 with dense layers), below the sparse limit 0.86 given after Table 4, which it approaches only as \(C \to \infty\). At batch 1, it stays between 2.3 and 2.5, where the weights still carry most of AR's bytes.

The calculator

The propositions give closed forms, but only under simplifying assumptions. The calculator below drops the simplifications of (A2): it evaluates every pass of a generation with the exact accounting at the end of this chapter, still under the roofline, for the whole pass (A1) or kernel by kernel (Part 1), and, unless measured routing is chosen, (A3), for any accelerator, model, attention layout and decoding setup. For the chosen setting, it reports both throughputs, the AR batch that matches TD, the memory each decoder needs, and the maps above. In addition, the page address records the setting, so it can be shared.

How the numbers are counted

    
Every gain in Parts 1–5 is a ratio of roofline bounds, counted in one of three ways.

    
A roofline time is a lower bound on the time of a pass, so each throughput is an upper bound, and \(\Gamma\) is the ratio of two such bounds, not a measurement. Part 9 compares these bounds with throughput measured in vLLM. The three accountings below differ in what they include, and each number in the blogpost says which one it uses. Exact accounting

  
The maps and the calculator above do not use Proposition 1. The maps compare one canvas (its \(S\) denoising passes and its commit) with \(b\) AR steps, at a fixed context \(C\); the calculator evaluates every pass of a generation. For a generation of \(G\) tokens, AR's passes are the prefill and \(G-1\) decode steps. For TD, they are the prefill, then, for each canvas, \(S\) bidirectional denoising passes and one causal commit. For each pass, we count the following:

  
    
    
  • 
FLOPs:
      
      
        
    • the linear layers;
      • 
        
    • the exact (query, key) pairs under causal, bidirectional and sliding-window masks, in-canvas keys included;
      • 
        
    • for sparse layers, the indexer's scan of every cached token and attention over the \(m\) selected tokens;
      • 
        
    • the vocabulary head on every logit position;
      • 
        
    • for DiffusionGemma, the self-conditioning soft embedding and MLP.
      • 
      
    

    • 
    
  • 
Bytes:
      
      
        
    • the weights, with \(U_{E,k}(N)\) experts per MoE layer for a pass of \(N\) positions;
      • 
        
    • the vocabulary matrix once per use;
      • 
        
    • KV reads and writes, including the index cache of sparse layers;
      • 
        
    • activations under a fused kernel schedule, or none when they stay on-chip;
      • 
        
    • logits and probabilities, \(\lambda\) times per output position, with \(\lambda\) the calculator's logit reads and writes per position.
      • 
      
    

    • 
    
  • 
Denoising-pass cost: a denoising pass multiplies every per-layer term (FLOPs, weights, KV reads, activations) by \(r\), while its vocabulary and self-conditioning terms are unchanged. For \(r \gt 1\), the memory footprint adds \((r-1)L\) layers of weights.
    • 
    
  • 
Time: the per-pass roofline, \(\max(F/\pi, Q/\beta)\). Under the calculator's kernel-by-kernel rule, it is instead the sum of that roofline over the kernel classes (dense layers, experts, global, window and sparse attention, indexer, vocabulary head). The FLOPs and bytes of the classes add up exactly to those of the pass.
